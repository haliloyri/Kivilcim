Two men on a small raft drank rainwater and caught fish and birds. It went on for 47 days. And the hardest part would begin only after they reached land, were taken prisoner, and finally came home.

[[story]]
Louis Zamperini ran at the 1936 Olympics and was a bombardier in the Second World War. As a boy he had been in trouble, and running had straightened him out. His bomber went down in the Pacific in May 1943, while on a mission to search for another lost plane. Of the eleven on board, only three survived.

According to Laura Hillenbrand's *Unbroken*, Zamperini, pilot Russell Phillips and a third man drifted on small rafts. They had almost nothing, a few chocolate bars that were soon gone. Eight men had died as the plane fell. The third man died around the thirty-third day of the drift. The raft travelled about two thousand miles, and no land appeared along the way.

Sharks circled close. The two remaining men lived on rainwater and on the fish and birds they managed to catch. One day a Japanese plane opened fire on the raft, and they still held on, for 47 days in all.

They finally reached land held by Japan and were taken prisoner. Zamperini spent about two years in prison camps, where a guard nicknamed the Bird tormented him. The guard picked him out again and again, because he was a well-known Olympic athlete. His family had already been told he was dead.

After the war, Zamperini came home full of anger. He had survived, but the weight of those years had not come off him. Getting home did not end at the camp gate.

In 1950 he went to a prison in Tokyo and met former guards face to face. Hillenbrand reports this as the book tells it. ##Staying alive on the raft was one thing, and letting go of the rage was another.##

[[lessons]]
$$**Coming through a hard time does not mean you are free of it.** Someone who wins a long dispute at work may still wake up angry at the old boss. Treating that feeling as a separate task lets you take one small step against it. You cannot change the past, but you can change how much room it takes up in your day. A task with its own size can be worked on a little at a time.$$
