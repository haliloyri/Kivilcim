Years of treatment had not moved their symptoms. Sixty-four women with long-standing PTSD were then offered something quite different from another round of the usual: one hour a week, for ten weeks, in a class. Nobody knew yet whether it would matter.

[[story]]
Bessel van der Kolk and his colleagues ran a trial with women whose PTSD had lasted for years and had not responded to treatment. *The Body Keeps the Score* describes it. Sixty-four women were divided into two groups. One group took a trauma-sensitive yoga class, and the other attended a women's health education class.

Both groups met for one hour a week, for ten weeks. In the yoga class the women learned to pay attention to what was happening inside their own bodies. In the education class they were taught about health topics. A comparison group like that helps separate the effect of meeting weekly from the effect of what is actually done in the room.

At the end, about half of the yoga group no longer met the criteria for PTSD. In the education group it was about one in five, so some women there improved too, and the difference lay in the proportion. ##For a problem that had held on for years, that gap was not small.##

Van der Kolk's explanation is that many survivors of trauma avoid sensations in their bodies or fear them. For many of them, looking inward feels like looking at something dangerous. Yoga lets them notice those sensations in a safe room, in a controlled way. He also argues that the body's own sense that the danger is over is a separate job from the mind knowing it. Talking can change understanding, but it does not always quiet the alarm in the nervous system, so he puts body-based work next to talk therapy rather than in its place.

One caution applies. The study involved sixty-four women, all of them women, so the result cannot be assumed to hold for every patient.

[[lessons]]
$$**When understanding is not enough, give the body a job.** Working something out in your head does not always settle the tension in your body. A person who has analysed her fear of public speaking a hundred times and still shakes on the way to the podium may know exactly why and feel no calmer. A regular body practice, such as a weekly walk or a stretching session, lets her watch how the tension changes over time and see for herself what actually helps.$$
