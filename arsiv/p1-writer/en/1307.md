A barbell has a heavy plate at each end and bare steel in between. One writer on risk looked at that shape and decided it described the best way to hold money when nobody can see what comes next, and he applied it to careers as well.

[[story]]
The image comes from the gym. In *Antifragile*, Nassim Nicholas Taleb argues that money, and a working life, should be shaped like a barbell: weight at both ends and nothing in the middle. The bar between the plates stays bare.

In investing, most of the money, something like 90 percent, goes into extremely safe holdings, so a bad year cannot wreck the whole. The small remainder goes into highly speculative bets where the loss is limited from the start. Nothing sits in between. If every bet on the speculative side fails, the safe holdings are still there. If one of them pays, the gain can be large.

The middle is what Taleb distrusts. A holding of moderate risk looks reasonable, but its true risk is hard to measure, so it can carry a large loss without offering the large gain. It looks safe and is not.

##With a barbell, the worst outcome is capped and the best outcome is left open.## The safe side means a bad surprise cannot wreck you, and the speculative side lets a good surprise pay far more than was risked. Since nobody can say which surprise is coming, he cares more about the shape of the holdings than about forecasts.

He applies the same shape to a working life. A stable base income sits at one end, and experimental projects with large potential sit at the other. Instead of settling into something half safe and half bold, a person keeps one end secure and the other adventurous, and does not blend them. The paycheck covers the bills, so a failed experiment does not threaten the rent, and the experiments are free to be odd.

[[lessons]]
$$**Keep one part safe and one part bold.** A woman who runs a small tailoring shop can treat her regular alterations as steady income and set aside a little time and money for one odd experiment, such as a line of children's coats. If the experiment fails, the shop is untouched, and if it takes off, she has found something new. That lets her try ideas without staking the whole business on any of them, and she can decide beforehand how much she is willing to lose. The safe side keeps paying the bills while the experiment finds out whether it works.$$
