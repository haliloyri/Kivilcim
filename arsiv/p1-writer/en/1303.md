A farmer walks out to the turkey's pen every morning with food, and the turkey grows more certain of him with each visit. On the thousandth day, its certainty is higher than it has ever been. That is also the day it has the most to lose.

[[story]]
Nassim Taleb tells this story in *The Black Swan*, borrowing it from an older example by Bertrand Russell, who had used a chicken. A turkey is fed by people every day. The first feeding is a small event. The second and third are a pattern, and after a while the turkey has a rule.

Each time the farmer shows up with food, the rule gets stronger. Being fed every day by friendly humans who look out for its interests is simply how life works. Taleb's version runs for about a thousand days, and not one of them contradicts it.

The bird's record is a clean line climbing upward. Every morning adds a point of evidence, and no morning subtracts one. By any ordinary standard of proof, the case is closed.

##The turkey's sense of safety is at its highest at the moment the danger is greatest.##

Then comes the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. Something unexpected happens to the turkey, and it has to revise its belief about the farmer, the feeding, and the nature of the world in an afternoon.

Taleb uses the turkey to explain the idea in his title. For a long time Europeans took it for granted that all swans were white, because every swan anyone had seen was white. When black swans turned up in Australia, one sighting overturned a belief that had rested on countless others.

The lesson he draws is about the shape of the evidence. A long run of calm days tells you the calm was real. It tells you very little about whether a rare and large event is waiting, because the turkey's thousand good days looked exactly the same from the inside, right up to the end.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask what your calm record is hiding.** A small print shop that has had the same big customer for years can treat every steady month as proof that the arrangement is safe. The steady months only prove the customer has paid so far, and they say nothing about the week that customer calls to cancel. Once the owner names that risk out loud, she can start quietly looking for a second client while the books still look healthy, long before the pressure arrives, and the steady months become a cushion rather than a comfort.$$
