The first step in Dave Ramsey's plan for getting out of debt is to save $1,000, before paying a single extra dollar toward the debt. The reason is a repair bill that shows up in the middle of a payoff.

[[story]]
In *The Total Money Makeover* (2003), Dave Ramsey does not begin his plan by paying off debt. He begins with a small emergency fund of $1,000, set aside before any extra money goes to the debts. The debt snowball comes second, and a full emergency fund comes third.

His reasoning rests on a scene most people know. Someone is paying down a debt month after month, and things are going well. Then a car repair bill lands, the kind of cost nobody put on the list. With no cash on hand, the credit card comes out, new debt is added while the old one shrinks, the plan stalls, and the person making the payments loses heart.

According to Ramsey, unplanned costs turn up in every stage of life, and the real mistake is to meet them unprepared, because for an unprepared person the card is often the only option. Money set aside for this purpose lets the repair be paid from the fund and leaves the card untouched.

He calls the $1,000 a buffer that covers most small surprises, and he does not present it as full protection. Starting small is also deliberate: waiting months to build a large fund would delay the debt payoff, whereas a first $1,000 can be saved quickly and the real work can begin. The plan does ask for one condition in return. During this period no new debt is taken on, so the card goes back in the drawer while the buffer stays where it is.

Once the debts are paid, the fund grows to three to six months of expenses, which is the third step in the plan. The first $1,000 has one job: to keep a debt that has been paid off from coming back.

[[lessons]]
$$**Plan for surprises before they arrive.** A freelance designer who keeps a spare charger and a backup drive in her bag does not turn one failed device into a missed deadline and a night of panic. Ramsey's $1,000 does the same job for a payoff plan: it keeps a small surprise from breaking a long effort. You can look at your own plan, find the spot where one ordinary problem would stop everything, and put a small cushion exactly there. A cushion of that size is easy to build and easy to explain to the people who share your money, which makes it more likely you will actually keep it.$$
