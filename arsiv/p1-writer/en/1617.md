In Ramsey's plan a couple sits down before the month begins and writes out where every dollar will go, until nothing is left over. Zero left over is the aim, and Ramsey presents it as a way of steering money and never as a punishment.

[[story]]
In *The Total Money Makeover* (2003), Dave Ramsey describes a budget as a plan written on paper before the month starts, and not as a record you fill in afterwards. Income minus planned spending should equal zero, which is why it is called a zero-based budget. He asks couples to write it together, so that both agree on where the money goes.

Zero left over means no dollar is left undecided. Every dollar gets a job, whether that is rent, food, transport, a debt payment or savings. The point, in Ramsey's telling, is to know where your money is going because you decided it ahead of time, and not to find out afterwards and wonder.

The budget is monthly and is rewritten each month, since income and costs are never quite the same from one month to the next. For the categories that tend to leak, he recommends cash in envelopes, one envelope per category, with the planned amount put inside. When an envelope is empty, spending in that category stops. The envelope for groceries turns into a gauge in the kitchen drawer, and you can see at a glance how much cash is left before the month ends.

It can sound like a plan of deprivation, but every line on the page is something you said yes to: yes to the rent, yes to the debt, yes to savings. The decision about spending was made at the start of the month, so there are fewer small negotiations along the way, and each of them has an agreed limit to check against. In Ramsey's plan the budget is also the ground the other steps stand on, because the extra money for the debt snowball or the emergency fund has to be found on paper first.

[[lessons]]
$$**Point your money somewhere before it arrives.** A parent who sets aside a fixed amount for pizza night at the start of the month knows when that night ends and feels no guilt halfway through. Ramsey's zero-based budget turns spending from a surprise at the end of the month into a decision made at the start. When you set each category's amount before the month begins, you stop wondering where the money went and know what each part of it was meant for. At the end of the month you can also compare the plan with what actually happened, and see which line you set too low.$$
