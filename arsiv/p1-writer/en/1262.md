[[story]]
One April in the early 1980s, Ryan Holiday writes in *Ego Is the Enemy*, the members of Metallica gathered before a recording session in a decrepit warehouse in New York and told their guitarist Dave Mustaine he was out. With few words they handed him a bus ticket back to San Francisco. The same day Kirk Hammett, a decent young guitarist barely in his twenties from a band called Exodus, was given the job. A few days later he played his first show with them.

It was the moment he had waited for. Metallica was known only in small circles then, but its music was already pushing the edges of thrash metal. Around that time Hammett came to a humbling realization: despite his years of playing, and despite the invitation, he was not as good as he wanted to be. At home in San Francisco he looked for a guitar teacher. ##He had joined his dream band and turned professional, and he still insisted he was a student.##

The teacher he chose was Joe Satriani, who gave lessons in a small music shop in Berkeley and had a name as a teacher's teacher, with prodigies like Steve Vai among his students. Satriani's own style made him an unusual match for Hammett, which was the point. Hammett wanted to learn what he did not know. Talent was never the gap, Satriani said. Kirk walked in as a really good player, already shredding, with a great right hand and most of his chords, but he had never learned the names of things or how they all connect.

The sessions were no study circle. Satriani's rule was weekly lessons, each one learned, and if it was not, Hammett was wasting everyone's time and need not come back. Many of Hammett's friends and contemporaries stormed out, in Satriani's telling, thinking him too harsh. Hammett stayed. For two years he returned each week for objective feedback, judgment and drilling in technique and theory. Even after that he brought Satriani the licks and riffs he was working on with the band, and learned to rein in the urge to play more, and to do more with fewer notes.

Hammett became one of the great metal guitarists, and Satriani sharpened his own technique along the way. Holiday finds the power of being a student in what it does to ambition: your ego goes into someone else's hands, and you cannot bluff a master. An education cannot be hacked, he writes, and the only shortcut is doing the work every single day. He ends the thought with Epictetus, who held that it is impossible to learn what you think you already know.

[[lessons]]
$$**Put your own assessment in someone else's hands for a while.** Hammett could have declared himself arrived but booked a teacher who told him every week what he did not yet know. A new hire who has written the same weekly report for a year can ask a senior colleague to read each one for the next two months and mark what is weak. The mark-ups show gaps your own eyes skip, and your progress gets measured by someone who cannot be bluffed instead of by how good the work feels, so you can fix specific marks instead of carrying around a vague sense that everything is fine.$$
