[[open]]
Every rat gets the same two bottles, one of sweetened morphine solution and one of plain tap water. Some rats live alone in small metal cages. Others live together in an enclosure that makes a cage look like a closet.

[[story]]
Bruce Alexander and his colleagues at Simon Fraser University published their rat studies between 1978 and 1981. Gabor Maté sets them beside the Vietnam data in the chapter "From Vietnam to Rat Park" of *In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts*, and the pair carries his argument that addiction's roots are environmental.

Rat Park was an enclosure with 200 times the floor area of a standard laboratory cage. Sixteen to twenty rats of both sexes lived in it, with food, balls, wheels and room to mate. The control rats lived alone in small metal cages.

Each rat had two drop dispensers. One held sweetened morphine solution and the other plain tap water, and the sweetener was there to cover the bitter taste of the morphine. As a control, the rats were first offered sweetened quinine.

The design had four groups of eight rats, thirty-two in all. One group was caged from weaning at 22 days until day 80, one lived in the park for the same stretch, one stayed caged until day 65 and was then moved to the park, and one went the other way. The dispensers recorded each rat's intake automatically.

The drinking split by housing. Rats in the park mostly chose plain water. In one condition the caged males drank up to nineteen times more morphine than the males in the park. Females sampled morphine more often than males, and the authors concluded that isolated cages and female sex raised consumption.

Another test kept rats in ordinary cages for 57 days with morphine as the only thing to drink. When they were then moved into Rat Park and given a choice, they picked plain water and showed only minor signs of withdrawal.

The picture is not settled. Bruce Petrie, a former student of Alexander's, could not repeat the result in 1996 and suggested the strain of rat might matter. Critics point to small samples, the bitterness of oral morphine, uneven measurement between conditions and equipment failures. One group that moved from cages to the park drank nearly as much as the caged rats when the solution was sweeter and more dilute. Johann Hari's 2015 TED talk made the study famous, and researchers say the media oversold it as a paradigm shift, since environment is not the only factor in addiction.

[[lessons]]
$$**Change one condition before blaming a trait.** When a behavior is strong in one place and weak in another, the place may be doing part of the work, so it helps to move one thing and watch what follows, which is the simplest test there is. A teenager called lazy about homework who gets it done at a library table instead of a crowded kitchen has shown that the kitchen was part of the problem. You can then drop the label, swap a single condition for two weeks and compare how the work goes under each arrangement.$$
