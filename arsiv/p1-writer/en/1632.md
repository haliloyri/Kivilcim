A whole book about what swallowed feelings do to the body has to end somewhere, and the obvious question is what to do about it. Its author answers with seven words. Every one of them begins with the same letter.

[[story]]
Gabor Maté's *When the Body Says No* spends most of its pages on the toll of stress, anger held in and needs never voiced. Then comes the last chapter, and it turns to the question every reader is left with: now that you can see this pattern, what do you do about it?

The chapter is called "The Seven A's of Healing." The publisher presents these as principles for healing and for preventing illness that grows out of hidden stress. The seven words are acceptance, awareness, anger, autonomy, attachment, assertion and affirmation.

Several of them point straight back to the book's main subject. Anger, autonomy and assertion are about saying what you need and where your limits are, instead of swallowing it, which is the pattern Maté describes in patient after patient. It is striking to find anger in a list of healing steps. Most of us treat it as something to get past quickly, and here a book about the cost of suppression closes on it. Attachment and affirmation have to do with how a person relates to others and to themselves.

Maté does not treat illness as the patient's fault, and the stance of the book is understanding, not blame. The list does not replace medical treatment, and it promises nothing. How strongly stress is tied to disease is still debated in medicine, so each word is better read as a place to start looking at your own days than as a guarantee.

It is also a short list for a long book. After hundreds of pages about what goes wrong, Maté leaves the reader with something small enough to carry around: seven words, and a hint of where to begin.

[[lessons]]
$$**Pick one word from the list.** Seven headings can look like a complete overhaul of your life, and in practice it is easier to work on one at a time. Someone who always bends their weekends around other people's plans could take only "assertion" and turn down a single invitation this week, using the words "I can't make it this time". The word you choose gives you a concrete situation to examine, and next week you can return to the list and pick another.$$
