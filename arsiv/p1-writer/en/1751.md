The morning after an air raid, the person walking over broken glass was the prime minister. He could have stayed in a shelter. Instead he chose to walk, on his own feet, the streets London had just lost.

[[story]]
Winston Churchill had become prime minister in May 1940. France fell in June, and Britain stood alone. In that loneliness, the prime minister being seen on the street was a matter of morale. On 7 September, hundreds of German bombers attacked the docks in the east of London, and the bombing continued for fifty-seven nights in a row.

Larson's book follows Churchill and his family through that first year in office, from May 1940 on. The first big test of how he would lead came the day after the bombing began.

In Erik Larson's *The Splendid and the Vile*, Churchill visits damaged streets after the raids, and the people who live there see him. On some visits what he sees visibly shakes him. He does not hide it. Residents watch the prime minister walk among rubble and broken glass with their own eyes.

His wartime weekends were spent with his family at Chequers. The day after the first raid, going to see the damage was his first test. One of the areas he visited most was the east London districts near the docks. Larson describes people calling out to him in those streets, and no one forcing him back into shelter.

Larson also describes him climbing onto rooftops to watch the bombing. It was not a safe thing to do, but it made him visible. Those around him tried to draw him into the shelter, and those visits made them uneasy.

London was bombed for months, and the government and the city kept working. A leader standing in the open was not a plan in itself. Still, people could see him among them.

##Churchill stood in the worst streets, where people could see him.## The plan was being made elsewhere, but being visible was a separate job, done by walking through the rubble.

[[lessons]]
$$**In a crisis, being seen with your people matters as much as your plan.** The manager of a large shop could email the team about a supply problem and shut her office door. If she goes down to the loading dock and sees for herself what happened, the team knows she is in the problem with them. That can build trust even before the plan is finished. People rarely wait for the perfect plan, but they do notice who showed up.$$
