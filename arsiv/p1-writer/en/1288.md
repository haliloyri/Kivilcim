[[story]]
In *Can't Hurt Me*, Goggins describes growing up in New York in a household built around his father's roller rink. His father, Trunnis, ran it, and the whole family worked there most nights, often until midnight. Between the ages of six and eight, David's job was the skating shoes. If a pair was out of place, Trunnis beat him with a belt.

The family slept in the rink's office, where the loud music made sleep close to impossible. Trunnis beat Goggins's mother with a belt when she disobeyed. When David stepped in to protect her, the belt came down on him as well. After she took him to the hospital for an ear infection, Trunnis beat her again.

When David was eight, his mother talked Trunnis into letting her have a credit card in her own name. A sympathetic neighbor helped her plan the escape. She and David drove to Brazil, Indiana. His older brother stayed with their father, and David saw him only occasionally after that.

Leaving did not make life easy. In Indiana they lived in public housing, paying seven dollars a month in rent, on his mother's part-time pay and a welfare check of $123 a month. Trunnis sent very little money.

##In third grade David developed a nervous stutter.## His hair began to fall out and patches of his skin lost color, which the book ties to toxic stress. Nights at the rink had already left him behind in class. He was the only Black child in his room, and some teachers shouted at him, mocked him and called him stupid.

He was threatened with expulsion and with placement in a special-needs facility. To stay in school he cheated on homework and tests, and by his teens he could barely read.

[[lessons]]
$$**Separate what happened to you from what you do next.** You may not be responsible for something that happened to you, and the next step still belongs to you. A student who fails an exam and thinks the questions were unfair can still decide which topic to study before the next one. That lets you acknowledge what happened without pretending it didn't, and take the next small move into your own hands instead of waiting for the circumstances to change first.$$
