[[story]]
Psychologist Susan David builds *Emotional Agility* on a refusal to treat emotions as something to remove. Feelings, she argues, are data and not directives: they tell you something about what matters to you, and they do not tell you what to do. She also writes that emotions signpost our values. Suppressing or ignoring them, she argues, backfires and can raise stress, and forced positivity is a trap. She introduced the concept to business readers in a 2013 Harvard Business Review article written with Christina Congleton.

Trouble starts when we get hooked, her word for being caught in a thought or feeling and reacting to it on autopilot. We are more likely to get hooked when we are distracted, running on autopilot or in the grip of a difficult emotion. The book's first step, showing up, means noticing the emotion without pushing it away or being carried off by it. David suggests labeling it, so that "I am a failure" becomes "I am having the thought that I am a failure."

Values come in at the third step, walking your why. David describes them as a kind of psychological keel that keeps you steady. Finding yours takes more work than it sounds, because many of our choices are not fully our own. One of her examples is a person who believes she likes golf when the real pull is that her friends play.

Knowing what you care about is only half the process of walking your why. The rest is acting on it, and the fourth step does that through tiny tweaks. The order the book offers is plain: notice the feeling, ask which value it points to, then choose an action that fits the value, whether or not the feeling fades.

[[lessons]]
$$**Let the feeling name the value, then let the value pick the action.** David treats an emotion as a signpost to something you care about, and it is the value, not the feeling, that steadies you. A manager who feels a knot of dread before giving a colleague hard feedback can ask what the dread points to, see that she cares about fairness, and shape feedback that is both fair and clear. The knot may still be there while she speaks, and the conversation can happen anyway.$$
