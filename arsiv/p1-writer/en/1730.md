A man flew to Europe for three weeks with one question: would he rebuild his company or shut it down? He packed an experiment with email as well. He came back with an answer, though not the one he expected.

[[story]]
Tim Ferriss founded BrainQUICKEN in 2001, an internet-based sports-nutrition supplements business. According to one source his days stretched to fourteen hours. That overwork, and the lack of any free time, is where *The 4-Hour Workweek* began.

At some point he had to decide whether to redesign the business or close it altogether. To think it through, he went to Europe for three weeks.

While he was there he tried an experiment with email. He checked his inbox far less often than before and wanted to see what happened when he looked less. He ended up with a routine of checking email only once a day.

According to Ferriss, the experiment showed him his own role. In the company he was the point where information and decisions jammed, the bottleneck. Staff and customers were waiting on him for answers and decisions.

##What slowed the company down was an owner who was always within reach.##

He reorganised the company so that people could solve problems without asking him first, and he handed small daily tasks to virtual assistants. After the three weeks he did not stop travelling: he moved through Europe, Asia and South America, and worked out his email and outsourcing system on the road.

That became the central claim of the book: you can shorten your working hours by taking yourself out of the way as the bottleneck. The method is summed up as D-E-A-L: definition, elimination, automation and liberation. Elimination means cutting what does not matter, and automation means handing the rest to other people or systems. Ferriss's own company is the case on which he builds that argument.

The account is Ferriss's own telling, and no independent source has confirmed the company's figures.

[[lessons]]
$$**If everything waits for you, you are the limit.** Picture a kitchen where every order has to be cleared with the head cook; the team brings each small decision to one person, and everything slows. In Ferriss's company the problems stopped coming to him once he set it up so that staff could settle them. If you decide in advance which decisions other people can make in your place, you free up your time and theirs, and nobody has to sit and wait for your reply.$$
