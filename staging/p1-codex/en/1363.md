Sheryl Sandberg wanted exactly one thing, and she said so to a friend: Option A, the one where her husband was still alive. The friend didn't soften his answer. Option A was gone. The work now was making Option B as good as it could be. That exchange gave her the title of a book.

[[story]]
In May 2015, Sheryl Sandberg's husband, Dave Goldberg, died suddenly. The family was on holiday. Sandberg found him after he collapsed in a hotel gym. They had two young children. She was a senior executive at Facebook and the author of *Lean In*, and none of that prepared her for the days that followed.

In *Option B*, written with the psychologist Adam Grant, Sandberg describes a stretch when she could think of only one wish: for Dave to be back. She didn't want a better way to cope. She wanted Option A.

A friend told her plainly that Option A wasn't available. Since it wasn't, the task was to make Option B as good as possible. He did not offer comfort in the usual shape. He offered a direction.

##Naming that Option A was gone did not make her feel worse. It gave her somewhere to start.## Adam Grant was a friend before he was a co-author. He is a psychologist, and as she began to function again he helped her look at what research said about people in her situation. He kept asking questions she hadn't asked herself.

What they found became the argument of the book. Resilience is not a fixed trait that some people are born with and others lack. It can be built. The building starts with being honest about the loss, and it depends on other people showing up with specific help.

Sandberg doesn't say that the pain shrinks quickly or that the loss turns into a lesson. She says the opposite: Option A remained the thing she wanted. What changed was her view of what she could do with the days in front of her, and who was willing to do them with her.

[[lessons]]
$$**Naming the loss opens a door.** Saying aloud that Option A is gone didn't erase the wish. Someone laid off can say "the job I had is gone," and only then ask what the best next move looks like this week.$$

$$**Resilience has company.** The friend who named Option B and the psychologist with the research both did something concrete. When a friend's mother is in hospital, "I'll bring dinner on Tuesday" helps more than "is there anything I can do?"$$
