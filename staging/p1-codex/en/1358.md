[[open]]
Seth Godin uses an old word for a modern problem. He calls a group of people who are connected to each other, to a leader and to an idea a tribe. Groups like that have always existed. What changed is how easy they became to start, and Godin says the thing now holding most people back is not the tools. It's the exposure.

[[story]]
The word tribe sounds ancient because the thing is ancient. People have always gathered around shared interests and someone willing to lead. In Godin's framing, those groups used to be limited by where you lived. Your tribe was whoever happened to be nearby.

The internet removed that limit. Someone interested in an unusual craft, a niche way of working, or an unpopular opinion can now find others who care about the same thing from anywhere. A tribe no longer needs a town. It needs a shared outlook.

This is the part of Godin's argument that matters most for marketers. He doesn't treat customers as a crowd to be addressed. He treats them as people who want to connect, and he gives the marketer a job beyond selling: helping the members of a group find and support one another. The connection between members, he suggests, is part of what holds the group together.

Then why doesn't everyone do it? Godin's answer is uncomfortable. Leading means being visible. Visible people can be criticized or rejected, and many choose to wait instead. The risk of standing up feels larger than the risk of staying quiet, even though staying quiet leaves the group unformed.

##For Godin, leading is a choice to give people something to move toward together, and it has nothing to do with holding a title.## Almost anyone can do it, because it asks for less than people assume.

What does it ask for? A point of view. A steady voice. Something you truly care about, shown consistently. Godin doesn't describe a large production or a big launch. He describes showing up in the same recognizable way until the people who share your outlook notice you.

Notice what that implies about timing. Nobody can build a tribe in a day, and nobody can build one without saying anything. The first step is to say the thing, in your own voice, to the few people who might care.

[[lessons]]
$$**Three links hold a tribe.** A leader and an idea aren't enough. A running club where members only talk to the coach stays an audience. Once two runners start swapping tips in the group chat, it begins to hold together.$$

$$**The empty seat stays empty.** Fear of criticism is the usual reason a group never forms. Someone who loves restoring old bicycles may wait years for permission, while other restorers are searching for exactly that person.$$

$$**Be steady before you are impressive.** A tribe forms around a consistent point of view. One honest post about your craft every week does more than a big launch announcement.$$

[[reflect]]
- A group you belong to, like a hobby forum or a class chat. Who connects its members to each other?
- Something you care about and rarely say aloud, such as a niche hobby.
- A place where people seem to be waiting for someone to start, like a neighbourhood with no regular meetups.

&&What is one thing you could say regularly, in your own voice, that the right people might be waiting to hear?&&
