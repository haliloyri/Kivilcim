Lynne McTaggart asks a question about numbers. If one person's intention matters, what happens when a thousand people hold the same one at the same time? She argues that the answer is more than a thousand times one. Her advice built on that idea is small, practical and easy to try, even if the science behind it is not settled.

[[story]]
In *The Intention Experiment*, McTaggart moves from the individual to the group. The earlier chapters deal with what one person's intention might do. Here she asks about people focusing on the same aim together.

Her proposal is that a group has a stronger effect than the people in it would have separately. The effect, in her telling, is greater than the sum of what each person contributes.

To explain it, she turns to the idea of a field. In her framing, living things don't exist in separate bubbles. They exist inside a shared field that links them and lets them influence each other. When many people focus the same way at the same moment, the field is strengthened.

She presents group intention as something that can be tested, with large numbers of people sending the same intention at the same time. The claim itself is simple: a group's intention is stronger than one person's.

##This is also where her advice becomes practical.## She doesn't ask readers to run a laboratory. She suggests that you agree with the people you love, or with a community you belong to, to hold a shared intention for good at set moments. It should be small and deliberate, and it should be done together.

Notice how little this depends on the physics. A family that sets aside two minutes each evening to think of a relative who is ill has done something real, whatever a field does or doesn't do. They have paid attention to the same person at the same time, and each of them knows the others are doing it.

The claims about the field are McTaggart's, and they have not been independently established. Treat them as an idea to hold lightly. The shared moment doesn't need the idea to be true in order to be worth having.

[[lessons]]
$$**The ritual works on the group.** Agreeing on a time and a purpose turns scattered goodwill into something people can count on. A family that lights a candle every Friday for a relative abroad knows everyone is thinking of the same person, whatever a field does or doesn't do.$$

$$**Hold the field idea lightly.** McTaggart's explanation is a proposal. You can borrow the practice without the physics, the way you join a team's moment of silence for a colleague because you want to stand with them.$$
