[[open]]
A Boston construction executive steps off a plane in Haiti wearing bright polyester plaid golf pants. The young medical student who has come to meet him is skeptical, and the outfit does nothing to help. What eventually turns the student around has nothing to do with clothes, and nothing to do with a sales pitch either.

[[story]]
Tracy Kidder's *Mountains Beyond Mountains* follows the doctor Paul Farmer, and one of the people who made Farmer's work possible was Thomas J. White, the man in the plaid golf pants. In 1983 White was president of J.F. White Contracting Co., a Boston construction firm, and he flew to Haiti to meet Farmer, then a young medical student working with poor communities there. Farmer was skeptical of him.

According to Partners In Health's own tribute, White won him over through his emotional response to the poverty and suffering he saw. It was a reaction, not an argument, that closed the distance between a contractor and a student. A few years later White helped found Partners In Health with a donation of $1 million, and the organization took shape in 1987 to fund the work in the village of Cange.

He did not stop at one gift. White sold his company, his assets and even his house to keep paying for PIH's projects, and over the years he gave away tens of millions of dollars. Nearly all the projects of the organization's first decade rested on his money.

The money bought plain, checkable things. There was a small clinic in Cange, a microscope, and PIH's first program for multidrug-resistant tuberculosis, which cost $30,000 per patient. Patients cured with those drugs helped show that MDR-TB could be treated in poor countries, and that evidence contributed to a change in World Health Organization policy. Today the main referral site for MDR-TB patients in Haiti, in Cange, is the Thomas J. White pavilion.

White died in 2011, aged 90. About a month earlier he had sent Ophelia Dahl, PIH's co-founder, a check for $5,000, all he could afford after more than two decades of giving. Dahl said of him, "Tom fought all his life against 'stupid deaths.'"

Look at the order of events. The visit came first, and the funding came after it. A contractor could have read a report about Haiti from his office in Boston and decided that someone else should deal with it. Instead he went, stood among the people his money would later reach, and let a skeptical young man watch how he reacted. Everything on the list that followed, a clinic, a microscope, drugs, was something Farmer could point at and verify.

[[lessons]]
$$**Go and see the thing yourself before you decide what to give or fund.** A report tells you the size of a problem, but standing in front of it tells you whether you will actually keep paying for it. White's trip came before his money, and his reaction was something a doubtful partner could read for himself. If you are about to approve a budget, back a colleague's project or promise your time, ask what you have seen with your own eyes. If the answer is nothing, a visit may be your cheapest next step.$$
