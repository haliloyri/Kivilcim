A concrete cell about three feet by nine, with no window and a single light bulb that never goes off. The man inside has no say over any of it. Each night iron shackles are locked around his legs, and the bulb burns on overhead.

[[story]]
Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman's *The Daily Stoic* keeps returning to Epictetus, the former slave who taught that the only things truly ours are our own judgments and actions. This story is about one man who took that teaching into the worst place imaginable: James Stockdale, a US Navy pilot.

On 9 September 1965 Stockdale was flying from the carrier USS Oriskany over North Vietnam when his A-4 Skyhawk was disabled by enemy fire. He ejected and came down in a small village, where he was badly beaten and taken prisoner. He would spend about seven and a half years in Hoa Lo prison, the "Hanoi Hilton", and was released on 12 February 1973.

As the senior naval officer among the prisoners, he helped organise their resistance. Later he was among eleven men held in a part of the camp nicknamed "Alcatraz", in windowless concrete cells with a light on all the time. At night the shackles went on. His leg was broken twice under torture.

He credited Stoic philosophy, especially Epictetus's *Enchiridion*, with helping him get through it. One episode shows what that meant in practice. In the summer of 1969, held in leg irons and tortured again and again, his captors planned to parade him in public for propaganda. He could not stop the guards, the irons or the interrogations. But the one thing still in his hands was his own appearance.

So he cut his scalp with a razor to disfigure himself. When the guards covered his head with a hat, he beat his own face with a stool. On another occasion, discovered with information that could have put his friends at risk, he harmed himself again to avoid being forced into a confession. His Medal of Honor citation describes the wound as near-mortal.

Stockdale got the medal from President Gerald Ford at the White House on 4 March 1976. After his return he spent about twelve years at the Hoover Institution, beginning in 1981, where he studied Stoicism and Epictetus.

What stands out is how narrow the field of choice became, and how completely he used it. A man who had no say over the door, the light or the chain still located the one decision his captors could not make for him. He found the last lever within reach and pulled it with full strength.

[[lessons]]
$$**When everything around you is out of reach, find the one small thing still in your hands and put all your effort there.** Someone stuck in a job that is about to be cut cannot decide the restructuring, but can decide how well and how honestly the next month of work is done. A parent facing a long illness in the family cannot decide the diagnosis, yet can choose how present to be in the room. Once you name that one thing, the rest of the situation stops pulling at you and your energy has a place to go, and you can take one small step each day instead of waiting.$$
