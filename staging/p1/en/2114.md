A fishing boat is passing a rock in the South Pacific where nobody lives. From the deck the skipper spots a burned patch on a cliff and finds it odd. He turns the boat toward the shore to take a closer look. Somebody aboard says he has heard a human voice, and the skipper answers that it is only birds.

[[story]]
Rutger Bregman's *Humankind* sets a real shipwreck against William Golding's *Lord of the Flies*, where stranded schoolboys turn on one another. The real one began in June 1965 in Nuku'alofa, the capital of Tonga. Six boys from a boarding school decided to run away, and they took a boat that was not theirs. One of them, Mano Totau, later said they meant to reach Fiji, and the wind was good that night.

A storm built in the middle of the night, and the wind tore the sail. For eight days the boat drifted with nothing to eat, and the boys drank rainwater collected in cans. On the eighth day they sighted ʻAta, a small island with no one on it. They swam ashore.

On land they sorted themselves out quickly. They split the work into kitchen, garden and guard duty, and they began and ended each day with songs and prayers. Later they found the ruins of an old village in the crater, with wild chickens, taro and bananas left behind. When one boy broke a leg, the others set it with sticks and looked after him while it mended.

They quarrelled too. Sione Fataua told *People* magazine in 2020 what happened when someone was truly angry: "you disappear for a few hours, look at the ocean and clear it out of your mind." Afterwards they talked, said sorry and prayed. Then it was over.

On 11 September 1966, fifteen months after they left, an Australian lobster fisherman named Peter Warner noticed burned patches on the cliffs of ʻAta from his boat, the *Just David*. Stephen, one of the six, swam out to meet it. According to Totau, a voice had been heard and Warner first put it down to birds. Then they saw Stephen in the water and five long-haired boys on the beach.

Warner radioed Nuku'alofa with the boys' names. The operator answered: "These boys have been given up for dead. Funerals have been held." Back in Tonga the boys were arrested, because the boat's owner pressed charges. According to published accounts, Warner paid the owner from the sale of the story to an Australian television station, and the charges were dropped.

Accounts differ on the boys' ages and on who did what, and most details come from the boys' own memories and later press reports. What they agree on is the shape of those fifteen months: a work roster, prayers morning and evening, and a fixed way to cool down after a fight.

[[lessons]]
$$**Agree on how to cool down and make up before the next fight starts.** In a shared flat or a small team, quarrels tend to get worse when nobody knows what to do once voices rise, because every person improvises a different exit. A fixed routine, such as stepping away for an hour and then speaking one at a time, takes the guesswork out of those minutes. You can then spend your energy on the problem itself, and nobody has to argue about who is allowed to leave the room or who has to speak first.$$
