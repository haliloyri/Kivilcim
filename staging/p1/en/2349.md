In a village called Wimbe, a family of tobacco farmers is down to one meal a day, sometimes less. The maize has failed and the store is empty. Their only son, fourteen, has just been sent home from secondary school after a few weeks. He is holding an unpaid bill and a great deal of empty time.

[[story]]
The fees came to roughly eighty dollars, according to published accounts of William Kamkwamba's life. For most families that is a modest sum. For the Kamkwambas, in the years 2001 and 2002, it was out of reach.

The cause was hunger. In 2001 the rains failed across Malawi and the maize crop collapsed. The family farmed tobacco and had seven children, and what they ate depended largely on the maize they grew themselves. By 2002 many families in Wimbe were getting by on one meal a day, or less. Money for school was the first thing to go, because food could not be postponed and a classroom could.

So William, who was fourteen, lost his place a few weeks after term began. Nobody had done anything wrong to him. There was no unkind teacher and no rule broken, just a harvest that did not come and a price that could not be paid. His memoir, *The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind*, is famous for a windmill, yet its first chapters live in this year, before any blade or wire existed.

What happened next is easy to skip because it looks so small. Kamkwamba has said that he could not accept that future and meant to do whatever he could to get an education. That is a decision, not a plan. He had no money, no teacher and no classroom. What he did have was a habit of wanting to understand how things work, and a small library in the district that the school gate could not lock.

Consider the choices that were open to him. He could have been angry, he could have waited for a rescue, or he could have asked relatives for help that they did not have. All of those are human, and none of them would have changed that particular week. The question that remained was not who to blame but what was still within reach today.

Asking it that way is the quiet skill in this episode. A famine, an unpaid fee, an empty desk and an empty kitchen arrived together. In a year when so many doors closed at once, he looked for the one that had not, and everything that followed came through it.

[[lessons]]
$$**When a setback closes your road, name the thing you refuse to lose and look for another way to reach it.** When a promotion falls through, a project is cancelled or a school gate shuts, the first reflex is often to drop the goal along with the route. Usually only the route has gone, which feels like the end but rarely is, and the goal is still standing there. Ask yourself who can no longer give you this and where else you could get it. Once you can answer that question, a loss becomes a starting point, and the next small step usually shows itself.$$
