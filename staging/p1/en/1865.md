A crowd grows outside a house on Garland Avenue where someone has only just moved in. Eleven people are inside. One shot will kill a man watching from a porch across the street. The owner is a doctor, and the trial ahead of him will bring in one of the most famous defence lawyers in the country.

[[story]]
Isabel Wilkerson's *The Warmth of Other Suns* follows Black Americans who left the South during the Great Migration. This story is about one of the tensions that migration produced, an event in Detroit in 1925. Ossian Sweet was a Black doctor. He had been born in Florida, took his medical degree at Howard University and worked at Dunbar Hospital in Detroit.

On 7 June 1925 he bought the house at 2905 Garland Street for $18,500. The neighbourhood was entirely white. Sweet moved in on 8 September, carrying his belongings through the door. Eleven Black people were in the house with him, and his wife Gladys stayed with them. The next day the crowd outside was larger still.

A shot came from the house. Leon Breiner, a white man watching from a porch across the street, was hit and died. Another man, Eric Houghberg, was wounded in the leg. All eleven people from the house were charged with murder.

The NAACP took on the case. It first hired one lawyer, then in early October approached Clarence Darrow. Darrow accepted, and on 15 October it was announced that he would lead the defence. The first trial, before Judge Frank Murphy, ended in late November with a hung jury. By then the number of defendants had fallen to eight, because charges against three had been dropped.

In April 1926 a new trial opened for Henry Sweet. An all-white jury acquitted him. The prosecutor, Robert Toms, then dropped the charges against the seven who remained.

What stands out is less the purchase of the house than the structure that formed around it afterwards. The house was bought as a right, but defending that right was more than one man could carry. An organisation, the NAACP, found the lawyers, brought Darrow into the case and kept the defence going through two separate trials. Sweet's claim had to be proven in a courtroom, and that took a team, a strategy and an experienced advocate. The right was his from the day he signed. Keeping it took everyone who stood behind him, across two trials and more than half a year.

[[lessons]]
$$**Before you take a hard step, decide who will stand behind you.** Being right does not mean you will pay the price alone, because heavy work usually leans on a team. When you say no to a client, ask for a raise or carry a mistake up to senior management, the support should be agreed long before the day it is needed. Who will speak for you, who will collect the documents, and whom will you call when things turn rough? With those answers ready, you can meet the objections that come later with much more calm.$$
