In Cache, Oklahoma, the owner of a twenty-two-room house is entertaining the President of the United States. The two plan to ride out together after wolves. The host is a Comanche who fought the same government years earlier. Now he sits at the same table with its president.

[[story]]
The host is Quanah Parker, one of the central figures of S. C. Gwynne's *Empire of the Summer Moon*. His mother was Cynthia Ann Parker and his father the Comanche chief Peta Nocona. According to the Texas State Historical Association's Handbook, under army pressure and hunger Quanah and the Quahadi Comanche gave up their independence and moved to the Kiowa-Comanche reservation. Published accounts say he led them to Fort Sill between late May and early June of 1875.

Surrender was not the end of the story, only the start of a new problem. The Comanche were not one body but separate bands, and on the reservation no one led them as a whole. Federal agents named Quanah principal chief to unite the bands. His people had not chosen him. The community, left without a leader, accepted the appointment. He kept the role for about a quarter of a century and promoted schools, ranching and self-sufficiency.

He built a twenty-two-room house at Cache, where he lived with seven wives and many children. The house is known as the Star House, and it was entered on the National Register of Historic Places in 1970. In April 1905 Theodore Roosevelt visited him, and the two hunted wolves near Frederick. The man the army had once chased was now riding with the president.

Published accounts also name him as an early leader of the Native American Church, and he held peyote ceremonies on the reservation. That can be read as an effort to carry old beliefs into the new order.

Quanah fell ill on February 11, 1911, while visiting the Cheyenne reservation, and died on February 23. A year earlier, in 1910, he had had his mother's remains moved to Post Oak Mission Cemetery. In 1957 both were reburied at the Fort Sill Post Cemetery.

Look at what he kept while the rules changed. After losing the war he learned the reservation's rules, accepted a post the government had handed him and turned to new work such as schools and ranches. He still kept his name, stayed at the head of his people and moved his mother's remains. The role was granted from outside, so it could be withdrawn at any moment, but how to use it was left to him.

[[lessons]]
$$**When the rules change, update your methods and keep the ties that hold you up.** When you join a new company, get a new manager or move to another city, the old methods often stop working, sometimes within a week. Learning new methods does not mean giving up who you are. Write down first what is not up for negotiation for you, then fit everything else to the new order, and tell someone you trust where that line sits. A line like that steers you through the change and makes each new offer easier to judge: is this changing my method, or me?$$
