A girl who had never sat in a classroom taught herself enough maths and grammar to take an entrance test. Years later she walked into a hall at Cambridge as a graduate student. Between those two scenes lies a short list of institutions you can name one by one.

[[story]]
According to published accounts of her life, Tara Westover was seventeen when she first entered a classroom. Her memoir *Educated* is the story of how a child raised in a survivalist Mormon family in Idaho got from there to a doctorate, and the outline of that route is public.

She prepared for the entrance exam on her own, learning enough maths and grammar to be accepted at Brigham Young University in Utah. She received her bachelor's degree there in 2008. Then she was awarded a Gates Cambridge Scholarship, and she went to Trinity College, Cambridge, where she completed a master's degree in political thought and intellectual history.

In 2010, according to her biography, she was a visiting fellow at Harvard University. After that she returned to Cambridge and completed a PhD in history in 2014.

Seen from the end, the route looks like a plan. Seen from the start, it can only have looked like a few unrelated tasks, and that difference matters. The person at the start of a long journey never sees the whole thing; she sees an entrance test and nothing past it. A later door is simply not visible yet, so it cannot weigh on the person standing at the first one.

Look at the shape of the path. It does not run in a straight line from the first classroom to the doctorate. It is a sequence of separate doors, each with its own entry requirement: a test for the first, a scholarship application for the second, a fellowship for the third, and a long piece of research work at the end. Each door was a task of a size one person could finish before looking at the next.

No account of her life says this was a method, and a single case proves no formula. Many things on the way, from teachers to timing, depended on people and circumstances that nobody can copy. What the record does show is that a distance which sounds impossible in one sentence is made of steps that were each concrete and each finishable, even if the whole was far bigger than any one of them. That is the part a reader can borrow, whatever the details of her own situation are.

[[lessons]]
$$**Plan the next door and leave the whole staircase unwritten.** Westover's route from a first classroom to a doctorate looks huge as one sentence, but it is a list of entries: an exam, a scholarship, a fellowship, a thesis. When you set a goal that feels too large, such as a career change or a new field, name only the next entry requirement and do it this month. Doing so turns the size of the goal from a reason to wait into a short, workable list of tasks, and each finished door shows you what the following one asks of you.$$
