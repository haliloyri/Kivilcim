Colorado, August, a cold dark hour before dawn. Ann Trason stands on the line of a hundred-mile race with seven Tarahumara runners from Mexico beside her. A helicopter circles above, an HBO crew films from the side, and ahead lies a long climb.

[[story]]
The 1994 Leadville 100 was one of the American races in which the Raramuri runners of Christopher McDougall's *Born to Run* showed up. Since 1992 the promoters Kitty Williams and Rick Fisher had been bringing runners from Mexico's Copper Canyons to ultramarathons in the United States. The first team drew attention but, held back by cultural differences and other difficulties, did not get the results it wanted. In 1993 Victoriano Churro and Cerrildo Chacarito finished first and second at Leadville. For 1994, Fisher and Williams brought seven.

Ann Trason lined up too. The Californian had won the Silver State 50 Mile in May in 7:29, then taken her sixth Western States 100 in a row, finishing second overall behind Tim Twietmeyer. At Leadville, a local, Johnny Sandoval, led from the gun and had a thirty-minute gap by mile thirty.

At the foot of Hope Pass, a chase group of five Tarahumara and Trason caught him. Juan Herrera, Martimano Cervantez and Trason traded the lead. Trason pulled ahead on the way up the pass and reached Winfield, the fifty-mile turnaround, in first place in under nine hours.

The second half was a different race. Herrera first closed on Cervantez, then reeled in Trason around Turquoise Lake until only a few minutes separated them with a half marathon still to run. There he passed her. Whether her legs had slowed at that point nobody can say from the record, which shows only that the order changed.

Herrera finished in 17:30, a course record at the time. Trason came in second thirty-six minutes behind him, in 18:06, and her time is still the women's course record at Leadville. All these years later it shows how strong a run that second place was.

What the result shows is how little the standings at the midpoint said about the finish. Being first at Winfield got Trason to mile fifty in under nine hours. It did not run the last half marathon around Turquoise Lake for her. Herrera was still gaining in that stretch. He had traded the lead with her on the way to Hope Pass, and in the final miles he took the race from the runner who had led the first half.

[[lessons]]
$$**Pace the first half of a long job so the second half still has something in it.** Suppose a small workshop gets a rush of orders and you finish the first week's batch early, even working through a night. That speed tells you what you can do in a week, and nothing about the fourth. Look at who is still gaining late in the effort, not only at who led at the midpoint. It lets you set your own tempo for the final stretch and keeps you from reading a rival's early lead as the final order.$$

[[reflect]]
&&On your next long project, how much energy and time does your current pace leave you for the final stretch?&&
