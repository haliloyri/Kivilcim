One morning in England a perfect circle appears in a wheat field. Newspapers print "aliens", and the people studying it say human hands could not have made it. Years later two older men show how they did it with a plank and some rope.

[[story]]
Carl Sagan tells this story in *The Demon-Haunted World*. Two Englishmen, Doug Bower and Dave Chorley, were sneaking into wheat fields at night and flattening the crop into circles. Their kit was plain: a plank, a length of rope, and a cap fitted with a bit of wire. The wire gave them a sighting aid, so they could hold a straight line while they trampled.

The circles drew attention quickly. Some people studied the crushed stalks and declared that wind could not have done this, nor any known force. Their opinions reached the press, and "mystery circles" grew into an industry of its own. Each time the two men went out, they fed that industry a little more material.

One night Bower and Chorley pushed further. They pressed a message into the crop: "WE ARE NOT ALONE." They signed it with two letters D, the initials of their own first names. Bower's wife Ilene began to wonder where her husband was disappearing to after dark.

In 1991 the men confessed. They went further and made another circle for reporters, using the same plank, the same rope and the same cap. A leading believer had earlier pronounced one of these formations genuine. It turned out not to be.

So why did it take so long? Because the believers were asking the wrong question. They asked, "Can nature do this?" and the answer was no. Not one of them asked, "Can a person do this in a single night?" The two questions lead to different places: one to outer space, the other to a plank.

That is also why the demonstration after the confession mattered. Once the men made a circle with reporters watching, the question was settled beyond argument. The surest way to knock down a claim is to produce the same result by a far simpler method.

What stands out is that neither man had any knowledge of space. They had their hands and a plank, nothing more. What inflated the affair was a single assumption held by the people studying it: something this neat could not be human work. Yet people, given enough patience, can produce remarkably neat things.

[[lessons]]
$$**Before reaching for an extraordinary explanation, ask the ordinary question: who could have done this, and what tool did they have in their hands?** Why a customer left, why a number suddenly fell, why a system crashed: in each case a grand cause comes to mind first. But a plain possibility, something as humble as a plank and a rope, often produces the same result. Rule out the most ordinary explanation first, then discuss the extraordinary one. That saves you time and keeps you from committing to a story that was never true.$$
