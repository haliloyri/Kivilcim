A man walks off a tennis court in west Beirut. A few minutes later he is lying in the trunk of a car, and the next time he walks anywhere free, six years and nine months will have gone by. The car takes him to a secret location, and that is where the counting starts.

[[story]]
Terry Anderson was the Associated Press's chief Middle East correspondent, reporting on a Lebanese civil war that had already run for a decade. On 16 March 1985 he finished a game of tennis in west Beirut. Armed men took him off the street, put him in the trunk of a car and drove him away. They were Shiite militants, and accounts name them either as Hezbollah or as the Islamic Jihad Organization.

They held him until 4 December 1991. By most counts that is 2,454 days, and a few sources say 2,455. Either way it comes to more than 350 weeks, or roughly 58,900 hours. The civil war itself had ended in 1990, so his captivity outlasted the fighting that had brought him to Beirut. He was moved from place to place during those years, and press accounts say about a year and a half of it was spent in solitary confinement. He ranks among the longest-held Western hostages taken in Lebanon. His sister, Peggy Say, is credited in those accounts with the campaign that kept his name in the news.

Howard Cutler and the Dalai Lama bring him into The Art of Happiness near the end, in the part about spiritual values. What Anderson credited with getting him through was prayer and religion. The source he named was a body of belief and a practice of praying. In a 1995 interview he described himself as a Christian.

Think about what that stretch of time takes away. The people holding him decided where he was and when he was moved. Prayer and religion sit in another category, because they need no permission, no equipment and no cooperation from anyone else. Whatever the days contained, this was something nobody had to hand him and nobody could easily take out of his hands.

The authors use him because the idea is easy to wave away in comfortable times. When life runs smoothly, a faith or a set of convictions can look like decoration. Anderson's six years and nine months show what one is for. He pointed to it as the thing that got him through a span that had no short version.

Anderson was freed on 4 December 1991. He died in April 2024, aged 76.

[[lessons]]
$$**Decide what holds you steady before the long stretch begins.** When a hard period has no end date, such as months of looking after a sick parent or years of a business that is struggling, willpower alone tends to run low, and what remains is whatever you already believe and already practise. A nurse who has a fixed evening ritual, a friend she calls and a conviction she returns to still has something to stand on after a terrible week. Work out which of these you have, and use it on an easy day, so that it feels familiar when a hard one arrives.$$
