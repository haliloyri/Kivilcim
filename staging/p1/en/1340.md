Every night, Arianna Huffington switches off her phone and takes it out of the bedroom. It doesn't charge on the nightstand. It doesn't sit face down within reach. For one of the most connected media figures of her era, the most important part of going to bed is sending the device somewhere else.

[[story]]
The routine didn't come from nowhere. Huffington built it after she collapsed from exhaustion, and in *The Sleep Revolution* she walks through it step by step. None of it is exotic. What stands out is how deliberately each piece separates the day from the night.

It starts with the devices. She turns off her phone and her other screens and moves them out of the bedroom altogether, so nothing is charging beside her bed. The point isn't a promise to herself not to look. The phone is simply not there to look at.

Then comes a hot bath. For her it's part of the ritual of closing the day, a clear step between being busy and getting ready to sleep.

After the bath, she changes into clothes meant only for sleeping. Not gym clothes, which belong to another part of her day. Dedicated sleepwear sends a simple signal: this part of the day is for sleep and nothing else.

##Last, she reads, and she's specific about what: real, printed books.## Not work documents, and not anything on a screen. The reading belongs to the night, not to the job she has just put away.

She explains why the screen matters. The blue light from phones and tablets suppresses melatonin, the hormone that helps signal to the body that it's time to sleep. So scrolling in bed isn't only a matter of losing track of time. The light itself works against the very thing she's trying to do.

Taken together, the steps are almost modest. Switch off, move the devices out, take a bath, change clothes, open a paper book. Each one is small enough to do on an ordinary evening. What they share is that none of them depends on willpower at the last minute. The phone isn't resisted. It's in another room.

For someone whose work lived on constant updates, that is a striking choice. The news would keep moving overnight. Her phone would simply not be in the room while it did.

[[lessons]]
$$**Distance beats willpower.** Huffington doesn't rely on promising herself not to check her phone. She charges it outside the bedroom. A device in another room asks nothing of you at midnight, while one on the nightstand asks you to say no again and again. Moving it once in the evening settles the question for the whole night.$$

$$**Small cues end the day.** A hot bath and a change into clothes meant only for sleep are ordinary acts. Neither takes much effort. Repeated every night, they mark a clear line: work and exercise are over, and this is the part of the day for rest.$$

$$**What you look at matters too.** It isn't only when you go to bed. Screen light suppresses melatonin, the hormone that helps signal sleep. Swapping the screen for a printed book changes what your eyes are taking in during those last minutes before sleep.$$
