After crossing a whole desert, Santiago is finally at the pyramids, digging in the sand where he feels the treasure should be. Then men come upon him.

What happens next is a beating, a robbery, and a mocking story from a stranger. That story turns out to be the most valuable thing anyone ever tells him.

[[story]]
This is the ending of Paulo Coelho's 1988 novel *The Alchemist*. Santiago left his life as a shepherd in Spain because of a dream: treasure hidden near the Egyptian pyramids. It has taken him a long journey across the desert to get here.

Now, at last, he stands before the pyramids and starts to dig.

Men come upon him as he digs. They beat him and take the gold he is carrying. When he explains what he is doing, that he is looking for a treasure he dreamed about, their leader mocks him.

The leader has a dream of his own, he says. It keeps coming back to him too. In it, there is treasure buried under a sycamore tree growing in the ruins of an abandoned church in Spain, a place where shepherds sleep. But he isn't fool enough, he says, to cross an entire desert just because of a dream.

He means it as a joke at Santiago's expense. Santiago hears something else entirely.

##He knows that church: it's the one where he slept with his sheep at the very start of his journey.##

The treasure was never at the pyramids. It was buried back where he began, under the sycamore in that ruined church.

So Santiago goes back to Spain. He returns to the abandoned church, digs beneath the sycamore, and finds a chest of treasure.

The irony is hard to miss. Two people had a dream about treasure. One of them refused to travel for it. The other crossed the desert, reached the pyramids, and failed to find anything there. Yet that failure, and the mocking words that came with it, is exactly what showed him where to dig.

The answer had been under him on the first night. He could only recognize it after going all the way to the pyramids.

[[lessons]]
$$**The long way can be the only way.** The treasure lay under the sycamore where Santiago slept at the start of the novel. On the first night, it was just a place to sleep with his sheep; nothing told him to dig. Sometimes the answer is close by, but you can only recognize it after a journey that seemed to lead somewhere else entirely. The trip to the pyramids wasn't wasted. It was what made the familiar place readable.$$

$$**Same signal, different choice.** The thief dreamed of the treasure too. His dream even pointed to the church and the sycamore. But he treated his dream as something only a fool would act on, and he kept nothing from it. Santiago acted, and the thief's own words handed him the location. Having the insight isn't what separates people; acting on it is.$$
