For years, Mark Manson could picture it clearly: himself on stage, living the rock-star life. He imagined it again and again. What he never did, in all that time, was the thing the picture required. So why did a dream he returned to so often never become a single rehearsal?

[[story]]
Manson tells this story about himself in The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck, and he uses it to take apart a question most of us ask all the time: what do I want?

His argument is that the question is nearly useless. Ask almost anyone and you get the same answers. Happiness. Success. A good body. Everyone wants the pleasant results, so wanting them tells you very little about a person.

Then there's his own case. For years he fantasized about being a rock star. He pictured himself on stage. But he never actually pursued it. The picture stayed a picture.

When he eventually looked at why, the answer wasn't a lack of desire. He clearly loved the result. ##What he didn't want was the struggle: the practice, finding bandmates, playing small gigs, lugging the gear.## He wanted the destination and had no interest in the road.

That led him to a better question. Not "what do you want?" but "what pain do you want in your life?" Put another way: what struggle are you willing to keep going through?

It sounds grim at first. It's actually clarifying. Every result comes with its own particular kind of difficulty attached. For a musician, it's the practice and the small gigs with heavy gear to carry. Choosing a goal, in Manson's view, means choosing that difficulty along with it. If you only want the reward, what you have may be a fantasy about the outcome rather than a real goal.

This is what Manson means when he says people are defined not by the rewards they want but by the struggles they are willing to choose. Plenty of people like the idea of the stage. The question worth asking is who chooses the rehearsals.

[[lessons]]
$$**Everyone wants the same rewards.** Manson's point about happiness and success is that these wants are almost universal. Saying you want them doesn't separate you from anyone, and it gives you no direction, because it says nothing about what you'll actually do on an ordinary day.$$

$$**A goal without the struggle may be a daydream.** Manson pictured the stage for years and never started the practice or the gigs. If you keep wanting something but never begin its unglamorous part, you may be in love with the outcome rather than committed to the goal.$$

$$**Pick the pain, not just the prize.** Asking "what pain do you want in your life?" makes you look at the daily cost. The musician's cost is practice and hauling gear. Every goal has its own version, and choosing the goal means signing up for that version too.$$

[[reflect]]
- Is there something you've said for years that you want, yet never started the unglamorous part of?
- Which hard, boring parts of your current work do you not mind at all?
- What might those parts say about what you actually want?
