Six ounces of water a day. A cabin so cold that moisture beads on the walls and frost forms on the windows. Three men are sharing a craft built for two, and the batteries have to last all the way home.

[[story]]
According to NASA's published account of Apollo 13, the command module had only about 15 minutes of power left when Houston told the crew to move into the lunar module. That is the situation Jim Lovell and Jeffrey Kluger build *Lost Moon* around. Aquarius had been designed to support two men for roughly two days. Now it had to carry three men for nearly four, and its batteries and water would not stretch that far at normal use.

The crew made one simple rule and applied it everywhere. They reduced their energy consumption to a fifth of normal, turning off every device that was not absolutely necessary. The water ration followed the same pattern: six ounces each per day, a fifth of normal intake. They lived on fruit juices and wet-pack foods, since hot water had been lost in the accident.

The cost to the men was real. The crew became dehydrated. Water condensed on the cabin walls and the windows frosted as the spacecraft cooled. By the end of the flight the three men had lost 31.5 pounds between them, according to the same account.

The rule worked. When Aquarius was finally jettisoned, the lunar module still had about 20 percent of its electrical power, and 28.2 pounds of water remained. The rule held for the whole trip, and the reserve at the end was small but real.

What deserves attention is the form of the rule, because a rule that is easy to say is easy to keep. A fifth is a fraction anyone can say aloud, check against a gauge, and apply without a meeting. It did not ask the astronauts to rank hundreds of small decisions every hour. They had one test for a switch or a cup of water, which was whether it was needed, and the answer was usually no.

It also made the cost visible. Six ounces of water a day is not enough to feel well, and the weight the crew lost shows it. A cut that is named, with its price attached, is easier to keep than one that pretends to be free.

[[lessons]]
$$**When a resource runs short, set one simple cut that everyone can remember and apply it everywhere.** A rule such as "a fifth of normal" beats a long list of case-by-case choices, because it saves attention as well as power. You can use it when a budget is cut, when a month's money or a team's time falls short, or when a household has a tight week to get through. Say out loud what the cut will cost people, check the gauge regularly, and keep your eye on the date the shortage has to last until.$$
