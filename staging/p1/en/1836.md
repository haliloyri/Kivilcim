[[open]]
On a Tuesday evening in Moscow, at half past seven, a man stands outside a bakery with a shopping bag. A foreigner walks past, carrying a different bag and eating a chocolate bar as he goes. Not a word passes between them, and yet everything has been said.

[[story]]
Oleg Gordievsky, the central figure of Ben Macintyre's *The Spy and the Traitor*, was passing information to Britain while serving in Soviet intelligence. This story is about a signalling arrangement set up for him. If Gordievsky ever fell into danger, there had to be a way to tell British intelligence, MI6, and to hear back from them whether the escape was on. A phone call, a letter or a face-to-face meeting was not safe under the eyes of the KGB.

The solution was silent. On Tuesday evenings at 7:30, Gordievsky would stand near a bakery in Moscow, around the Kutuzovsky district, holding a Safeway bag. That meant: I am ready to run. An MI6 officer who saw him would walk past with a Harrods bag, eating a chocolate bar as he went. That was the answer: "Understood. We are starting."

Nobody would speak, and not even eye contact would be needed. The bags came from ordinary British shops and would draw no attention on a foreigner in Moscow, yet those two bags, at that hour, on that corner, meant something to exactly two people.

The arrangement was set up years in advance, and for years it sat unused. Every Tuesday evening, month after month, a preparation stood ready on that street that nobody noticed. When Gordievsky came under threat in the summer of 1985, the preparation worked. He did not have to invent a new method. Going to a bakery with a bag was enough.

What is striking in this story is how ordinary the signal was. There was no secret device and no coded message. There was a scene agreed in advance, and the scene itself was so plain it would arouse no suspicion. The value of such an arrangement lies in the fact that it stands through the years it is unused, and then works with a single step on the day it is needed.

The signal's strength lay in one thing: if danger was near, what could be seen had to look like nothing at all. A man carrying a bag and a foreigner carrying a bag meant nothing to anyone watching. The silence of the arrangement was its insurance. Both sides knew the same scene, and neither had to say a word in it.

[[lessons]]
$$**Set a signal, a plan and a fallback route even for the day you hope never comes.** When a colleague leaves, a system fails or a customer pulls out without warning, you cannot stay calm and invent a new method at the same moment. A simple signal agreed in advance, and a first step everybody knows, takes the place of thinking in a panic. Answer now, while everything is fine and nobody is frightened, the question "if this happens, what do we do first?" and cut the answer down to one short sentence.$$
