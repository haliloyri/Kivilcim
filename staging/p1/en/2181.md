[[story]]
Cal Newport's *Slow Productivity* draws on stories of great thinkers who did not rush, and Isaac Newton's time at his family farm is one of the best known. In the spring of 1665 Newton, a student at Trinity College, Cambridge, had just earned his undergraduate degree. A few months later, as plague spread, he left Cambridge for Woolsthorpe Manor, the family farm in Lincolnshire, about sixty miles away.

He stayed away for roughly two years, until the plague faded in the spring of 1667. Historians note that he was probably not shut away the whole time, and that he seems to have been back in Cambridge at least once in 1666. Still, the farm was quiet, and nobody was setting him assignments.

Three lines of work are tied to those months. In mathematics he kept pushing at the problem of quantities that change, which he called fluxions and we now call calculus. By the end of 1666, according to published biographical accounts, he had effectively solved it. In optics he darkened his room, let a narrow beam of light through a hole in the shutter and passed it through prisms. The experiments led him to conclude that white light is made of all the colours of the spectrum.

The third line was gravity. Newton reasoned that the force pulling an apple to the ground might be the same force holding the Moon in its path. The popular story of an apple falling on his head, though, did not happen as it is usually told. More striking is the next detail. When he tried the calculation at the time, the numbers did not work. He returned to the idea and got it right years later, before the *Principia* appeared in 1687.

So the famous period was no burst of finished results. It was a few open questions, plenty of unbroken time, and a mind allowed to stay with one problem until it gave way. One of the three threads was still unresolved when he left the farm, and it took years more.

It is tempting to say the plague caused the discoveries. The evidence shows only that a young man with very little to do except think did some of the most lasting thinking of his life, and that the thinking was uneven, partial and slow to mature.

[[lessons]]
$$**Give a hard problem long, uninterrupted stretches and few competing demands, and judge it by years, not weeks.** Most of us cannot retreat to a farm, but we can protect one morning a week with no meetings, no inbox and a single question on the table. Notice that Newton's gravity calculation failed first and worked later; unfinished work may simply be work that has not yet had enough time, and it deserves another look before anyone calls it a failure, so keep it where you can see it. A problem that gets only twenty minutes between interruptions never receives the long look that Newton gave his.$$
