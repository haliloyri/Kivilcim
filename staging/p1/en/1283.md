[[open]]
Two hawks are fighting high above the desert. A young stranger stops to watch them, and in that moment he sees something else: an army, swords drawn, riding into the oasis behind him.

Nobody attacks an oasis. Everyone knows that. Now he has to decide whether to say anything.

[[story]]
The scene comes from Paulo Coelho's 1988 novel *The Alchemist*. Santiago, the shepherd who left Spain to chase a dream of treasure at the pyramids, is crossing the desert with a caravan. They reach the oasis of Al-Fayoum.

The desert around it is dangerous. Tribal wars are being fought across the sands. But oases have a special place in that world. By tradition, they are treated as neutral ground. Whatever the fighting outside, the oasis is left alone.

One day Santiago walks out into the desert. Overhead, two hawks are fighting in the sky. As he watches them, he suddenly has a vision: an army with drawn swords, riding into the oasis.

He is a stranger there, with no standing and no one to vouch for him. The tradition says oases are never attacked. All he has is what he saw while watching two birds.

##He decides to tell the tribal chieftains anyway.##

He goes to them and describes the vision. The chieftains have to weigh a stranger's warning against a rule everyone respects. They choose to believe him. They order the oasis armed.

But the belief comes with a condition. Santiago is warned that if the weapons are not used by sundown, one of them will be used on him. He has staked his own life on his reading of the hawks.

The next day, armed horsemen do ride into the oasis. They find it ready. The prepared defenders fight them off, and the attackers are defeated.

The stranger who spoke up is rewarded and offered a position as counselor of the oasis. The episode also leads him to someone who had been waiting for him all along: the alchemist, the man who gives the novel its title.

[[lessons]]
$$**Seeing is half; saying is the rest.** Santiago's vision would have changed nothing if he had kept it to himself. The part that mattered was walking into the chieftains' tent, knowing a weapon would be turned on him if no attack came. Noticing a sign is common. Acting on it, and accepting the risk of being wrong in front of others, is rarer. He had no rank to lean on, only the specific thing he had seen.$$

$$**Small details can outrank big rules.** The tradition said oases are never attacked, and everyone at Al-Fayoum relied on it. Two hawks told Santiago otherwise. An accepted rule tells you what usually happens. It doesn't tell you what is happening right now. Paying attention to a detail that doesn't fit is how you catch the moment the rule stops holding, before anyone else has noticed.$$

[[reflect]]
- Have you ever noticed a warning sign that everyone else seemed to overlook?
- When you did, did you speak up, or did "that never happens here" keep you quiet?
- Who is the person who could act on what you've noticed?
