[[story]]
Long before he wrote *Make Time* with John Zeratsky, Jake Knapp noticed in 2012 that his phone made him twitchy. The moment that mattered came while he was playing with his sons. His older son asked what he was looking at on the phone. Knapp got defensive first, then angry with himself and the phone, because he felt he was not really there with his children.

He started deleting apps by hand, beginning with the biggest drains on his attention. Twitter, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, ESPN and every game went. He switched Safari off in the settings. He left email for last, since he calls it the very worst distraction on the phone. He deleted Gmail, and to disable Apple's Mail app he removed his Google account from the phone as well.

What stayed was music, podcasts, maps, the camera and the flashlight. John Zeratsky had a name for apps that always have something new to show: infinity pools. Knapp's phone no longer had any. The first days made him nervous. Then the feeling gave way to something he describes as weirdly peaceful, a head that was "untethered."

It cost him something. He lost his reputation as a quick replier to email and drifted away from some friends on Facebook. In return, he says, his attention span grew longer and he was more present with his wife and children. He also made progress on long projects, including writing and publishing two books.

When he truly needs an app, he installs it again or uses a laptop or a tablet. A restaurant's web page can be read inside Maps, with no browser. For a task that needs the full browser, he switches Safari on, finishes it and switches it off again. Reinstalling an app takes him very little time, and he feels relief when he deletes it afterwards. Six years on, the setup was still in place. Today his email signature tells colleagues that he only checks email twice a day.

The logic is simple. Deleting an app takes away the need to tell yourself "I won't look" every time the urge arrives. A small obstacle takes its place: to look at something, you first have to reinstall it. Those few steps give the urge a moment to be weighed.

[[lessons]]
$$**Instead of forbidding what steals your attention, make reaching it take a few extra steps.** A freelance translator could take the chat app off her phone and open it only on her laptop, so each check becomes a deliberate choice and a client's message still gets answered at the desk. The thing is still within reach, but it now asks for some effort, and that effort makes you ask whether you really need it. Pick one app, take it off your home screen for a week, and note when your hand still goes looking for it.$$
