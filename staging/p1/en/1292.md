A room of MBA students is having drinks with the founder of McDonald's. Ray Kroc asks them a simple question: "What business am I in?" They laugh, because the answer is obvious. Hamburgers, of course. Kroc tells them they are wrong, and his answer changes how you look at every golden arch you have ever driven past.

[[story]]
This story reaches *Rich Dad Poor Dad* secondhand. Robert Kiyosaki heard it from a friend who was an MBA student at the University of Texas at Austin when Ray Kroc, the founder of McDonald's, came to speak.

After the talk, a group of students went out for drinks with Kroc. At some point he put a question to them: "What business am I in?"

The students laughed. It sounded like a joke, or a test with only one answer. They told him he was in the hamburger business. Everyone in the country knew what McDonald's sold.

Kroc said no. He was not in the hamburger business. ##His business was real estate.##

Kiyosaki explains the logic behind the answer. The franchise business, in his telling, was the tool used to buy the best locations and street corners. The burgers and fries brought in the franchisees and the customers. The land underneath was what the company kept. Over time, McDonald's became one of the largest owners of real estate in the world.

The students were not foolish for saying hamburgers. That is what the company sold, and selling it well mattered. But they answered the question of what McDonald's did every day, not the question of what made it wealthy. Those turned out to be two different things.

Kiyosaki uses the story to draw a line he returns to throughout the book. Your profession is what you do for a paycheck. Your business, in his sense, is the collection of assets you own. Most people treat the two as the same thing. His advice is to keep your job, but also to "mind your own business" by steadily building that asset column on the side.

A note on sources is worth adding here. Kiyosaki was not in the room himself; he is passing on his friend's account. And historically, the real-estate strategy at McDonald's is usually credited to Kroc's finance chief rather than to Kroc personally. The lesson Kiyosaki takes from it is his own interpretation.

Still, the question travels well. It works on a company, and it works on a person. Ask someone what they do and you usually get a job title. Ask what business they are in, in Kroc's sense, and the answer can be very different, or it can be silence.

[[lessons]]
$$**What you sell and what makes you rich can differ.** McDonald's sold hamburgers, yet in the story Kroc named real estate as his business. The product brought in customers and franchisees; the street corners were what built lasting value. Looking only at the product misses where the wealth sits.$$

$$**Your profession is not your business.** Kiyosaki's point is that a job pays you for your time, while your business is what you own. He does not tell readers to quit. He tells them to keep the paycheck and also build an asset column, to "mind your own business" alongside their work.$$

$$**The obvious answer deserves a second look.** A room of MBA students all said hamburgers and laughed at the question. The fact that an answer is obvious to everyone is a good reason to ask what it might be leaving out.$$
