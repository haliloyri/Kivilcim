A young shepherd lies down to sleep in an abandoned church, where a sycamore tree has grown up through the ruins. He dreams, again, of treasure hidden far away in Egypt. It will take him an entire journey to understand that dream.

[[story]]
Paulo Coelho's novel *The Alchemist* follows Santiago, a shepherd in Andalusia in southern Spain. He likes his simple life with his sheep. But one dream keeps returning. In it, a child takes him to the pyramids of Egypt and tells him that he will find a hidden treasure there.

Santiago is unsure what to do. Then he meets a mysterious old man who claims to be a king. The king encourages him to follow the dream. So Santiago does something frightening: he sells his sheep, gives up the only life he knows, and sets out.

The journey is long and hard. In Tangier, he is robbed on his first day and is left with nothing. He takes work with a crystal merchant and slowly earns enough to continue. He joins a caravan across the desert. He falls in love at an oasis. He meets an alchemist who teaches him to listen to his heart and to the world around him. He faces danger, doubt and many moments when it would be easier to turn back.

Finally, he reaches the pyramids. He begins to dig, sure that the treasure is there.

Instead, thieves attack him. They beat him and take what he has. As they leave, one of the thieves mocks him. Two years ago, the man says, he had a recurring dream of his own. In it, he was told to go to Spain, to a ruined church where shepherds slept, where a sycamore grew. Under that sycamore, the dream said, there was a treasure. But he was not foolish enough, he says, to cross a whole desert because of a dream.

##Santiago realizes the thief is describing the very church where his journey began.##

He returns to Spain, to the ruined church and the sycamore tree. He digs. And the treasure is there, where he had been sleeping all along.

It would be easy to conclude that the journey was pointless. Coelho suggests the opposite. Santiago could not have found the treasure without the journey. Only by going to the pyramids could he have heard the thief's words. And only by crossing the desert, working, loving and learning did he become the person who could understand them.

The contrast with the thief is part of the lesson too. The thief had the same kind of dream. He simply didn't follow it.

[[lessons]]
$$**Sometimes you must go far to see what's close.** Santiago's treasure was under the tree where he began. He couldn't have known that without leaving. Many people only see the value of home, or of something they already have, after a journey that shows it to them from a new angle.$$

$$**The journey changes the traveler.** The crystal merchant, the desert, the alchemist: each part of the trip taught Santiago something. He came back not just with treasure but as a different person. Even when a journey ends near where it started, what you learn along the way is part of what you find.$$

$$**Not following a dream has a cost too.** The thief had a similar dream and dismissed it. He never went, and he never found anything. Coelho's story suggests that the bigger risk isn't failing on the road, but never setting out at all.$$

[[reflect]]
- What did you learn on a journey, literal or not, that you couldn't have learned at home?
- Is there a "treasure" in your life you overlook because it's close?
- Which of your dreams have you dismissed, the way the thief dismissed his?

&&What journey, big or small, might help you see what's already near you?&&
