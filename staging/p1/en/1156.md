Picture two people who are equally smart and equally good with people. They have the same terrible day. One shakes it off by evening. The other is dragged down for a week. Shirzad Chamine believes the difference can be measured, and trained.

[[story]]
In *Positive Intelligence*, executive coach Shirzad Chamine starts from a familiar question: why do some people do so much better under pressure than others?

IQ explains part of it. Emotional intelligence, EQ, explains another part. But in his work with leaders, Chamine felt something was still missing. Some very intelligent, emotionally aware people still spent huge amounts of energy fighting their own minds.

He proposed a third measure. He called it **PQ**, the Positive Intelligence Quotient.

##PQ is simple to define: the percentage of time your mind is serving you rather than sabotaging you.##

When your Saboteurs are in control, like the Judge criticizing everything or the Controller trying to grip every detail, your mind is working against you. When you're calm, clear and curious, it's working for you. PQ is the ratio between the two.

Chamine describes a tipping point at a score of around 75. Above it, your mind tends to lift you up, and you handle challenges with more resilience. Below it, your mind tends to pull you down, and ordinary setbacks weigh far more than they should.

The alternative to Saboteur mode, he says, is a part of the mind he calls the **Sage**. It's the calmer, wiser perspective that sees challenges as something to work with. The Sage has five powers. It can **empathize**, with yourself and others. It can **explore**, with curiosity instead of judgment. It can **innovate**, finding new options. It can **navigate**, choosing a path based on deeper values. And it can **activate**, taking clear action without the anxiety and drama the Saboteurs bring.

The good news in the book is that PQ isn't fixed. Chamine treats it like a muscle. He describes three strategies for building it: weaken your Saboteurs by noticing and naming them, strengthen your Sage by practicing its powers, and strengthen the brain circuits behind the Sage.

That last one is surprisingly practical. Chamine calls the exercises **PQ reps**. A single rep takes about ten seconds. You simply bring your full attention to a physical sensation: your breath, the feel of your feet on the floor, the texture of the cup in your hand, the sounds in the room. Then you go back to what you were doing.

Ten seconds doesn't sound like much. The point is repetition. Done many times a day, those small moments of attention gradually make it easier to shift out of Saboteur mode when it matters.

[[lessons]]
$$**Measure how often your mind is on your side.** PQ isn't about how smart you are. It's the share of time your own thinking helps rather than hurts you. Chamine's tipping point of around 75 suggests that a modest shift in that percentage can change whether your mind feels like an ally or a burden.$$

$$**There's a calmer mind underneath.** The Sage, with its powers to empathize, explore, innovate, navigate and activate, isn't a new personality. It's a mode that's already available. The work is learning to reach it more often, especially when the Saboteurs are loudest.$$

$$**Tiny reps build the muscle.** Ten seconds of attention on your breath or your feet feels trivial. Chamine's point is that it works like physical exercise: many small repetitions, not one heroic effort. That makes it realistic for busy days, which are exactly when you need it most.$$

[[reflect]]
- Roughly what share of today did your mind spend helping you, and how much sabotaging you?
- Of the Sage's five powers, empathize, explore, innovate, navigate and activate, which do you use least?
- When is your mind most likely to slip into Saboteur mode during an ordinary day?
