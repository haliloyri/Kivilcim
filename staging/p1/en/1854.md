A man of fifty-eight walks out of a lodge in a wooded valley in southern New Mexico and sets off on a twelve-mile run. Nobody finds it odd, because he runs every day. That evening the lodge waits for him, and so do the days after it.

[[story]]
Micah True, known as Caballo Blanco, is one of the names tied to Christopher McDougall's *Born to Run*. In March 2012, aged fifty-eight, he left the Wilderness Lodge and Hot Springs, where he was staying in New Mexico's Gila Wilderness, on a Tuesday for a twelve-mile run. According to the lodge's co-owner it was a routine outing, since he ran daily. He went alone, and he did not come back.

The Gila Wilderness is heavily overgrown. State Police Lt. Robert McDonald said it is thick with vegetation and that going out there alone is risky. Four days passed between that Tuesday and the Saturday on which he was found. The report does not say how much of that time was spent in a search, only how it ended.

On Saturday evening friends found him. He was about a mile southeast of the Gila Cliff Dwellings, beside a cold stream, with his legs in the water and his water bottle next to him. They alerted search crews, and the crews recovered the body soon afterwards. On Sunday it was turned over to the Office of the Medical Investigator.

The cause of death had not been disclosed when the report was written. The lodge co-owner said he had no known health problems, that she did not think he had much with him, and that she suspected he might have been injured. That was a guess and not a finding, and it stays a guess here.

True's name belonged to a race. Since 2003 he had been organising one in Copper Canyon to draw attention to the Raramuri runners and to help get food to them. The book had been out for three years. The man who started the canyon race went out on an ordinary daily run in a New Mexico valley and for four days could not be reached.

Nobody has shown that anyone made a mistake, and an ordinary run still turned into a search. He ran daily, and this was a routine run. None of that removed the thick undergrowth or the risk of being alone, and how familiar a run feels says nothing about how long it will take or who will know where to look if something goes wrong.

[[lessons]]
$$**Leave your route and your expected return time with one person before you set out.** An office worker who walks alone on weekends, even on a path she knows well, can twist an ankle in a valley with no phone signal. If someone knows which trail she took and when she meant to be back, the search has a place to start. A short message makes the first hours of any search both faster and better aimed, and it takes a minute to send, which is a small price for a hiker who may never need it but cannot know that in advance.$$
