[[story]]
In 1997 Bill Campbell was at home by his pool in Palo Alto when Steve Jobs asked him to join Apple's board. As Campbell told Fortune in 2014, the words were: "I'd like you to join the Apple board." He answered "For sure." He called that kind of request one that "takes your breath away," and said he had felt the same rush only once before, when Columbia asked him to become a trustee.

The two had history. Campbell had watched Jobs from his own years at Apple, through the Macintosh division and then NeXT. Jobs later described him to Fortune with a farm image: Campbell had gone from being one of the prize stalks of corn on the farm to being the farmer. They took regular Sunday walks near their Palo Alto homes. Jobs said they were for "the things that have got me concerned and things I haven't yet figured out."

Then came a complication. Campbell was also advising Google's Eric Schmidt and attending Google board meetings, the only outsider to do so. Jobs objected. Campbell told Fortune that Jobs would yell at him about it, and his defence was simple: "I'm just coaching them on how to run their company better."

Neither man walked away. Campbell held both roles for years, and Jobs remained close to him through the rest of his life. When Fortune's Jennifer Reingold was preparing the 2008 profile of Campbell, who had worked hard to stop it, Jobs called her to say that bad things happen when people write stories that shouldn't be written. Weeks later, she reports, he phoned again to praise Campbell as if the first call had never happened.

The arrangement ended in 2010. After the Fortune profile made his role public, Campbell told the New York Times, it had become problematic as Apple and Google began competing, and he stepped back from Google. Campbell kept his seat on the Apple board until 2014, a stretch of seventeen years.

Look at how Campbell handled it. Jobs knew about the Google work, and Campbell described it in one plain sentence without dodging. Jobs could shout about the conflict while the friendship carried on, and for years the arrangement held because nothing about it was secret.

[[lessons]]
$$**Say plainly what the conflict is, and keep showing up around it.** Campbell's Google work bothered Jobs, and Campbell gave one honest account of what he was doing instead of hiding it or dropping the friendship. When two of your commitments pull against each other, state the overlap out loud to the person it affects, answer their objection in a sentence, and then keep the ordinary rituals of the relationship going. A conflict you have disclosed can be argued over and eventually settled, while one that is discovered later can only be explained away, which is a much worse place for any relationship to start from.$$
