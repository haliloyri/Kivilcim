[[open]]
A young shepherd in Andalusia has a life that works. He has his flock, the open road, and the freedom he chose over the priesthood. Then a dream arrives: a child, the Egyptian pyramids, a hidden treasure.

He could shrug it off. But the dream comes back a second time.

[[story]]
This is how Paulo Coelho's 1988 novel *The Alchemist* begins. Santiago is a shepherd in the south of Spain. His parents wanted him to become a priest. He chose instead to travel with his sheep, moving across the countryside with the flock.

The dream is simple and specific. A child leads him to the Egyptian pyramids and tells him that he will find a hidden treasure there. He has it once. Then he has the same dream again.

Santiago goes looking for an explanation. He asks a gypsy woman to interpret it. Her reading is direct: go to the pyramids. And she names her price in advance, a tenth of whatever treasure he finds.

Then an old man appears. At first he seems like a stranger. He reveals himself to be Melchizedek, the king of Salem. He tells Santiago about his "Personal Legend," the thing he has always wanted to accomplish. And he offers a promise that sits at the heart of the book: when you truly want something, the whole universe conspires to help you achieve it.

The king also asks for payment. He wants a tenth of Santiago's sheep. In return he gives the boy two stones, Urim and Thummim, to help him read omens along the way.

Now Santiago has to choose between the flock he knows and a dream about a place he has never seen.

##He sells his sheep.##

With the money, he crosses the sea from Spain to Tangier, in Africa. It is the first real step toward the pyramids, paid for with the whole life he had built.

And on his very first day in Tangier, he is robbed of all his money.

The flock is gone. The money from the flock is gone. He is in a foreign city, at the start of a journey he has barely begun, with nothing left of the trade he made. The dream that pulled him here has not yet delivered anything, and the first step has already gone wrong.

[[lessons]]
$$**A calling asks for something concrete.** Santiago didn't set off with his life intact and a dream added on top. He sold the flock he knew for the chance to follow a dream about distant pyramids. Following something you've always wanted usually means letting go of something comfortable and real, not just adding a new plan to your week. The dream came twice before he acted, and the price was the very thing that made his life secure.$$

$$**Commitment is not a shield.** Santiago did everything the story asks of him: he listened to the dream, sought advice, paid the king and made the leap. On his first day in Tangier he was robbed of every coin. Deciding to go after something doesn't protect you from setbacks, and a bad first step doesn't mean the decision was wrong. Santiago's story is still at its beginning when he loses everything.$$
