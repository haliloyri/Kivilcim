[[open]]
In a data centre in New Jersey, in a rack, a coil of fibre-optic cable about 61 kilometres long sits in a rack. It carries no extra traffic. Its only job is to make every message wait a moment before it reaches the machine that matches trades.

[[story]]
The wait is 350 microseconds, a little over a third of a thousandth of a second. It belongs to IEX, the exchange that Brad Katsuyama and Ronan Ryan founded in 2012 and that sits at the centre of Michael Lewis's *Flash Boys* (2014). Incoming orders pass through the coil before they reach the matching engine. According to the Globe and Mail, the pause gives the engine time to update prices and blocks tactics that depend on stale ones.

IEX opened for trading on October 25, 2013, as a dark pool, and it stayed small. On its first day it matched about 500,000 shares and routed just under a million more. Later it handled less than 2 percent of US equity trading. To become a full exchange it needed approval from the Securities and Exchange Commission, and the delay was exactly what its opponents attacked. Citadel Securities, the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq argued that a deliberate delay could produce stale prices and clash with Regulation NMS, the rule set that governs how markets must honour each other's quotes.

The argument came down to one word. Regulation NMS requires that automated quotes be accessible "immediately." Does a pause of 350 microseconds count as immediate, or is it an intentional delay that breaks the rule?

On June 17, 2016, the SEC approved IEX as a national securities exchange. On the same day it issued an interpretation of Rule 600(b)(3). In it the Commission read "immediate" to bar intentional delays beyond a de minimis amount, and SEC staff guidance said that delays shorter than one millisecond qualify as de minimis. Chair Mary Jo White said the day's actions "promote competition and innovation."

The SEC did not simply wave the idea through. It said other exchanges that wanted a delay of their own would be judged case by case, and it said its staff would study within two years how intentional access delays affect market quality. Katsuyama, then 38, said IEX was grateful and humbled by the support from the investor community.

Look at how the dispute ended. A word that had been argued over in the abstract acquired a number. Under that interpretation, 350 microseconds sits comfortably below the line at 1,000. Everyone can now check any new proposal by measuring it.

[[lessons]]
$$**When a rule says a vague word, ask for the number that goes with it.** A landlord who promises to fix problems "promptly" and a tenant who understands that as the same day will each feel wronged within a week. Naming the figure in the agreement, whether it is two days or a week, turns an argument about character into a check on a calendar. A number does not settle whether the rule is wise, but it lets both sides see when it has been kept, and that is usually where the fighting stops. It also gives both of you something to point at when the arrangement starts to slip.$$
