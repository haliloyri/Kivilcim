A young man in a delivery job rings the bell of a church rectory in New York, a bag of groceries in his hands. The woman who opens the door does not simply take the bag and close it again. She asks him a question. She wants to know whether he speaks French.

[[story]]
Deogratias Niyizonkiza, known as Deo, had fled the civil war in Burundi and reached New York City in the 1990s with almost nothing. According to a profile in Columbia Magazine, he first lived in an abandoned building in Harlem with other African immigrants, and he found work delivering groceries. The building was in such poor condition that he eventually left it, and for a time he slept in Central Park.

He was 22 when the grocery delivery took him to a church rectory. There he met Sharon McKenna, a former nun. She asked whether he spoke French. She gave him a large tip, and she hugged him.

That was the whole encounter, but it did not end there. McKenna learned about his situation and found Charlie and Nancy Wolfe, a couple who took him in. Deo came to call them his American parents. Less than two years after he had arrived in the country, he had learned English and was a student at Columbia University's School of General Studies.

Tracy Kidder later wrote about his life in the book *Strength in What Remains*, published in 2009. The clinic that Deo later founded in Burundi has a banner on its original building that reads "Sharon McKenna Health Center."

Look at what she actually did. She had no plan to rescue anyone. A man arrived at her door doing a job that most people barely register, and she asked him something about himself. The question was practical, whether he spoke French, but it carried a message: you are a person with a language and a history, not just a delivery. The tip and the hug said the same thing in other words.

What followed shows how far a single act of attention can travel. It was not the only thing that mattered, since the Wolfes took him in and Deo did the studying. But it was the first link in a chain, and the chain ended in a university and, later, a clinic whose first building carries her name. Nobody can say whether it would have happened without that doorway. The story only shows that the doorway was where it began, and that no grand gesture was needed: a question, a tip and a hug were enough.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask the person in front of you one real question about themselves, because being seen as a person can start a chain you cannot predict.** The courier, the cleaner, the new hire at the edge of the meeting table: each usually goes unnoticed. Asking a short, genuine question, with real curiosity about the answer, such as what languages someone speaks or where they come from, tells them they are more than their role. You may never learn what that moment leads to, but it costs you thirty seconds and can be remembered for years. Start with the people who already pass through your day, because they are the easiest to overlook and the easiest to reach.$$
