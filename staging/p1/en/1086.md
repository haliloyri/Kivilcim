[[open]]
Picture a quarter of a million people standing shoulder to shoulder in the summer heat. Nobody mailed them an invitation. No website listed the date. There was no reminder email and no RSVP. They simply turned up. If you've ever struggled to get six colleagues into a room for a Tuesday meeting, that should stop you. What pulls that many people to one place when nobody asked them to come?

[[story]]
In the summer of 1963, about 250,000 people gathered on the Mall in Washington, D.C., to hear Martin Luther King Jr. speak. Simon Sinek comes back to this scene in *Start With Why*, and he keeps pointing at a detail that's easy to skip past: no invitations went out. No website announced when to show up. People came anyway.

So why did they come? Sinek's answer is a little surprising. They didn't really come for King. They came for themselves, for what they already believed. King didn't hand them a new conviction. He put words to one they were already carrying.

Then Sinek makes the observation that gives the whole story its edge. King gave the "I Have a Dream" speech. He did not give the "I Have a Plan" speech.

That matters because plans aren't the enemy here. A movement needs plans, demands, steps and strategy. But a list of steps on its own rarely makes someone travel to a city and stand in a crowd. King talked about what he believed rather than reciting what needed to be done. The people who shared that belief took his cause and made it their own.

One piece of history is worth adding here. It isn't necessarily part of Sinek's telling, but it deepens it. Accounts of that day say the gospel singer Mahalia Jackson called out to King while he was speaking:

> "Tell them about the dream, Martin."

King moved away from his prepared text. ##He set the script aside and spoke about the dream.##

It's tempting to read that as pure improvisation, lightning out of nowhere. It wasn't quite that. King had used "dream" passages in earlier speeches. When he left the script, he reached for material he had already worked through and already said out loud. The moment felt spontaneous because solid preparation sat underneath it.

Sinek sees the same pattern outside politics. He points to organizations like Apple, which leads with a belief, "Think Different," before it ever gets to the products. The message isn't "here are our specs." It's "here's what we believe, and if you believe it too, you'll find your way to us." People come for the same reason the crowd came to the Mall: the belief is already theirs.

A quarter of a million people showed up without invitations. What gathered them wasn't a plan. It was a dream they recognized as their own.

[[lessons]]
$$**A plan tells people what; a belief tells them why.** Sinek's contrast of "I Have a Dream" with "I Have a Plan" doesn't dismiss planning. King's movement had demands and strategy. But the speech that moved people described what he believed, not a sequence of tasks. Steps coordinate people who have already decided to care. The reason is what makes them decide in the first place.$$

$$**People follow you for what they believe.** Those 250,000 people didn't show up because a famous man invited them. Nobody invited them at all. They came because King voiced something already true for them. So when you lead, the real test isn't how impressive you are. It's whether you're naming something your listeners already hold. And when you only list features, as Apple chose not to, people compare. When you state a belief, they choose a side.$$

$$**The inspired moment rests on preparation.** When King set aside his text at Mahalia Jackson's urging, he fell back on dream passages he'd already used at earlier events. The "why" didn't replace the work. It rode on top of it. If you want to speak from conviction when the notes fall away, you need to have practiced that conviction beforehand.$$

[[reflect]]
- Think about the last time you tried to persuade someone. How much of what you said was steps, and how much was the reason behind them?
- Name a cause or group you joined because it matched something you already believed. Who first put that belief into words for you?
- Suppose you had to talk about your current project with no slides and no notes. Do you have your own "dream passage" ready, something you've said before and truly mean?

&&In something you want others to act on, are you telling them your plan or your dream?&&
