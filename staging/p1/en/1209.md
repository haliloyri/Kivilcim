[[story]]
David Goggins begins *Can't Hurt Me* with the hardest parts of his past. He grew up with a violent father, in poverty, facing racism and struggling badly at school. By his own account, he left childhood full of fear, insecurity and excuses, and with every reason to believe nothing would change.

For a while, nothing did. In his twenties, Goggins was overweight, working night shifts as an exterminator, and in his words going nowhere. Then, one night, he watched a documentary about Navy SEAL training: men pushing through freezing water, exhaustion and pain, many of them quitting, a few enduring.

Something in him responded. He decided he wanted to become a SEAL.

He called recruiters. Most weren't interested. When one finally took him seriously, he gave Goggins the hard facts. To be eligible, he had to lose more than a hundred pounds, and he had less than three months to do it.

##It looked impossible. Goggins did it anyway.##

He describes the months that followed with brutal honesty: training for hours a day, eating very little, pushing through exhaustion and doubt. The pounds came off. He reached the weight limit and passed the tests.

But the physical transformation, he argues, depended on a mental one first. Central to it was a tool he calls the **Accountability Mirror**.

Goggins took sticky notes and wrote on them, and put them on his bathroom mirror where he'd see them every day. Some notes were goals: lose the weight, pass the test, become a SEAL. Others were harder to write. They named his insecurities and his excuses, the things he'd been hiding from. Every morning, he had to look at himself and at those notes and face the truth, without softening it.

The mirror wasn't about feeling bad. It was about ending the habit of lying to himself. Goggins argues that as long as he made excuses, blaming his father, his circumstances or his bad luck, he could never change. The moment he took full responsibility for what he did next, change became possible.

His past didn't disappear. But it stopped being the thing that decided his future.

[[lessons]]
$$**Honesty with yourself comes first.** The Accountability Mirror forced Goggins to face goals and insecurities every morning. Change, he argues, can't start while you're lying to yourself. Writing down the uncomfortable truth, somewhere you can't avoid it, turns vague dissatisfaction into something you can actually work on.$$

$$**A hard past doesn't have to decide the future.** Goggins's childhood was genuinely painful, and he doesn't pretend otherwise. But he came to believe that blaming it kept him stuck. Acknowledging the past while taking responsibility for the next step is what finally let him move.$$

$$**Discipline can make the impossible routine.** Losing over a hundred pounds in under three months sounded absurd. It happened through relentless daily effort, one hard day after another. Most big transformations look impossible from the outside and turn out to be built from ordinary days of refusing to quit.$$
