[[story]]
In June 2007 William Kamkwamba stood on a conference stage in Tanzania and gave the talk now known as "How I built a windmill." It was TEDGlobal, held in Arusha, and he was there as a fellow. The path that led him to that stage was surprisingly short and made of very ordinary links.

It began with a newspaper. In late 2006 a Malawian paper ran a story about his windmill, and local journalists had gone to see it. The story was spotted by Mike McKay, who wrote a blog called Hacktivate, and he passed it on. According to published accounts, McKay's post then caught the attention of TED's organisers, who tracked Kamkwamba down and invited him on a fellowship.

A newspaper item is usually forgotten by the next day. A blog post can be found for years. That difference is the hinge of this episode. The people who wrote about him had also seen the thing itself: the windmill was standing there, turning and working, not just described in a sentence. A story about an object you can point at is easier to retell than a story about a claim, and a blogger will share a tower more readily than a promise.

The newspaper, according to published accounts, also brought him help to return to school. In the years after the conference he went to the African Leadership Academy in Johannesburg, and then to Dartmouth College, graduating in the class of 2014. A project that started with a tower in a village had opened a road to school.

What is instructive is that no link in the chain was large, and none looks planned in advance. A local newspaper, a blog post, a conference fellowship. None of them alone could have changed a life. But each one left a trace that the next person could find.

Had William built the windmill and left it in his village, the work would still have been valuable. Leaving a trace others can find multiplies its effect. Being visible is not showing off but the way work reaches other people. Whoever writes, photographs and shares makes the next meeting possible, and you cannot know whom you will meet or when. You can only know that without a trace, it will not happen.

[[lessons]]
$$**Make the work you do visible somewhere other people can find it.** A project, a solution or an idea, however good, stays limited to one room for as long as nobody outside that room has any way of seeing it. A short article, a photograph or a local news item can start a chain when it reaches the right person at the right time. Luck knocking is not in your hands, but leaving an address where it can find you is your job, and the clearer the address, the likelier it is that the right person arrives, perhaps from a direction you never expected.$$
