Ask most people how much money they would need to feel financially secure, and you'll usually get a shrug, or a huge, vague number. Tony Robbins thinks that shrug is part of the problem. You can't reach a goal you've never defined.

[[story]]
In *Money: Master the Game*, Tony Robbins notices something about how people think about money. Almost everyone wants more of it. Very few have any idea how much they actually need. "More" is not a target. It's a feeling, and it never quite gets satisfied.

So Robbins asks readers to replace the vague wish with something specific. He describes **five financial dreams**, each one a step higher than the last.

The first is **financial security**. At this level, your investments produce enough income to cover your essential costs, like your home, food, utilities, basic transportation and insurance, without you having to work. You might still choose to work. But if you stopped, the basics would be covered.

The second is **financial vitality**. Here, your investment income covers the essentials plus roughly half of your current wants: some of the meals out, the clothes, the small luxuries.

The third is **financial independence**. Your investments sustain your current lifestyle entirely, without you working.

The fourth is **financial freedom**. You can live the life you'd like to have, including a few upgrades, without working.

The fifth is **absolute financial freedom**: you can do virtually anything you want, when you want.

Robbins then asks readers to do something most people never do. Calculate the number for each level. What do your essentials actually cost per year? How much would you need invested to produce that income?

##When people actually do the math, Robbins says, many discover that financial security is far closer than they imagined.##

It isn't a mysterious fortune. It's a number, and often a reachable one. Suddenly the goal stops being "get rich someday" and becomes a concrete plan: save this much, invest it this way, reach security by this year.

Robbins argues that this shift matters psychologically as well as financially. A vague dream can feel impossible, which makes it easy to give up on. A specific, achievable first step creates momentum. Once someone reaches or approaches the first level, the next one starts to seem possible too.

Not everyone needs absolute freedom. Many people find that security or independence would genuinely change their lives. The point is to know which dream you're chasing, and what it will take.

[[lessons]]
$$**Vague goals don't create action.** "I want more money" can't be planned for, measured or achieved. Robbins's point is that a number turns a feeling into a target. Once you define what you actually need, you can build a plan around it and track your progress toward it.$$

$$**Break the big dream into levels.** Security, vitality, independence, freedom and absolute freedom give you steps instead of one impossible leap. Each level is meaningful on its own. Reaching the first changes your sense of what's possible and makes the next one feel within reach.$$

$$**The first level may be closer than you think.** Many people assume financial security requires a fortune. When they add up their essential costs, they often find a far more reachable number. Doing the calculation can replace anxiety with a clear, motivating goal. Knowing your number also makes it easier to say no to spending that doesn't serve it.$$
