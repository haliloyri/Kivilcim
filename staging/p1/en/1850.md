Scott Jurek has won the Western States 100 seven times. In Mexico's Copper Canyon he lines up beside a 26-year-old goat herder whose sandals are cut from old tire rubber and tied to his feet. The trail is steep, the heat is brutal, the rocks are loose. One of them has the titles. The other has the canyon.

[[story]]
The 2006 race that Christopher McDougall's *Born to Run* made famous took place in Mexico's Copper Canyon. Micah True, better known as Caballo Blanco, had been organising a race there since 2003 to draw attention to the Raramuri (Tarahumara) runners and to help get food to them. In 2006 he wanted two men on the same starting line. One was Scott Jurek. The other was Arnulfo Quimare.

Jurek had won the Western States 100 seven times in a row and was one of the best-known names in ultrarunning. Arnulfo was a 26-year-old goat herder. On his feet he wore huaraches, handmade sandals cut from used tire rubber and wrapped around the foot. One man arrived with years of measured, planned training behind him. The other had no such programme. Both accepted the invitation and stood on the line, and with that True had put the famous champion and the canyon's own runner on the same rocky ground.

The course ran close to fifty miles through steep canyons carved by rivers, over stony trails, across river crossings, in blistering heat. According to National Geographic, Raramuri runners follow no formal training plan, do not time their runs, do not taper before a race and do not use recovery drinks. They still run very long distances, again and again. Children run barefoot on rocky trails, build calluses on their soles and rarely get hurt.

By the end, Jurek had lost to Arnulfo. Seven titles were not enough to get past a goat herder on that trail.

The logic of the result is plain, though easy to miss. Jurek's titles were real, but they were won on other courses, under other conditions. The Copper Canyon set its own terms with its heat, its stones and its river crossings, and a body raised on those terms had an edge that no later equipment and no later title could add. Nobody had to explain the course to him, and nobody had to sell him a new sandal. Arnulfo's sandal was no expensive invention. It was a piece of rubber cut from an old tyre, and the man wearing it was someone who knew how to run in that canyon and was ready for its conditions.

[[lessons]]
$$**Look for the expert where the work is actually done.** Say you are opening a second branch of a neighbourhood bakery. A consultant from big chains and the apprentice who has stood behind that counter for years know different things, and only the apprentice knows when customers arrive in the morning and what annoys them. A title says something worked elsewhere, and says very little about the counter and the street in front of you. If you learn to ask the consultant and the apprentice different questions, you can weigh each answer by what that person is actually able to know.$$
