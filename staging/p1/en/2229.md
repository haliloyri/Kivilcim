[[open]]
A minibus races along Oxford Road in Johannesburg, and the driver brakes only to check the intersections. In the back, a woman holds her baby son tight and whispers a plan to the older boy beside her: at the next red light, they jump. The boy is fast asleep.

[[story]]
Trevor Noah and his mother had been standing outside Rosebank Union, waiting for a minibus. Noah was so tired he was close to falling asleep on his feet. When no minibus came, his mother suggested hitchhiking, a car stopped, and the driver agreed to take them.

Almost at once, a minibus swerved in front of the car and cut it off. Two men climbed out, a Zulu driver and a companion, and the driver was carrying an iwisa, a traditional Zulu war club. They dragged the car's driver out and demanded to know why he was picking up their customers. Noah's mother told them to leave the man alone and said she and her children would ride with the minibus instead, so they transferred.

They were the only passengers. The minibus driver lectured her for travelling with a man who was not her husband, and when he heard her speaking Xhosa, he threw ethnic stereotypes at her. He grew more menacing and finally said she would learn a lesson that night. Then he sped up, slowing only at intersections.

Holding Noah's baby brother Andrew, his mother whispered that they would jump at the next traffic light. Noah had fallen asleep and never heard it.

At the light the driver slowed. She pulled open the sliding door, threw Noah out as far as she could, curled around Andrew and leaped after him. Noah hit the pavement hard, and the two of them tumbled across the road. She screamed at him to run, and they did. The men chased them briefly, then gave up. At a 24-hour petrol station, the family called the police, but the men were long gone. Noah's arms were torn and bleeding, and his mother was cut too, while Andrew was unharmed because she had wrapped herself around him.

The argument that followed is the strangest part. Noah, then nine, was furious that nobody had told him the plan, and said she had simply thrown him out of a car. She said she had told him to jump. He answered that being woken up would have been better. They went on to argue about whether God or the Devil had been involved, and in the end they were laughing, bloodied, at the petrol station. CBS News published this scene as an excerpt from his memoir.

What stands out is how fast his mother decided. The plan had a clear moment, the slowest point of the ride, and she used it. What it lacked was a listener: the person who most needed to know the plan was the one asleep.

[[lessons]]
$$**Name your exit point in advance, and make sure the person who has to move with you has heard it.** In a crisis even the best decision is only half finished if the person carrying it out never received it, and the cost shows up afterwards as anger. Noah's mother picked the right moment, the traffic light where the minibus would be slowest, but she carried the plan alone. Telling a team before a risky day "if this goes wrong, the first step is this" takes two minutes, and forgetting to say it costs far more in the moment itself.$$
