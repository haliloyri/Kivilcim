A young sailor wants to be home for Christmas. He filled in his leave request weeks ago and nobody has answered it. A captain who has just taken over the boat sets out to find the piece of paper, and the search shows him something about the ship that no inspection had.

[[story]]
L. David Marquet describes in *Turn the Ship Around!* how he spent his first period on USS Santa Fe: he asked questions out of curiosity instead of testing people on answers he already knew. Two questions came up again and again. What should he not change? And what got in the way of people doing their jobs?

The answers pointed at the process. People described delays, careless handling of evaluations and transfers, leave requests that went nowhere, and reports and records that nobody reviewed. One example stayed with him. A junior sailor had put in a request for Christmas leave many weeks earlier and had heard nothing. The procedure sent the request through seven people.

Marquet tracked the request down and sorted it out. His conclusion was that the system had failed, not the people.

Seven people on a path means seven chances for a piece of paper to sit in a tray. None of them had to be careless for the request to stall, and none of them could be pointed to as the reason it did. A sailor looking at that process would learn one thing: asking is pointless.

Other scenes pointed the same way. At a meeting of department heads, everyone arrived late, and the meeting began late because they were all waiting for the captain. Every day, the department heads reported to the executive officer before leaving the boat, so the executive officer in effect owned their tasks. Marquet wanted them to report their own accomplishments and plans instead.

He also saw how the ship treated good ideas. The weapons officer, Dave Adams, wanted to improve his department, and found that his superiors and the chiefs resisted his proposals. For Marquet this was the limit of a leader-follower ship: ideas had to climb a ladder, and every rung could say no.

To turn it around he started with the chiefs, the senior enlisted people who supervised the day-to-day work, and tied their daily tasks to a larger sense of purpose rather than to checklists and inspections alone.

He did not replace anyone among the 135 crew members. In his reading, people who still wanted to do well were reacting to routines that made no sense, and their frustration was a sign of that wish. He chose a leader-leader approach instead. According to a published summary, Dave Adams himself took command of Santa Fe twelve years after Marquet's tenure.

[[lessons]]
$$**When a request waits for weeks, count the signatures before you blame the person.** A new colleague's laptop that takes three weeks to arrive looks like carelessness until you list everyone who has to approve it, and then it looks like a design. Once you see the number of hands in the path, you can ask the useful question: which of these steps protects something, and which exists only because it was always there? That question lets you remove a step and keep the trust in the people. It also shows the person who waited that you looked at the road they travelled rather than at them.$$
