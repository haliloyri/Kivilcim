[[open]]
A couple driving a mountain road at night reach home two hours late, and they cannot say why. Two years later one of them, under hypnosis, draws a star map. Does the map really show a star system?

[[story]]
Carl Sagan discusses the case of Betty and Barney Hill in *The Demon-Haunted World*. In September 1961 the couple were driving at night along a road in the White Mountains, and arrived home two hours later than expected. Betty was a social worker and Barney worked for the Post Office. They could not account for those two hours.

Their unease grew. Using hypnosis, the couple tried to reach what had happened in the missing time, and what they described became one of the first modern accounts of alien abduction. About two years later, under hypnosis, Betty drew a star map showing what she said she had seen during the abduction.

The story of the map is where it gets interesting. An amateur astronomer named Marjorie Fish tried fitting the drawing to real stars and claimed it matched a system called Zeta Reticuli. This was presented as the strongest evidence in favour of the case.

Sagan and Soter said the match was weak. In a match of this kind, you can choose from thousands of stars the ones that fit the drawing and leave out those that do not. In other words, someone selecting the right pattern is likely to find whatever pattern they are looking for.

Nobody in this story is accused of lying. The fear Betty and Barney felt, and what they remembered, was a real experience. The question is what that experience was made of. Hypnosis is a method open to shaping memories: what is told under hypnosis seems true because it is vivid and detailed, but vividness is not proof of accuracy.

That is the quiet point of the story, and it needs no villain. A drawing made in good faith, a match found in good faith, and a memory held in good faith can all stack up into something that feels like evidence, while none of the pieces has ever been checked against anything outside itself.

[[lessons]]
$$**A vivid, detailed memory does not show what it rests on; ask first how the memory was brought out.** A scene a customer describes down to the last detail, "In that meeting you said this," or a discussion you remember yourself, may be as shaped by questions and expectations as it is true. Detail gives confidence but does not verify. If an important decision rests on a memory, go to the record, the email, or the account of a third person who was actually there.$$
