A famous visitor spends a whole day inside a submarine, mostly watching. Again and again, officers walk up to the captain and begin with the same short phrase, then give their reasons. The captain answers with just two words and goes back to what he was doing.

[[story]]
Stephen Covey, the author of *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*, rode USS Santa Fe about a year after its captain, L. David Marquet, had vowed never to give an order again. Marquet tells the story in *Turn the Ship Around!*, and it is the answer to his own vow. He had handed a copy of Covey's book to every officer and chief, and the crew had asked themselves what it would sound like if everyone acted proactively.

Throughout the day, officers came to Marquet with statements that began "I intend to…". One of them said: "Captain, I intend to submerge the ship. We are in water we own, water depth has been checked and is 400 feet…" Marquet's answer was two words: "Very well."

Marquet describes the old way too. An officer of the deck would ask permission to submerge, and the captain would give the order. In his account of the new way, in a podcast interview, the statement has three parts. It says what the officer will do, why that is technically correct, with the hatches shut, the water depth checked and the people below, and why it serves the mission.

Notice what moved. The captain used to hold the decision and the checking. Now the officer had to do the checking before he opened his mouth, because the sentence could not be said without it. The excerpt lists the phrases that hand control upward: "Request permission to…", "I would like to…", "What should I do about…". It lists the ones that keep it: "I intend to…", "I plan on…", "I will…".

A review of the book in the Submarine Review records what Covey made of it. After a short-notice surfacing of the boat, during which Marquet issued no orders and sailors simply stated their intentions and reasons before carrying on, Covey called Santa Fe the most empowered organization he had ever seen.

Marquet called the habit a small change in language that moved ownership of plans to the crew. He also admits the price in his podcast: "I'm gonna build leaders, I'm gonna give up a little bit of production." He chose to spend some speed to get people who could think.

[[lessons]]
$$**A sentence that has to carry its own reasons makes the speaker do the thinking first.** Compare a request to order new laptops with the announcement "I plan to order three laptops, the budget line has room, and it ends the delay on the Friday delivery". The second version cannot be said without checking the budget and the date, so by the time you hear it, the hard part is done and your answer can be as short as the captain's. Ask your team for that form on one kind of decision for a while, and watch who starts bringing you reasons instead of questions.$$
