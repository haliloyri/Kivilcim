Today millions queue for hours in front of a Van Gogh painting. For most of his life, Vincent van Gogh was a man who could not work out what to do and kept leaving the jobs he took.

[[story]]
In *Range* (2019), David Epstein tells the late and winding path of Van Gogh. Vincent first became an art dealer and was dismissed. Then he tried teaching and could not hold on. He worked in a bookshop and did not stay there either. When he tried to become a preacher, he went so far that even the church found him excessive.

At twenty-seven he began painting seriously. He had no academic training and no share in the myth of inborn talent. His first drawings were crude and out of proportion. But he worked without stopping, copied different masters and drew the same landscape again and again.

##For Epstein, Van Gogh's strength lay in the years that look wasted.##

Dealing in art had taught him how paintings are seen and sold. Preaching taught him to touch the pain of ordinary people. His passion for literature taught him to put story and feeling into a picture. Each failed job had stored something for the next step.

The letters to his brother Theo show this searching plainly. For years he asks what will become of him, and in every job he tries he hits a wall. After he took up painting, he did not settle on a single technique either. He blended schools and kept changing his colours and brushstrokes. The paintings of roughly the last ten years before his death are among the most loved in the world today.

Each job ended against a wall, yet each one made the way he looked at the world a little richer. Failing at one profession did not dull the next; it became an experience that fed it. He never stepped into the safe mould where a painter who specialised early can sit comfortably. Perhaps that is why his style looks like no one else's, and why finding his road late did not leave him behind.

[[lessons]]
$$**Do not throw out the attempts you count as failures.** Someone who has left three different jobs may have carried a skill out of each: talking to customers from one, a software program from another, steady work habits from a third. Put together, they form a store of experience that people on a single track do not have. Finding your road late does not mean that what you gathered along the way was wasted.$$
