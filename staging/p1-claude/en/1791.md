Two rats get the same electric shocks at the same strength and the same frequency. Only one of them hears a warning tone before each shock.

[[story]]
In *Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers* (1994), Robert Sapolsky describes an experiment on stress. Two rats receive the same electric shocks at the same intensity and frequency. There is just one difference: one rat hears a warning signal before each shock, and the other gets no sign at all.

The result is striking. The rat that got the warning develops markedly fewer ulcers. The strength and frequency of the shocks are the same for both, so the difference lies in neither the size nor the number of the shocks. One rat simply knows when the shock is coming.

##The rat that could predict the shock developed far fewer ulcers than the one that could not.##

The warning signal gives the animal a safe period between shocks. As long as the signal does not come, it can relax. The rat with no signal has no such refuge, because it cannot know when the next shock will arrive. Prediction does not remove the load, but it narrows the time in which the load passes through the body.

From this experiment Sapolsky lists the factors that shape how heavy a load feels. Predictability comes first. Then come a sense of control, an outlet for frustration, social support, and a sense that things are not getting worse.

The same load becomes lighter when one of these is added, because the body finds something to lean on. Give a rat something to run on or gnaw, and the same shock is easier to bear. For people, being able to choose the timing of a task can make the same task more bearable, since when it starts and ends is in their hands.

So stress is not measured by the size of the load alone. Knowing when the same load will arrive, how you will meet it and who is beside you changes the price the body pays.

[[lessons]]
$$**Reducing uncertainty lightens the load.** Someone waiting days for a result relaxes a little the moment he learns the day it will be announced, because the end of the wait now has a date. The work itself is unchanged, but he no longer has to stay on guard for something that might come at any moment. In your own tasks, adding a date, a sequence or a warning lowers the tension without shrinking the task.$$
