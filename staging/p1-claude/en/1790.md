[[story]]
In *Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers* (1994), Robert Sapolsky describes spending summers over many years in East Africa watching wild baboons. Baboons spend most of the day foraging and resting, so the day is mostly calm. Most of their daily tension comes from other baboons, not from lions.

For a long time Sapolsky watched who pressed whom and measured stress hormones in the same animals. In many cases males low in the hierarchy had higher levels, even with no lion around. That is an understandable picture in a baboon troop: the animal on the lower rung faces those above it every day.

##But rank alone was not decisive.##

Position told only part of the story. It also mattered how stable the hierarchy was, whether the animal could tell a real threat from a harmless event, what outlets it had for releasing tension, and what social ties it had. Some baboons redirected tension onto others lower than themselves and ended up with lower hormone levels. Others built closeness through grooming, picking through each other's fur.

What Sapolsky saw was that two baboons in the same position could carry very different loads. Both stand on the same rung, but one may be far more worn down than the other. One has an outlet for its tension and a neighbour it grooms and trusts. The other carries each day's pressure with nowhere to put it, and its hormone levels show the difference.

None of this means rank does not matter. In many cases a lower rank means more stress. But how an animal lives its position says more than the position itself. A stable order, the ability to tell threats apart and ways to release tension let the same load be carried lighter. Sapolsky's measurements show that difference directly in the animals' hormone levels.

[[lessons]]
$$**Look at how you live your position, not only at the position.** Of two people at the same level at work, one has a colleague to talk things through with and the other does not. The load is the same, but the first person's shoulders are lighter by evening. Finding someone with whom you can share tension is one of the easiest ways to carry your work without making it smaller.$$
