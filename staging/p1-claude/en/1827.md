[[open]]
Not a single genius wrote the jokes of one of television's most famous comedies. Dozens of writers pass ideas around a table, and nobody insists on whose idea it was.

[[story]]
In *Give and Take* (2013), Adam Grant argues that creative teams shine not through individual geniuses but through a generous culture. His example is the writers' room of The Simpsons. Dozens of writers produce jokes around a table for hours.

One writer throws out a gag, another builds on it, a third ties it to a better ending. The joke that comes out in the end belongs to nobody and to everybody. In the credits, it is not one writer's name that appears but the team's.

##Nobody insists, "that one was mine."##

In this room, someone who insists on "my joke" breaks the flow. Generous writers care about dropping their own idea to make someone else's better. When an idea lands on the table, what is discussed is not who threw it but how good it is.

Nobody tries to win alone. Everyone tries to make the show win. This attitude keeps ideas from staying within one person's limits. Each writer brings his own view, and everyone at the table moves it forward.

Grant's emphasis is this: not caring about individual credit frees the total intelligence of the team. When everyone shares an idea instead of guarding it, ideas grow faster and higher. Generosity here is not a sacrifice but a condition of creativity.

Sharing credit has a quiet price as well. Nobody's name is cited alone and nobody collects applause alone. But everyone around the table knows they are part of the work done. That knowledge lets the team walk into the next idea more easily.

In the writers' room, an idea's worth is measured not by who said it but by what it becomes when it joins the other ideas on the table. For a long-running show to produce a new episode every week, ideas have to flow without sticking to anyone.

[[lessons]]
$$**Let go of whose idea it is and look at how good it is.** If three employees renewing a neighbourhood cafe's menu argue, "I suggested this", the menu never gets finished. When they let one person's idea be developed by another, a menu comes out that none of them could have found alone. A team that shares credit generally produces better ideas.$$
