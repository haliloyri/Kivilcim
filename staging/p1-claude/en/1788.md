A few minutes after the lion has gone, the zebra is grazing again. Its heart rate is back to normal and the alarm is off. For a human, switching off that same alarm is much harder.

[[story]]
Robert Sapolsky begins *Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers* (1994) with a zebra that spots a lion. The body switches to an emergency plan at once: the heart speeds up, the muscles get energy, digestion pauses. The danger is real and the body prepares for it exactly when it should.

If the zebra escapes, it calms down within minutes and goes back to grazing. When the lion leaves, the alarm ends.

A human body has the same emergency plan. But what sets it off in a person is rarely a lion. Thinking about the rent, an exam or the distant future can start the same response. These thoughts can last for days or even months. The body prepares in the same way, with no way of telling a worry from a lion.

##For the zebra, stress is brief and physical. For humans it is often psychological and social, which makes it last longer.##

That is where the trouble begins. The body prepares to run without running. The heart speeds up, digestion slows, and there is no lion to flee. According to Sapolsky, if this preparation goes on, the body pays a price over time. The book's title rests on that explanation: the zebra can finish its stress, while a person may not be able to.

The zebra does not pay the price because when the danger ends its heart slows, its digestion restarts and it goes back to grazing. A human, even after the danger has passed, can keep the alarm on as long as the thought stays. The difference is not in the lion but in how the alarm is switched off.

That view changes where the problem lies. If the danger is real, the alarm has done its job. If it is only a thought, the same preparation drains the body's resources without protecting against anything. The question is no longer how to endure it, but what is ringing the alarm.

[[lessons]]
$$**Check whether a lion or a thought is ringing the alarm.** Someone who still cannot sleep at midnight because of tomorrow's presentation can ask himself whether the thing his body is preparing for is a real danger. If it is only a thought, there is nothing left to do tonight and he can switch the alarm off on purpose. That does not mean ignoring the worry. It means choosing to answer it in the morning, not at midnight.$$
