[[open]]
Being a giver looks admirable, but giving in the wrong way leaves a person empty. The most successful givers learn something that the burned-out ones never do.

[[story]]
In *Give and Take* (2013), Adam Grant says giving looks beautiful, but the wrong kind of giving wears a person out. The giver who sacrifices himself completely, cannot say no and runs at once to everyone's every request ends up exhausted. No energy is left to give, and good intentions become a load dragging him to the bottom.

How do the most successful givers do it? They are generous too, but not naive.

##Self-protective givers give in batches, not in scattered bits.##

For example, they deliberately set aside a certain part of the week for others and keep the rest for their own work. They spot takers and do not allow themselves to be exploited all the time. When they see that the person in front of them only wants to take, they narrow their help and spend their energy on those who truly need it. When necessary, they learn to say no.

This is not giving up generosity. On the contrary, it makes generosity sustainable. A person who helps constantly and without a plan has to stop at some point. A person who spreads help over set times can go on for years.

The distinction Grant describes is simple. The giver at the bottom gives to everyone at every moment and neglects his own work. The self-protective giver chooses when, to whom and how to give. Both have the same good intentions, but their results are very different.

Looking after yourself is therefore not selfishness, because an exhausted giver can eventually help nobody. It is the condition for helping more people in the long run. A giver who protects his energy can offer the same help for longer and to more people.

Giving in batches turns help into a plan: you know in advance how much time you will give, and stopping when it is up creates no guilt. Spotting takers is part of the same plan, because unlimited giving only drains the giver's resources.

[[lessons]]
$$**Gather your help into set times.** A student who helps friends at every moment while studying loses his own study time. If he makes two evenings a week "help evenings" and keeps the rest for himself, he supports his friends and also prepares for his own exam. That way his generosity becomes sustainable, and he goes on without disappointing anyone.$$
