A genius who put his own interests first was left with fewer and fewer people willing to work beside him. How does a gifted man end up alone?

[[story]]
In *Give and Take* (2013), Adam Grant uses Frank Lloyd Wright as an example of a taker's long-term cost. Wright's genius was beyond dispute. But according to Grant, he tended to use the people around him for his own advantage.

He asked the young architects who came to work with him to pay for the privilege of learning. Instead of design work, he often had them labour in the fields or do household chores. He ignored their contributions and kept the reputation and the credit for himself.

In the short run this gave him power, control and cheap labour. In the long run the price was heavy.

##When talented apprentices saw they were not valued, they left him.##

In Grant's telling, in the years when Wright should have been most productive, he struggled to find work and to keep the talent he had. His genius stayed where it was, but the people who could turn his ideas into reality had left him.

Taking often pays in the short run. Credit is collected, cost is pushed onto others and a person stands out. But in the long run bridges burn. People do not forget a taker, and when they get the chance they quietly withdraw their support.

Grant tells this story as a warning. Wright's mistake was not lack of talent but not seeing the contribution of those around him, and in time it cost him dearly. To admit that you cannot do a job alone also means seeing the people who carry the job. His genius was enough to design the building. But without the people to hold up what he had built, it was hard to keep the work going.

The taker's trap is to see gains that can be measured in the short run and not the losses of the long run. Cheap labour, all the credit gathered in one person and every decision made by one hand bring immediate benefit. But each of those benefits is won by using up a separate relationship.

[[lessons]]
$$**See the people who carry your success.** A manager who presents a report prepared by his team under his own name collects praise in the short run. When the team members notice, they put in less effort on the next project or go elsewhere. Naming the person who contributed costs you nothing, and protects the trust that will carry you in the long run.$$
