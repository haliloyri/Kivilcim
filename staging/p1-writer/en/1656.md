A domain cannot change without the consent of the field responsible for it. That sentence, from Csikszentmihalyi, moves creativity out of one person's head and into a room of judges.

[[story]]
In *Creativity* (1996), Csikszentmihalyi explains creativity as a system of three parts. The individual uses the domain's symbols to produce something new. The domain is the body of symbolic rules and procedures, such as mathematics or music, that the individual works with. The field is the group of experts who decide whether the new idea enters the domain, the gatekeepers. All three must be present for a creative product to appear.

He defines a creative person as someone whose thoughts or actions change a domain or establish a new one. A domain, he adds, changes only with the explicit or implicit consent of the field. When the field finds a product valuable, it joins the domain, and only then is its maker counted as creative. The model draws on his 91 in-depth interviews.

In a lecture he gives Einstein as the example: the ideas were certified as creative by a small number of leading physics professors. In the model's terms, Einstein was the individual, physics was the domain, and those few professors formed the field.

Place matters as well. In his account, where you live decides your access to information about the domain, how much novelty stimulates you, and how close you stand to the field's centres of power. The field is easy to picture in modern art, in a city like Manhattan: the people there who decide which new paintings or sculptures deserve to be seen, bought and collected, and so added to the domain.

In this model, making something new is only half of the story. The maker also has to learn the domain's symbols, since they cannot change what they cannot use, and then put the work in front of the field.

[[lessons]]
$$**Find out who will judge your work before you polish it.** Someone writing a funding proposal who asks the person signing it what they check first can skip hours of polishing the wrong parts. Knowing the rules of your field is not enough; you also need to know what its judges look at. Showing a draft early to one expert tells you how far you still have to go.$$
