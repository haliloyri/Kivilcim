[[story]]
Gavin de Becker opens *The Gift of Fear* (1997) with Kelly. She is carrying a bag of groceries up to her apartment when it tears, and the cans of cat food roll down the stairs. A man on the flight below picks them up and offers to carry them the rest of the way.

Kelly feels uneasy from the moment she hears his voice. She tries to say no. The man keeps offering, and he keeps using the word we, as if the two of them were in this together. Her refusal gets no purchase against it.

She does not act on the feeling, and the man comes up the stairs with her cans.

##He ends up inside her apartment, where he attacks her, and she barely manages to get away.##

The detail that matters for this story is the order. The unease came first, in the first seconds, before the offer and before the insistence. Kelly had the information early, and the polite exchange that followed pushed it aside. On the stairs she had no reason she could name, only the sound of his voice, while he stood there holding her cat food and offering to help. Turning down a man who seemed to be doing her a kindness felt rude, and the fear of being rude won.

Nothing in the scene looks like a warning until you know how it ends. There are cans of cat food on the steps and a stranger being helpful, and the only signal is the unease Kelly felt at the sound of his voice.

De Becker's argument in the book is that such a feeling deserves attention even when you cannot defend it. It is a message, and you do not need a witness or a proof before you listen to it.

[[lessons]]
$$**Treat an unexplained unease as information.** A woman at a train station feels a strange pull to wait for the next carriage and tells herself it would be silly to leave a perfectly good seat. If she acts on the feeling without explaining it to anyone, she loses a few minutes and learns what her own judgment was noticing. You can allow yourself to step back, decline or ask a second person to be there before you have an argument ready. The feeling does not need to win a debate to be worth a few minutes of your day.$$
