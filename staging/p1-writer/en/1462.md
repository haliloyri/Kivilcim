[[story]]
Joshua Foer went to the 2005 U.S. Memory Championship as a journalist. His book *Moonwalking with Einstein* (2011) tells what happened next. At the contest he met Ed Cooke, an English memory grandmaster who was 24.

The two got along. After the competition, hours of talk and several rounds of beer in a local bar, Cooke offered to coach him. Reviews describe Foer's own memory as ordinary, and Foer took the offer anyway.

About a year of training followed. ##In 2006 Foer won the USA Memory Championship and set a U.S. record in speed cards: one deck in 1 minute 40 seconds.## The man who had arrived as a reporter left holding the title, and a full deck of 52 cards memorized in under two minutes.

The result needs to be kept in scale. At the World Memory Championships in London he placed 13th overall, with a bronze in names and faces. He did not become the world's best. He became good enough to win at home, from a standing start, in about a year.

The book uses that year as its argument: memory is a trainable skill, and a journalist with an ordinary memory is the evidence. One person's year is not a controlled trial, and Foer was coached by a grandmaster, which is not an ordinary start. The book also checks the champions against the savants. It examines Daniel Tammet, and Foer comes to suspect that Tammet has more in common with the contest competitors than with the "Rain Man" type of savant. The book debuted at number three on the New York Times bestseller list and stayed eight weeks. But the sequence stands. A reporter came to watch, found a teacher and a method, and walked into the room a different competitor.

[[lessons]]
$$**Before writing a skill off, put a coach and a year of practice next to it.** A shop assistant who says she is hopeless with customers' names has probably never tried a method with someone who is good at it. She has only tried to remember harder. Finding one colleague who recalls names and asking how she does it turns "hopeless" into a question about a technique, and a single afternoon with her will show whether there is anything to practise. You get to learn whether the weakness was a limit or just an untrained habit.$$
