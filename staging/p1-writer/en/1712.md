Socrates names a penalty for good people who refuse to rule: being ruled by someone worse. In Plato's Republic, that fear is the only thing that makes them take power at all. Thrasymachus has just claimed the opposite, and Socrates answers him.

[[story]]
In the first book of *The Republic*, Thrasymachus argues that justice is nothing more than whatever benefits the stronger. Socrates puts a very different measure against it, and asks whose job ruling ought to be.

In his account, good people do not want to rule for money or honour, and being paid to rule would feel shameful to them. So why would they do it? The penalty for refusing is being ruled by someone worse, and a good person takes up the task to avoid that.

Socrates compares the ruler to a doctor and a shepherd. A real doctor aims at the good of the patient, a shepherd at the good of the flock. A ruler should aim at the good of those who are ruled and not at his own profit. By that measure, a man who looks for the advantage of the stronger is not a good ruler.

In the seventh book the same idea joins the allegory of the cave. Those who have seen the truth outside the cave are required to go back down and share in ruling, instead of staying up there to think. It costs them something, because they leave behind a life they value more.

Down below are people who take the shadows for real, and living among them and deciding for them is less attractive than staying above. Socrates draws a sharp conclusion: the city whose rulers are least eager to rule will be governed best, and the city whose rulers are most eager will be governed worst.

His reasoning is that people who love power for its own sake will fight over it, while those who have another life they value will treat government as a duty. The person who looks readiest to rule may therefore be the one least fit to.

[[lessons]]
$$**When someone seeks authority, look at whether they want the post or the work.** Residents looking for a volunteer to run their building could listen first to the problem the candidate wants to solve. A person who can describe that problem in detail may be treating the position as a tool. That makes it easier, when you vote, to tell who is chasing the title. You can ask yourself the same question when you think about standing for something yourself, which is the harder test.$$
