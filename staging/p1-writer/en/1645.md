[[story]]
In *Braving the Wilderness* (2017), subtitled *The Quest for True Belonging and the Courage to Stand Alone*, Brené Brown separates two things that feel alike from the inside. Fitting in means conforming to other people's expectations, for example nodding along in a meeting to avoid a quarrel. True belonging, in her account, does not require us to change who we are. It requires us to be who we are.

The wilderness of the title is her image for unknown territory outside your comfort zone, and the subtitle names the price of going there: the courage to stand alone.

The test she gives is practical. True belonging means feeling secure enough to say and live out your values whatever others think. Fitting in asks you to swap your values for the room's. Belonging, according to one summary of the book, is mostly about learning to belong to ourselves first.

Brown's own start was small. She began writing herself permission slips, and the first said she was allowed to be goofy, have fun and enjoy life. A slip is a modest tool, a sentence on a piece of paper, and it points at the habit she was trying to loosen: asking the room before asking herself.

##The book names three obstacles to belonging: a lack of shared humanity, a lack of compassion and fear of becoming an outsider.## Grouping people by ideology, race or politics fuels division, and treating outsiders as less than human makes us forget who they are.

She then lists strategies. Resolve conflict with curiosity and healthy boundaries instead of defensiveness. Question false us-versus-them splits. Remember what unites us by being present in joyful and painful moments. Build trust in yourself and in others.

[[lessons]]
$$**Check whether you are agreeing or belonging.** A team member who nods in the meeting and complains in the corridor is fitting in, because the corridor holds what she actually thinks. If she says one honest sentence in the room, she risks a disagreement, and she also finds out who can hear her. Noticing which of the two you are doing lets you choose where to speak.$$
