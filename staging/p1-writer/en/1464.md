[[story]]
In 1928 a Russian journalist known as S. came to the office of the psychologist A. R. Luria and said he had an unusually powerful memory. Luria tested the claim. He went on studying S. for about thirty years.

Joshua Foer's *Moonwalking with Einstein* (2011) carries the subtitle *The Art and Science of Remembering Everything*, and S. is the person in its pages who actually did. He could memorize passages in a language unknown to him and abstruse formulas he could not follow. He kept them for years.

##The price was a mind that could not clear itself.## Reviews of the book describe it as cluttered with trivial details and sensations. S. struggled to abstract and to generalize. Practical life and steady work gave him trouble.

According to one summary, Foer connects S. with a character in fiction. Borges wrote "Funes the Memorious," the story of a man cursed with perfect recall, and a quotation attributed to Foer on a quotation site ties being human more to forgetting than to remembering. One summary says that Luria himself wrote about what he called the art of forgetting.

There is a catch to the legend of S. A magazine piece on his case says his memory was less than perfect and only loosely photographic. He also used techniques similar to the method of loci, which is what Foer trained with in his own year. The difference shows up elsewhere in the case: S. could not edit.

Set beside Foer's own year, the case marks a limit. Foer, whom reviews describe as starting with an ordinary memory, trained to hold lists on purpose, with a method he could bring out for a contest. S. could not easily let the details go. The subtitle promises remembering everything, and S. is the page where that promise is tested.

[[lessons]]
$$**Decide what is worth keeping before you try to remember it.** A project manager who saves every message in the hope of recalling the right one later ends up searching through hundreds of them. If she picks the three decisions that matter and who owns each, she can say them from memory and let the rest go, and she will find that the three are easier to act on than the hundred. The choice about what to drop is part of remembering, and you get to make it on purpose, on a calm Friday, rather than leaving it to a cluttered inbox.$$
