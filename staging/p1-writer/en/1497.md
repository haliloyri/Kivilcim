[[open]]
Gallup asked employees a single question and set the answers beside the results of their business units. The question described no task at all, only what a person does best. The numbers painted a surprising picture of what goes on in a workplace.

[[story]]
The question was: at work, do you have the opportunity to do what you do best every day? Gallup put it to 198,000 employees in 7,939 business units across 36 companies, in a meta-analysis, and compared the answers with how the units performed.

Employees who strongly agreed were 50 percent more likely to work in business units with lower turnover, 38 percent more likely to work in more productive units, and 44 percent more likely to work in units with higher customer satisfaction scores. Those are associations, not proven causes. Units that increased their number of strongly agreeing employees saw comparable gains in productivity, customer loyalty and retention.

Then Gallup asked the same question across a broader database of more than 1.7 million employees in 101 companies in 63 countries. ##Only 20 percent of employees in the large organizations surveyed strongly agreed that their strengths are in play every day.##

The article says the share falls the longer an employee stays and the higher the person climbs. It also states that eight out of ten employees feel somewhat miscast in their role, though it does not clearly name where that figure comes from.

Gallup offers an example of a target: raising the strongly-agree share to 40 percent would double it, and 60 percent is not too aggressive a goal for the greatest organizations. A manager's job, it says, is to watch for clues to each person's natural talents and then place people where those talents can become strengths.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask the question about your own week.** The answer to Gallup's question says less about personal failings than about how tasks are handed out. An office worker can look at the week's task list on Friday and note which days gave room to what they do best. If none did, the note becomes a concrete reason to ask a manager about swapping one task with someone else.$$
