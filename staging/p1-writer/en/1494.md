A land mine took a Cambodian farmer's left leg. The artificial limb he was fitted with did its job, and he still did not get better. He felt constant anxiety about the future. What the doctors and his neighbours came up with next had four legs.

[[story]]
Johann Hari tells the story in *Lost Connections* (2018). One summary credits it to Derek Summerfield, a doctor who was studying mental health in Cambodia, though tellings differ on the details. The farmer lost his left leg to a land mine. He received an artificial limb and did not recover. He felt constant anxiety about the future, and despair.

His work had been the rice paddy, and now the paddy was too hard for him. Doctors and neighbours came and sat with him. They talked through his life with him.

##Then they bought him a cow, so that he could become a dairy farmer.## He had lost his old work and his picture of what came next, and the cow gave him a job he could do and a reason to get up.

Over the following months and years, his depression went away. Locals described the cow as an analgesic, and an antidepressant. The farmer, in other words, had been treated with the one thing the paddy and the leg had taken away, a daily job with a point to it.

Hari uses the case in his larger argument that depression is far from simply a chemical imbalance, though he allows that chemistry may be a small part of it.

Hari puts the story inside the book's frame. Among the nine causes of depression and anxiety he lists is losing a hopeful or secure future, and losing meaningful work. The farmer's despair follows that pattern, and the repair does too.

One cow is an anecdote, and a long way from a controlled trial. The account is secondhand, one man in one village, and its tellings differ on small points. Critics note that Hari is a writer rather than a scientist and accuse him of cherry-picking his evidence. Read it as an illustration, with that in mind.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask what useful role a person could still have, and help them take a first step toward it.** A retired teacher who has stopped leaving the flat may be asked by two neighbours whether he would go over their children's reading with them on Thursday afternoons. The invitation gives him somewhere to be and something to know, and the children's progress gives him something to look forward to on the next Thursday. It sits next to medical care and does not replace it, and you can offer it at once without waiting for anyone's diagnosis.$$
