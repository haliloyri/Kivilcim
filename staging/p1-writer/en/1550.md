An engineer understands what an executive seat demands, and has the strength to fill it. He just does not want to sit there. Gino Wickman tests whether a person fits a role with three short questions.

[[story]]
In *Traction: Get a Grip on Your Business*, Gino Wickman puts the people in a company through two separate filters. The first is the right-person question: does this individual share the company's core values? The second is the right-seat question: does this individual fit the seat? To measure the fit with the seat, he asks three questions he calls GWC.

G stands for Get it, meaning to grasp the job. Does the person understand what the position asks and how the work runs? W stands for Want it, meaning to actually want the job, with no one pushing from behind. C stands for Capacity, the ability to do it, covering mental, physical and emotional strength as well as time. All three must be a yes, and if one is a no, the person is not right for that seat.

One summary shows this with an engineer. He grasps an executive position and has the capacity to run it, but he does not want it. Two yeses do not cancel one no. In Wickman's chart this person, however capable, is not the right fit for that seat.

Wickman asks these questions when hiring and also of every seat that already exists. Does each person grasp their seat, want it and have the strength to run it? When both values and seat are met, the result is the state he calls right people in the right seats. If one of them fails, the chart shows from the start who has to fix what.

For values, the tool is the People Analyzer. Each employee is rated against each core value as "most of the time", "sometimes" or "never". Placed side by side, the two charts bring two separate questions into focus: is this person one of us, and does this seat fit this person?

The fine point of GWC is that the no is written against the seat, not against the talent. The engineer is not mismatched because he is a poor engineer. He is mismatched because he does not want that seat.

[[lessons]]
$$**Test a role with three questions: does he grasp it, want it, have the capacity.** A workshop owner who wants her best repairer to take over the accounts and orders can ask separately whether he understands that work, whether he really wants it and whether he has the time and energy for it. If one of the three is missing, the problem may be the shape of the seat and not the worth of the person. That points to a way of moving a good worker somewhere else without losing him.$$
