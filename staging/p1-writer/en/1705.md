A pager went off at a random moment of the day, and the person holding it had to write down what they were doing and how it felt. When Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi read the notes, the best moments were not the easiest ones.

[[story]]
Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's curiosity started with painters. Some of them painted for hours, forgetting hunger and tiredness, and then lost interest in the canvas as soon as it was finished. He wanted to understand what that absorption was, who had it, and under what conditions it arrived.

According to *Flow*, he asked many different people about the activities that swallowed them, including artists, athletes, chess players, rock climbers and dancers. The work looked nothing alike. Yet the people described the same thing, whether they were at a board, on a rock face or on a stage.

Then he built a method. Participants carried pagers that signalled at random moments of the day, and each time they wrote down what they were doing and how they felt. That way he collected feelings from the moment itself instead of memories of it.

As the notes piled up, a pattern appeared. The most absorbing moments came when the challenge of a task and the person's skill were both high and close to each other. A task far below a person's skill brought boredom, and one far above it brought anxiety.

Clear goals and immediate feedback helped keep people in that balance. Someone who could see at once what they were doing and how it was going sank further into the work. It also meant the same job could bore one person and overwhelm another: what mattered was where the task met the person.

One finding stood out. People reported this absorbed state more often at work than in their free time, yet they still said they would rather be somewhere else. The moments they were most absorbed in were often the ones that stretched them, even as they said they wanted comfort.

[[lessons]]
$$**Make the boring task harder and the overwhelming one smaller.** A secretary who writes the same report every day could set herself a target of finishing a little faster or catching one more error each time. For a task that overwhelms her, she can pick only the next small piece and look at that piece's clear goal. Absorption then stops being something that happens to you depending on your mood and becomes a balance you can aim for by adjusting the difficulty. Whether you lean towards boredom or anxiety tells you which direction to move.$$
