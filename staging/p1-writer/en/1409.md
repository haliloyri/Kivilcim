[[open]]
A stranger on the street calls you stupid. You have never met him, and he has never met you, yet you catch yourself wondering how he could possibly know, and whether everyone else can see it too.

[[story]]
Don Miguel Ruiz gives the second agreement in *The Four Agreements* a scene to stand on. If I see you on the street and say "Hey, you are so stupid," without knowing you, he writes, it is not about you. It is about me. But if you take it personally, you may start to believe you are stupid, and a thought arrives: how does he know? Is he clairvoyant, or can everybody see how stupid I am?

That chain of thought is what Ruiz calls living in the dream of hell, and it rests on one assumption, that the remark describes you. His claim is that nothing other people do is because of you. It is because of themselves. Each person lives in a world of their own beliefs and opinions, a kind of movie playing in the head, and what they say comes out of that movie. We take things personally, Ruiz says, because we agree with whatever was said, and the moment we agree the poison runs through us and we are stuck in that dream. Whatever happens around you, he writes, do not take it personally.

To take something personally, in his words, is to take someone else's emotional garbage and make it yours. Praise works the same way. If someone calls you wonderful, that too is about them, and most of the opinions you hold about yourself came from somebody else in the first place. A person who lies to you is afraid. A person who speaks badly about others probably speaks badly to himself as well, and needs compassion instead of retaliation.

Ruiz is plain about the payoff. Make it a strong habit not to take anything personally, he says, and you avoid many upsets: your anger, jealousy and envy will disappear, and even your sadness. He does not hide that this takes a very strong will. His practical suggestion is to write the agreement on a sheet of paper and stick it on the refrigerator, so you see it every day. If you can begin to live by the Four Agreements, he promises, the drama of hell will disappear before your eyes and you will be creating a dream of your own.

[[lessons]]
$$**Read a sharp remark first as the speaker's story.** Ruiz's stranger calls you stupid without knowing you, so the remark can only describe what is going on inside him. A waiter whom a customer snaps at over a late order can notice that the complaint belongs to the customer's afternoon, then check whether the order really was late. He fixes the part that is useful and leaves the rest where it came from, so the answer goes to the late order and not to the insult, and the rest of his afternoon stays his own.$$
