A graduate student sat down with the error counts from several hospital units and expected the strongest teams to sit at the bottom of the list. They sat at the top. It took her a while to understand what the numbers were actually measuring.

[[story]]
In *The Fearless Organization* (2018), Amy Edmondson goes back to her first research, done as a graduate student. She was studying how teamwork related to medication errors in hospital units. The obvious guess was that the best teams would make the fewest mistakes.

The data said otherwise. The units with the strongest teamwork and leadership reported the most errors.

She came to read the gap as a difference in climate. In some units, a nurse with a doubt could say it out loud and ask the others to check whether that was the right dose. In others, people put things under the rug if they thought they could get away with it. An error that nobody mentions never reaches the count, so a quiet unit and a careful unit can look identical on paper.

##The units reporting the most errors were not necessarily making the most. They were the units where an error could be said aloud.##

Edmondson later gave the climate a name: psychological safety, a belief that your voice is valued and that it is safe to raise ideas, questions, concerns and mistakes. The book opens with a neonatal nurse named Christina Price, who keeps quiet about a medication a premature baby needs rather than challenge the physician in charge. Silence, the book argues, can be a reasonable response to a risk the person feels.

For leaders, the book describes the moment the bad news arrives. Edmondson calls it responding productively: thank the person who brought the problem or the unusual idea, then turn straight to solving it, so that people keep speaking up. An open door is not enough, she says. A leader has to ask for views directly.

Edmondson is firm about what the term does not cover. She calls the idea that it means being nice the most important misconception, and says it is closer to the opposite of tiptoeing. She also rejects the worry that it lowers ambition. In her words, a team can stretch and accomplish great things together only with psychological safety.

[[lessons]]
$$**Read a rising count as a question about climate.** A restaurant's complaint log may fill up in the month the manager starts asking staff to write every complaint down, even though the food has not changed. Before you decide whether things are getting worse, ask whether people have become more willing to say what they see. Then think about how you answered the last person who told you something uncomfortable, because that answer is part of the climate too.$$
