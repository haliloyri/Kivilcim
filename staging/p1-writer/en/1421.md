A man in an antique store is about to be shot. He throws his hands up to his face, and the bullet bounces off his gold wedding ring. Hal Elrod tells this story to show you a miracle he does not want you to wait for, and to set up a very different kind.

[[story]]
Donnie Register turns up in Hal Elrod's *The Miracle Equation* (2019) as the book's picture of a miracle nobody can explain. He was working in an antique store when he was about to be shot. He raised his hands to his face, and the bullet bounced off his gold wedding ring. Nothing in that account is something anyone could have planned, rehearsed or earned.

Elrod calls events like this passive miracles. They are rare, they cannot be accounted for, and they happen to people instead of being made by them. Most of us will never meet one, and the book does not ask us to count on it.

##The miracle Elrod cares about is the one you make.## He calls it an active, or created, miracle: a result reached through belief, determination and hard work. His claim is that you have more control over this kind than you think. Register could not choose where the bullet went, but with a created miracle you choose what you sit down to work on each morning.

He allows that doubt has a place. Skepticism is reasonable, he says. The trouble starts when skepticism slides into cynicism, the shrug of what is the point, because a cynic has already decided that nothing can be created and so does not begin.

For the people who do begin, Elrod has a name: Miracle Mavens. A Maven goes after created miracles instead of sitting by the door for a passive one. The first tool in that pursuit is the equation the book is named for, unwavering faith plus extraordinary effort, the vow to stay with one goal and the daily work behind it.

A created miracle does not come with a guarantee, and Elrod says so. Setbacks will happen on the way, and faith, in the equation's terms, is what gets you back up after them. He asks you to read a roadblock as something to learn from, not a hurdle that ends the attempt. Even if you miss the end goal, he says, you come away with something that can lead you down a different road.

The ring and the Maven are two ends of one idea. One is an event that cannot be repeated. The other is a way of spending your time that can be started on any ordinary Tuesday.

[[lessons]]
$$**Separate what you are hoping for from what you can build.** A shop owner who wants more regular customers can wait for a mention in the paper, or she can phone the customers who bought from her last year and ask what they would want next. The first is luck that may never come, and the second is a task for this afternoon, with a phone in her hand and a list of names. When you sort a wish into one of those two kinds, you can drop the waiting and put your effort where it counts. $$
