Sticky notes covered the bathroom mirror, and the man in front of it weighed 297 pounds and wanted to become a Navy SEAL. David Goggins started closing the distance between those two facts with a mirror and a pad of notes.

[[story]]
In *Can't Hurt Me*, David Goggins recounts the time he weighed 297 pounds, about 135 kilos. The book places that weight in 1999. He had a goal at the time: to become a Navy SEAL. There was a wide gap between the body he had and the one that goal required. He was working in pest control at the time, a job that looked nothing like a SEAL candidate's life.

He found his tool in the bathroom mirror. In front of it he was alone, and the only person listening was himself. He wrote his goals on sticky notes and stuck them across the glass, and he called it the Accountability Mirror.

When he looked into it, he told himself the truth about where he was. He did not tell himself a softened story. The idea in the book is that the person who needs to hear the truth is the person who can do something with it.

Each night he wrote the next day's goals on the notes, and when the day was over he made sure he had actually worked toward them. Every day had a concrete task, and every evening had a way of facing it.

##The person who needed to hear the truth was the only one who could act on it.##

According to the book, Goggins lost more than a hundred pounds in this period. Accounts differ on the time and the final weight, and three months is the figure most often given. Pinning the whole loss on one practice would be an overreach. Still, the notes, the talk at the glass and the nightly check were the form of the promise he made to himself each day.

The book also recounts that Goggins went through SEAL training and finished it after more than one attempt. The mirror's part in that is the book's own framing, not a proven cause.

[[lessons]]
$$**Say your situation to yourself without softening it, and write tomorrow's task where you will see it.** Someone who has put off going to the gym after work for months can say to the mirror, "I didn't go once this week", rather than "I was too tired". Then they write down tomorrow's single concrete step, such as packing the bag, on the same mirror. The point is not to blame yourself but to leave nothing vague about what tomorrow holds. While the note stays where you can see it, the task stays in front of you and checking it tonight gets easier.$$
