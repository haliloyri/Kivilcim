Take two patients with similar troubles in a psychiatrist's office. Each names a different culprit. One blames himself for nearly everything, and the other blames the world. Both would leave the room stuck, for opposite reasons.

[[story]]
M. Scott Peck describes two patterns in *The Road Less Traveled*. The first is the neurotic, who assumes too much responsibility. A partner's anger is his fault, and a child's failure is his mistake, and when he is in conflict with the world he takes it for granted that the fault is his, so every quarrel ends at his door. Each pattern settles the question of fault before the facts are in.

The second has a character disorder and assumes too little. For this patient, the system is broken or luck did not help, and in the same conflict he takes it for granted that the world is at fault. The blame always points outward, and so nothing is left for him to change. Peck suggests there is a comfort in this, since change then becomes someone else's job. The neurotic, meanwhile, carries weight that was never his.

Peck's line about the pair is blunt: neurotics make themselves miserable, and people with character disorders make everyone else miserable. ##Neither pattern lets a person grow.##

Peck treats the two as the ends of a range, and each of us sits somewhere along it. Most people are a mix, so the work never finishes. In each quarrel they have to sort out what is theirs to answer for and what is not, and that takes a fresh look every time, because no rule settles it in advance.

There is a price to accepting a share. If your choices helped cause a situation, your choices can help change it, and that is both a power and a weight. Accepting a share therefore works as an admission and also as an opening for action.

[[lessons]]
$$**Sort out your share of a conflict.** A manager whose project slipped might blame the whole team, or he might take all of the blame himself. A fairer account lists what he decided, what the others decided and what was out of everyone's hands. That lets him repair his own part and ask the others to look at theirs, and it keeps the conversation on what can still be changed instead of on who was at fault. Nobody is asked to accept more than their own share, so the talk stays easier for everyone involved.$$

&&What part of a disagreement you are in right now belongs to you, however small it is?&&
