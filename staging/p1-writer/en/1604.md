At the end of an ordinary shower, with the water still warm, a hand goes to the tap and turns it to cold. That one gesture is where a famous method asks you to begin, not with an ice bath.

[[story]]
Wim Hof, known as the Iceman, developed his method over nearly forty years, and *The Wim Hof Method* (2020) rests on three pillars: cold exposure, breathing and mindset. The first chapter on cold is called "A Cold Shower a Day Keeps the Doctor Away." Its title sounds like a promise, but the instructions inside are modest.

Hof's own feats in the cold are what made him famous, among them experiments at Wayne State University in which he held his core temperature in extreme cold. He warns readers not to try those at home.

Hof does not start people in an ice bath. He recommends a four-week programme of cold showers instead, so the body is not shocked. After your normal warm shower, you finish with cold water, at least five days a week.

The first week asks for 30 seconds. Each week after that you add another 30. By the fourth week you stand under cold water for two minutes at the end of every shower. If 30 seconds is too much, he says to begin with 15.

He also gives permission to go slowly. You can enter the cold gradually, feet and legs first, then arms, then the torso. His rule is to follow your body's feedback and not force anything.

##The whole first week is half a minute of cold at the end of a shower you were taking anyway.## There is no new appointment to keep and almost nothing to decide.

One caution belongs here. Researchers are not certain that cold exposure improves health and stress the way Hof says, and a 2024 meta-analysis of eight studies of the method, with 15 to 48 people each, rated their quality very low and called for more research. The American Heart Association and the British Heart Foundation advise talking to a doctor before cold exposure.

[[lessons]]
$$**Attach the hard thing to a routine you already have.** Someone who wants to read more might put one page on the pillow every night instead of promising an hour a day. The page rides on a habit that already happens, so no willpower is spent deciding when to start. After a few weeks the extra pages can arrive without being planned, and on a night when you are too tired for more, you have still read the first one.$$
