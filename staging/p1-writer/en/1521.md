One line from Michael Singer's book gets quoted again and again in summaries: the prerequisite to true freedom is to decide that you do not want to suffer anymore. It sounds too simple to be a method, and the chapters around it say how he thinks it works.

[[story]]
Part three of *The Untethered Soul* (2007) is called Freeing Yourself, and its first chapter is titled "Let Go Now or Fall." The summaries describe its teaching in a few words: release fears and disturbances as early as possible, and let negative thoughts pass through you and float on by.

Summaries compress the part into two claims. You can never live a fear-free life, and the only way past fear is to let it go. And when you widen the focus of your consciousness, you step out of the confines of the personal self. Other chapters in the part are titled "Stealing Freedom for Your Soul" and "Pain, the Price of Freedom."

Singer's image for what goes wrong is a cage. The personal self, one summary says, functions like a cage that consciousness cannot get out of, and clinging to thoughts is what builds it. The same summary compares it to animals in small zoo cages, and to being so absorbed in a film that you forget the room you are sitting in.

The summaries add a worked example. Seeing your partner talk with someone else at a party upsets you, and the jealousy takes over your evening. Reflecting on your own death, the example goes, shows that their happiness matters more than the argument you were about to start.

The chapter on death gives the sharpest version of the exercise. Imagine you will die in a week, the summary says, then ask why you are not doing those things now. Elsewhere the book carries titles such as "Letting Go of False Solidity" and "The Spiritual Path of Nonresistance," which suggest that the cage feels firmer than it is.

Nothing here comes with evidence beyond the teaching itself. The summaries present no measurement, and this is a spiritual practice, not a treatment. Anyone dealing with severe anxiety or depression should not treat it as a substitute for professional help.

[[lessons]]
$$**Decide you are done suffering over one thing, then act on it today.** A person who replays an unfair remark from a colleague all weekend can ask, each time it returns, whether carrying it is doing anything for them. Usually it is not, and asking lets the thought pass without a fight. You can pick one grievance this week and notice how much of your attention it was taking. If the thought comes back, you can treat that as one more passing visit and ask the same question again, which keeps the practice small and repeatable.$$
