[[open]]
In a fable, an eccentric billionaire tells two struggling people that the fix for their lives starts at five in the morning, and he has a plan for every minute of that first hour. The plan is stranger than the alarm.

[[story]]
Robin Sharma's *The 5AM Club* is told as a fable. An eccentric billionaire mentors two people who are both struggling, an entrepreneur and an artist, and he asks them to rise at five a.m. Neither of them is in a good place, and the first thing he offers is a time of day.

Sharma calls the hour from five to six the Victory Hour. Its value, he argues, is that the world is still quiet. Nobody is asking you for anything, so the first hour belongs to you before the day's demands get a turn. The claim is a narrow one. Sharma does not say mornings are virtuous, only that this hour is one nobody else has claimed yet.

He splits the hour into three blocks of twenty minutes. Twenty minutes go to Move, which is intense exercise. These are the names the book gives them: Move, Reflect and Grow. Twenty go to Reflect, such as journaling or planning the day. The last twenty go to Grow, which means learning, reading for example. Body, head and knowledge each get an equal slice, and nobody has to decide on the spot how to divide the time.

It looks ordinary on paper: some exercise, a notebook, a book. What Sharma adds is the placement and the structure, the same three jobs in the same order at the quietest hour of the day.

He presents rising early as a habit you can install, not a fixed trait. He admits the start is hard, with an adjustment period before it becomes automatic. And his explanations of why the early hour works, which draw on brain and hormone effects, are his own reading. The book does not back them with measurements.

[[lessons]]
$$**Protect your time before other people can ask for it.** Time you hope to find later in the day tends to disappear among everyone else's requests. A freelance designer who wants to learn Python could give twenty minutes to the start of the morning, before the first client message lands, instead of waiting for an evening that never has room. Sharma's Victory Hour works as a guarded stretch of quiet, and splitting it into three jobs means you do not have to work out what to do first.$$
