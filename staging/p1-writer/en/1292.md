A room full of MBA students had just heard Ray Kroc speak, and now they were holding beers with the founder of McDonald's at their table. He asked them a question they thought they could answer in one word. He told them they were wrong.

[[story]]
Robert Kiyosaki tells this story in *Rich Dad Poor Dad*, and he tells it secondhand. Ray Kroc, the man who built McDonald's into a restaurant chain, had given a talk to an MBA class at the University of Texas at Austin. Afterwards the students invited him out for beers, and he came along.

Over the table, with the beers in front of them, Kroc asked the room: "What business am I in?"

The students answered with what the sign above every McDonald's says. He was in the hamburger business.

##Kroc shook his head. His business, he told them, was real estate.##

Kiyosaki reads that answer as strategy. The most important thing in McDonald's business, he says, is location, so the company went after the land under the restaurants, the best corners. A hamburger was what customers paid for. The ground the restaurant stood on was what the company was really building. A student would have watched burger sales, and Kroc was watching corners.

Kiyosaki then turns the story around to his reader. Most people, he says, think of their business as their job, the thing they get paid to do each day. Many spend their working lives building someone else's business, the owner's, while their own column of assets stays close to empty. In the book his advice is different. Keep your day job, but mind your own business: the column of assets you are building next to it.

He says it shows up in the order of a person's purchases. The rich buy assets first, and the luxuries come afterwards, paid for out of what the assets earn. Many other people buy the luxury first and call it an investment.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask what your work leaves you owning.** A freelance designer may see that all her income comes from hours, and that nothing she builds keeps earning when she stops. She could set aside part of each project to create a template or a set of icons that she can sell again to new clients. You start to look past the invoice at what is still yours after the job is done, and you can compare two offers on that basis as well as on the fee, which makes the next offer easier to weigh.$$
