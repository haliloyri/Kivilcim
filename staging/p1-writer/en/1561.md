A university hospital in Massachusetts opened a clinic for patients its own doctors could not fully help. It had no new drug to offer. The treatment was eight weeks of learning to sit still and pay attention, and the book that grew from it is built on that practice.

[[story]]
Jon Kabat-Zinn founded the Stress Reduction Clinic at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center in 1979, and *Full Catastrophe Living* grew out of its work. The clinic was a referral service. Physicians and other health providers sent it patients who were falling through the cracks of health care. Their diagnoses varied widely, and they had one thing in common: they were not responding completely to traditional treatment.

What the clinic offered was the Stress Reduction and Relaxation Program, later renamed mindfulness-based stress reduction, or MBSR. It was an eight-week course that taught mindfulness meditation and mindful hatha yoga. Part I of the book walks through the practices one by one: the body scan, mindfulness of breathing, mindful yoga, walking meditation, and paying attention to ordinary activities such as eating. Alongside them it sets out the attitudes the practice rests on: non-judging, patience, beginner's mind, trust, non-striving, acceptance and letting go.

A description of the program in Tricycle magazine adds the weekly rhythm. There was a class of about thirty people that met for two and a half hours, 45 minutes of practice a day, six days a week, and a day-long silent retreat. Each person met staff individually before joining. The program, as that article describes it, leaned on not striving, on telling healing apart from curing, and on watching thoughts as events in awareness. Those details come from that article, which says the book outlines the clinic's program, and not from the book itself.

The same piece mentions more than five thousand people who had taken the course. Their diagnoses ran from cancer and AIDS to high blood pressure, back pain and panic attacks, and 85 percent reported a significant reduction in symptoms. These are the participants' own reports, not a controlled trial. Today MBSR is offered in more than 740 hospitals, clinics and stand-alone programs around the world.

[[lessons]]
$$**Train attention through a daily routine.** The clinic asked patients for practice nearly every day for eight weeks, not one afternoon of good intentions. A nurse finishing an evening shift who wants to stop carrying her irritation home could spend a minute on the walk to the car noticing only her feet on the ground. After a few weeks, gathering her attention would feel less like a chore and more like something she already knows how to do.$$
