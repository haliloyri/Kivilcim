In Belfast, a mother of ten was taken from her home and never came back. Her neighbours kept quiet, and so did the organisation behind it. Years later, tapes recorded on the promise of staying sealed until death began to speak.

[[story]]
Jean McConville was a widow with ten children. In December 1972 she was taken from her flat in Belfast and vanished. Her children saw the night she was taken. The IRA was responsible, and it had accused her of being an informer. For decades the neighbourhood and the organisation both stayed silent about what had happened.

Patrick Radden Keefe's *Say Nothing* tells that silence. When she disappeared, the children were left on their own for a time and then scattered. The IRA denied it for years and only in 1999 admitted to some of the "disappeared". Her remains were found on a beach in 2003 by a man out walking. Even that did not explain everything.

Over the years, Boston College's Belfast Project recorded interviews with former paramilitaries. The project was run by the journalist Ed Moloney and a former IRA member, Anthony McIntyre. Among those who spoke were Dolours Price, who had gone to prison for a bombing in London in 1973, and Brendan Hughes, who had been one of the organisation's commanders in Belfast. They were promised that their tapes would stay sealed until they died.

Hughes died in 2008 and Price in 2013. In 2011, legal action over the recordings began in the United States. Whether the promise of secrecy would hold was being tested.

In the years afterwards, the politician Gerry Adams was questioned in connection with the case and released without charge. The case was not closed even years later.

##On one side stood decades of silence, and on the other tapes that were promised to stay sealed.## Keefe shows the two belonging to the same story. One happened because people stayed silent, and the other because someone spoke.

[[lessons]]
$$**Give a confidentiality promise only with the things that could force it open in mind.** An adviser who tells a client "this stays between us" should know that a court or the law might override it. Saying the promise together with its limits lets the other person decide knowingly what to share. You then give a word you can actually keep, and you can sleep afterwards. A smaller promise that holds beats a large one that breaks.$$
