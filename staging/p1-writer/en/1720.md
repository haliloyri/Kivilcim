[[story]]
Researchers Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper set up a jam tasting table in a market. On some days it showed 24 kinds of jam and on others just 6. In *The Paradox of Choice*, Barry Schwartz uses findings like this to show that more options do not always bring more freedom.

The large display stopped more people. According to the figures reported, about 60 percent of passers-by stopped at the 24-jam table, against about 40 percent at the 6-jam table. Abundance had caught people's attention.

The difference showed up in buying. Among the shoppers who stopped to taste, only about 3 percent of those who faced 24 jams bought one. Among those who faced 6 jams, the figure was about 30 percent. Tasters had also been given a coupon for money off a jar.

##The 24 jams drew a crowd, but the table with six produced a much higher rate of purchase.## As the options multiplied, comparing got harder, and the decision tired the mind more.

Schwartz draws a wider conclusion. As options grow, so does the opportunity, but so can the cost of comparing, regret and the tendency to put a decision off. In his book the weight does not fall equally on everyone: he separates maximizers, who always look for the very best, from satisficers, who settle for one that is good enough. Maximizers carry the unchosen alternatives in their heads and tend toward more regret. Expectations rise with the number of options, and every road not taken can feel like a loss.

One caution belongs here. Later studies have not found the effect every time, and one review put the average effect close to zero. So the 24 and 6 figures are an interesting scene, not a law that holds everywhere.

[[lessons]]
$$**When a decision feels heavy, shorten the list yourself before comparing.** A group of friends going back and forth between twelve restaurants for dinner lightens the load from the start if they first agree to pick only three. With fewer options a decision comes faster and you are more likely to be pleased with the result. Instead of glancing back at the nine restaurants you dropped, you go forward on the path you chose and enjoy the evening.$$
