Five lines, written once a day before the first message arrives. Robin Sharma does the arithmetic on them, and the total after a year is far larger than any single day makes it look. It is not a hard sum.

[[story]]
Robin Sharma's *The 5AM Club* is built around protecting the first hours of the morning for yourself. Inside that frame sits a small, practical suggestion: each morning, write down five small goals you want to finish that day. It is not a big project and not a life plan, just five things that fit inside a single day and can be crossed off before it ends.

Sharma's point rests on arithmetic. Five small wins a day come to 1,825 in a year, five times 365. That is thirty-five in a week and a little over 150 in a month. Each one looks unremarkable. The pile does not. A single day of five small wins feels like nothing, and a year of them is a long list.

It needs saying that this is a sum, not a measurement. The figure illustrates how small wins can stack up, and it is not a report of what happened to anyone. The book does not show that a person who wrote five goals every morning for a year finished exactly 1,825 tasks.

What the suggestion does in practice is move a decision. When you write the list in the morning, you choose what the day's small victories will be. If you do not, incoming messages and other people's requests make that choice for you. This ties in with Sharma's wider idea of keeping an early, quiet block for planning and focus, so that the list is written before the requests begin.

There are two ends to it. In the morning you decide what counts. In the evening you count how many you finished, and in between you glance at the list now and then. The gap between written and done tells you how realistic the list was, and tomorrow's list can be adjusted. Sharma presents it as a habit to watch yourself with, not a promise to keep at all costs.

[[lessons]]
$$**Decide what counts as a win before anyone else does.** Most days are set by whoever writes to you first, and by evening it is hard to say what a busy day actually produced. A project coordinator who opens the team chat before anything else starts the day on other people's questions. If she writes five small tasks first, she has a list she chose, and by evening she can count what she finished against it.$$
