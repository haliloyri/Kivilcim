Unwavering Faith plus Extraordinary Effort equals Miracles. It is written like a sum with two terms, and the author insists that is all a big goal needs. The odd part is what the first term asks of you: a decision you make before you know how you will feel.

[[story]]
Hal Elrod's *The Miracle Equation* (2019) carries its whole plan in the subtitle: two decisions that move your biggest goals from possible, to probable, to inevitable. The first decision is unwavering faith. In the summaries of the book it reads as a vow to yourself to stick to one goal, no matter what is going on outside and no matter how you feel inside, with no excuses.

That last part is the unusual one, because it leaves nothing to wait for on a good day. Setbacks will come, and the book says faith is what gets you back up after them, so the vow has to be in place before the setback arrives.

The second decision is extraordinary effort, and Elrod's description is plainer than the name. It is consistency. The key to success, he says, is not one remarkable action but a series of small actions you take every day.

##Choosing one goal matters as much as choosing the vow.## Elrod advises picking a single overarching mission instead of juggling many, the way an Olympic athlete concentrates on one sport. He then breaks the mission into monthly targets, which gives each month its own urgency.

The environment gets a turn as well. If obstacles stand between you and the daily action, he suggests removing them: gym clothes kept in the car, for instance, so the decision is half made before you leave the house. The book closes with a 30-Day Challenge, and a chapter called "Maintaining Miracles" about keeping it going once the first push is over.

Elrod also asks you to break the work into micro-steps, study the proven paths other people have taken, and block time for it on the calendar, so the action has an hour of its own and does not float.

The faith decision is not made once. It is made again each time a setback arrives, and the book treats a roadblock as something to learn from. Even if you miss the end goal, he says, you pick up something that can lead you down a different road.

The sum has no clever third term. Belief you keep whatever the mood, and small work you repeat until it counts.

[[lessons]]
$$**Decide the commitment before the mood arrives.** A goal that waits for the right feeling moves only on the days that feel good, and those days come less often than the work needs. A woman who wants to finish a certificate course picks that one course, sets a fixed evening for it each week and treats the evening as settled whatever her energy is, instead of waiting until she feels like studying. When you make the choice in advance like this, a flat day stops being a reason to skip, and you can watch whether the small sessions are actually adding up over the weeks.$$
