Four diseases, Peter Attia says, will most likely end the lives of most people who read this sentence. He calls them the Four Horsemen, and he believes one early warning may show up well before any of them arrives.

[[story]]
In *Outlive* (2023) Attia lists the Four Horsemen: heart disease, cancer, neurodegenerative disease such as Alzheimer's, and type 2 diabetes together with metabolic dysfunction. He says the odds are overwhelming that most people will die of one of them.

Age is not the whole story. Attia cites a figure: half of major cardiovascular events in men, and a third in women, happen before 65.

Medicine 2.0, in his telling, steps in once disease is established, when the diagnosis is already written on the chart and the damage has had years to build. Medicine 3.0 begins much earlier and weighs the risk of doing nothing.

He puts metabolic health at the centre of that early work. Elevated insulin is, in his phrase, the canary in the coal mine of metabolic disorder. A miner's canary reacted to bad air before the miners did, so it warned before the problem grew. In a glucose tolerance test the patient drinks a sugary solution holding 75 grams of glucose, and blood is drawn at set times afterwards. Early insulin resistance shows as a sharp rise in insulin during the first 30 minutes that stays high for the next hour.

The link to the other horsemen is that insulin resistance goes along with higher risk of cancer, Alzheimer's and cardiovascular death. In Attia's reading, insulin sensitivity is the variable that connects all four. That is an association and his clinical view, with no proven single chain of cause behind it.

Medicine 3.0 uses five tactics: exercise, nutrition, sleep, emotional health, and outside molecules such as drugs, hormones and supplements. All of them aim at the years before disease shows.

Attia's framework is a clinician's reading. It does not promise that one remedy covers all four diseases.

[[lessons]]
$$**Watch the early signal, not only the late damage.** A fleet mechanic who checks the oil every month sees trouble well before the engine light comes on, and by the time the light shows, the job is usually bigger. In your own work, budget or health, you can look for a gauge that moves first and check it on a schedule. That gives you time to act while the fix is still small and the options are still open, which is the whole point of a canary.$$
