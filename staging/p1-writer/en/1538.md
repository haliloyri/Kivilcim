[[story]]
Matthew Syed opens the case in *Bounce* (2010) with his own childhood. He grew up on an unremarkable road in south-east England, with no sporting background in the family. He went on to become England's top table tennis player and a two-time Olympian.

For a period in the 1980s, by one review's account, his street and the area around it produced more outstanding table tennis players than the rest of the nation combined. Syed wanted to know why. Talent scattered by chance onto one road did not seem a good enough answer.

A teacher at his local school, who was also England's top table tennis coach, had recruited Syed, his older brother and other promising children to a local club. A summary of the book gives the coach's name as Peter Charters and the club as Omega, a small one-table hut open around the clock to its members. Twenty-four-hour access meant a child could play whenever the urge came.

At home the brothers practised against each other for hours a day, starting on a tournament-sized table in their parents' large garage. ##Each of them always had someone good to play.##

The street fits his wider claim in the book, the primacy of practice: excellence generally takes around ten years, or 10,000 hours, of purposeful practice, often with coaching. His own road is his case study.

Syed turns the question around. If more young people had shared his opportunities, he concedes, he might not have been number one in England, possibly "number one thousand and one." His explanation for the street is not a rare gene but a coach, a club that never closed and a brother across the net.

[[lessons]]
$$**Before crediting talent, look for the room, the coach and the partner.** A neighbour who reads brilliantly may simply have grown up in a house where an adult read aloud every evening. Nobody noticed, so it was called a gift. When you see an impressive skill, ask where the person practised and who practised with them, and you will know what to give a child, a colleague or yourself. Often the first gift is a regular place and one good partner.$$
