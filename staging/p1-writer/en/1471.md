By ten thirty at night Charles Darwin was in bed, and his scientific work for the day had taken up about four hours of it. The rest of the day held a sofa, a gravel path, a newspaper and a backgammon board.

[[story]]
Alex Soojung-Kim Pang uses Darwin in *Rest* as his lead example of a creative worker with a short working day. Darwin began in his study after eight o'clock and worked about ninety minutes. Then he rested for about an hour on the sofa while his wife Emma read to him, a novel or his letters. After that came about ninety minutes more of work.

Around midday he took his main walk on the Sandwalk, a gravel path about a quarter of a mile long at the back of his garden. Pang treats it as an example of deliberate rest. At one o'clock he ate with the family, then went back to the sofa with *The Times* and wrote his letters.

The afternoon brought another rest and another walk. Then Darwin returned to the study for about an hour of work. Supper followed, and Emma played the piano, and a game of backgammon filled the evening before bed at ten thirty.

Added up, the working time is about four hours. Pang does not read the rest as idleness. He argues that hard creative work has to be done in short blocks, and that the brain recovers between them during walks and quiet hours.

The book's larger argument rests on a day like this one. Pang holds that work and rest are partners, that rest is active, and that it is a skill that can be learned. Darwin's sofa, backgammon board and gravel path are the everyday form of that claim, because they are gaps placed deliberately between the hours of effort.

Not everyone accepts the four-hour figure. Critics say it may understate how long Darwin's scientific work really went on, and that one man's routine does not show that four hours is right for every profession.

[[lessons]]
$$**Split hard work into short blocks with a real break between them.** Work that needs full attention can be done in one long sitting or in parts, and the break can be a planned piece of the job. A grant writer who drafts in two morning blocks with a short walk between them can give the afternoon to email and small tasks. The most tiring work then gets the hours when you are freshest. Writing the break into the calendar protects those hours instead of leaving them to whatever time is left.$$
