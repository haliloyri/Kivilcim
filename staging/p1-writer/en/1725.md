A motivational coach sat down across from Ray Dalio, John Bogle and Carl Icahn. He had not come to share his own view of the markets. He had come to ask the people who run money how they do it, after a crisis that had hurt many ordinary savers.

[[story]]
Tony Robbins is known for coaching people on peak performance, not for writing about finance. After the 2008 financial crisis he turned in that direction. In interviews he has said that seeing how much people suffered in the crisis pushed him to write a book about money.

In the same interviews he says the crisis reminded him of his childhood, when people could not afford food or school for their kids. He also says he concluded the system had to change, and names the documentary *Inside Job* among the things that deepened his frustration.

*Money: Master the Game* came out in 2014. Its subtitle promises seven simple steps to financial freedom. The content leans less on Robbins's own opinions than on conversations with figures at the top of finance, and Ray Dalio, John Bogle, Carl Icahn and Paul Tudor Jones are among those named.

Each carries a different idea. Dalio founded the investment firm Bridgewater, and he explains his approach to a portfolio meant to hold up in any economic weather, known as All Weather. Bogle founded the fund company Vanguard and championed index funds, which do not pick single stocks but follow a market index. Icahn is known as an activist investor, and Tudor Jones founded a hedge-fund firm. It is also reported that Robbins had coached Tudor Jones for years, which may have helped open some doors.

On one side sit large institutions with their strategies, and on the other an ordinary investor who has no access to them. The book's stated aim is to narrow that distance, by putting methods that work for institutions in front of ordinary people.

##After the crisis, a coach known for motivation went to the people who hold the answers instead of supplying his own.##

[[lessons]]
$$**Where you do not have the answer, ask the people who know it best.** Say you cannot make sense of a clause in your rental contract and a friend of yours works as an accountant. Instead of guessing, you can bring that friend two specific questions. That is what Robbins did with Dalio, Bogle and the others: he took the question to the specialist rather than filling the gap with his own view. You come away with a more accurate answer and something you can pass on to others who ask the same thing, because you took the question to the right person.$$
