[[open]]
A book about getting up at five in the morning gives a whole chapter to rest. Its title puts two things most people keep apart, hard work and recovery, side by side and gives them the same weight.

[[story]]
Robin Sharma's *The 5 AM Club* (2018) is mostly remembered for its hour before sunrise, and Sharma lays that hour out in detail: twenty minutes of movement, twenty of reflection, twenty of learning. One chapter, though, is about something else. It is called "The Twin Cycles of Elite Performance."

The book is built as a story in which an entrepreneur and an artist take lessons from an eccentric billionaire, and the twin cycles are one of those lessons. The first cycle is a stretch of intense, focused, high-quality work. The second is a stretch of deliberate recovery. Summaries of the chapter call them the High-Excellence Cycle and the Deep-Refueling Cycle, and they are meant to follow one another in turn.

Sharma's claim is short. Elite performance without deep recovery wears down the productive assets of the person doing it. On his own site he calls rest a necessity for growth and for using your talents fully, and he says it is no luxury. Recovery here counts as part of the work: it is the maintenance of whoever does the work.

He says the model has helped executives and athletes he coaches, though the sources name none of them. What the chapter gives us is a pattern, with no scene to retell. A hard block of work comes first, then a planned stretch of recovery, then another hard block, and the recovery has the same standing on the calendar as the work.

Someone who follows that pattern is not caught off guard by tiredness. The time to recover is already set, so a person who has been pushing hard does not have to earn a break by running down a little further first.

[[lessons]]
$$**Schedule recovery as carefully as the work.** A line cook who works every shift of the week finds by Friday that he can no longer taste what he is plating, and one fixed day off in the middle of the week changes what leaves the kitchen on Friday. Sharma's two cycles say the same about any demanding job: focused effort keeps its quality only when a planned stretch of real recovery sits beside it. If you put the recovery on the calendar before a heavy period starts, you can finish that period tired in a way you expected, and you have something to defend when someone asks for one more deadline.$$
