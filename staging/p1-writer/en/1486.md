[[open]]
A flip phone that cannot get online, a printed road map and a beach house in Provincetown. That was the kit for three months of life without a connected screen, and the man who tried it later wrote a book about attention.

[[story]]
Johann Hari describes the experiment in *Stolen Focus* (2022). He settled into a beach house in Provincetown, Massachusetts, and used a flip phone that had no internet. When he needed to get somewhere, he used a printed road map. According to one review of the book, he stayed three months, alone and without screens.

The first days were uncomfortable. Hari reports feeling agitated at the start. The longer he stayed away, though, the easier it became to clear his head and stay with a single task. And when he let his mind wander, he says, it brought new ideas.

The book's own website gives reported figures for the problem he was looking at: US college students focusing on one task for about 65 seconds, and office workers for about three minutes. They are reported numbers, and the site does not say which studies they come from.

##Hari does not treat his own calm as the answer.## His book argues that lost attention comes from forces bigger than one person's discipline, and it sorts them into twelve causes. Exhaustion is one, constant interruption by smartphones another, switching between tasks a third, and the decline of mind-wandering and pollution are on the list as well.

His remedies are collective. He calls for regulating tech companies, treating attention as a public health issue, limiting internet use, shorter working hours (a four-day week is mentioned) and more time for children to play away from screens. The name he gives the whole push is an attention rebellion.

The book also goes looking elsewhere. It features a favela in Rio where everyone lost attention in an unusual way, veterinarians who diagnose dogs with ADHD, and an office in New Zealand that found a technique to restore its workers' attention.

The beach house shows what a quiet phone can do for one person who can leave. The rest of the book asks why that quiet is so hard to get back at home, at work and at school.

[[lessons]]
$$**Test a calm hour before you trust it as a plan.** Someone who finishes a report easily in a quiet library may find the same report impossible at an open-plan desk with chat pinging. The difference is a clue about the setting and not a verdict on the person, so name what the library had and the desk lacks. Then you can ask for one of those things at your desk, and tell apart what you can change yourself from what belongs on a manager's list, instead of blaming yourself.$$

[[reflect]]
&&Think of one place or hour where you stay with a task easily: what does that setting have that your usual desk does not?&&
