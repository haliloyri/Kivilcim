One word in Brené Brown's trust checklist is the only one that needs an audience to be tested. Six of the seven can be met alone at a desk. This one shows up in a choice.

[[story]]
The trust framework in *Braving the Wilderness* (2017) is an acronym, BRAVING, and each letter stands for one element: Boundaries, Reliability, Accountability, Vault, Integrity, Non-judgment and Generosity. In Brown's descriptions, boundaries means respecting what the other person says is and is not okay, and asking when it is unclear. Reliability means doing what you say you will do. Accountability means owning mistakes, apologizing and making amends. Vault means not sharing what is not yours to share. Non-judgment means each person can ask for what they need and talk about feelings without being judged. Generosity means giving the most generous reading to the intentions, words and actions of others.

Integrity is the element about values, and Brown defines it in three parts. It means choosing courage over comfort. It means choosing what is right over what is fun, fast or easy. And it means practicing your values, not just professing them.

##Integrity, in her definition, means practicing your values, not just professing them.## A value that hangs on a wall is professed. A value that decides something on a day when the fast way is open is practiced, and the definition is built around that difference.

The element connects to what the book says about belonging. True belonging means feeling secure in communicating and living out your values despite what others may think. That puts the value test in front of other people, who may not share it. Fitting in would ask you to drop the value for the room, and integrity is the element that keeps it.

The framework also points in two directions. Building trust in other people is one half. Building trust in yourself is the other, and it is one of the strategies the book proposes for finding true belonging.

[[lessons]]
$$**Test a value by the moment it costs you something.** A shop owner who says honesty matters and then returns the extra change a customer overpaid has practiced the value, not only said it. The moment worth watching is the one where the easy option is open and the right one takes longer. If you spot that moment in your own week, you can choose on purpose instead of by habit.$$
