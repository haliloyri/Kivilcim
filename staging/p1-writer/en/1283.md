[[story]]
Paulo Coelho's novel *The Alchemist* (1988) follows Santiago, a young shepherd from Andalusia, who travels toward the Egyptian pyramids chasing a recurring dream of treasure. Early on, a king named Melchizedek tells him to follow the omens and gives him two stones, Urim and Thummim, while warning him to rely on his own decisions. In a crystal shop in Tangier, Santiago shows he is learning: watching a tired man climb the hill, he suggests the shop sell tea.

Later, tribal wars in the desert force his caravan to stop at the oasis of Al-Fayoum. The oasis is neutral ground. Inside it, nobody is allowed to carry weapons.

One day Santiago is in the desert watching two hawks flying high above him. One of them attacks the other. In that instant an image comes to him: an army, swords drawn, riding into the oasis. Then it is gone.

He tells the camel driver he has been travelling with. The camel driver tells him to take it to the tribal chieftains. So Santiago, a newcomer of a few days, stands in front of the men who rule the oasis and describes the hawks and what came after.

The chieftains decide. The next day the men of the oasis will break the old agreement and carry arms. If no attack comes, Santiago will pay for the warning with his life. If it comes, he will be rewarded.

That night, as he leaves the chieftains, a stranger on horseback blocks his path and threatens him. Santiago says he is not afraid to die. The stranger turns out to be the alchemist Santiago has been looking for, and he tells him it was a test of courage.

The following day about five hundred tribesmen ride into the oasis with their weapons hidden under their robes. The men of the oasis are armed and waiting, and they defeat them. ##Santiago is given fifty pieces of gold and asked to become counselor of the oasis.## The alchemist takes him on as his student, and the journey continues with a teacher at his side.

[[lessons]]
$$**Say the warning sign you noticed to the people who can act on it.** When you spot something others have missed, staying quiet feels safe, especially if you are new or junior, but a warning only helps once someone hears it. A trainee electrician who notices a scorch mark behind a panel and tells the site lead, knowing he might look foolish, can stop a small fault from becoming a fire. Saying what you saw, plainly, and what it is based on lets you share the risk and leave the decision with the people who can make it.$$
