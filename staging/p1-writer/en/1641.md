Standing over a sleeping child, a parent can feel a sudden jolt of fear that something might happen. Brené Brown says joy arrives with exactly that jolt, and that the people who feel joy most have a habit for it.

[[story]]
In *The Gifts of Imperfection* (2010), Brené Brown lays out ten guideposts. The fourth is titled cultivating gratitude and joy, meaning letting go of scarcity and the fear of the dark. The central finding in that chapter is that joy is not a state that simply arrives.

By her account, in years of research she never met anyone who could truly feel joy without regularly practicing gratitude. The word practice matters: gratitude here is something done, not a mood. It is a behavior that gets repeated.

The word scarcity in the title points to a feeling that nothing is enough: someone who is never safe enough, successful enough or well off enough waits for the next thing without seeing what they already have. The practice of gratitude puts what is already there against that way of looking.

The book also describes a trap in joy. According to Brown, when joy comes, many people feel fear settle in straight away. She calls it foreboding joy. A parent looking at their child, in the middle of a lovely moment, pictures the worst. The reason is that joy is among the most exposed emotions: opening up to it also means facing the chance of loss.

The people who feel the most joy are not exempt from this fear. What differs is what they do with it. Instead of rehearsing disaster in their heads, they stop and name what they are grateful for. Attention moves from a future loss to what is present now.

Brown also explains why this matters. If we try to numb painful feelings, we numb joy and gratitude as well, because emotions cannot be dulled selectively. Practicing gratitude instead of chasing the fear is how a person stays open to joy.

[[lessons]]
$$**When joy arrives, look at what you have.** Someone laughing at the family dinner table who suddenly thinks "what if something happens" can stop trying to silence the thought and name three things on the table aloud instead. Attention returns from a possible future loss to the real moment in front of them, and the joy is not cut short. Doing this every day turns joy from something left to luck into a practice you work at, and when the fear returns there is an answer ready for it.$$
