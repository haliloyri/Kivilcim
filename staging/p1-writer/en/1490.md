Critics said Portugal would turn into a haven for drug users, and the country went ahead anyway with a new law. Johann Hari went to Portugal to ask a doctor who helped shape the policy what became of that prediction, and what the money did instead.

[[story]]
For *Chasing the Scream*, published in 2015, Hari traveled to Portugal and spoke with Dr. João Goulão, a physician involved in building the country's approach to drugs. The policy took effect in 2001. Possessing any drug for personal use stopped being a crime. Selling stayed illegal, so a user was no longer treated as a criminal while a dealer still was.

Critics expected the worst. Portugal, they said, would become a drug haven. The government, though, moved the money. What had been spent on punishing users went to treatment, and to helping people back into ordinary life, including work.

For decades the usual answer to drugs had been punishment, and Hari's book is largely an argument against that habit. Portugal offered a rare chance to see the opposite tried on a national scale.

The book reports that injecting drug use roughly halved. HIV infections fell, and so did deaths from overdose.

##Hari's reading is that punishment pushes users further from the very bonds that could help them stop.##

His reasoning runs like this. A person labeled a criminal hesitates to ask for help and hides, and so drifts away from people who could support them. The message of a penalty is that you are guilty, and Hari argues that this feeds the sense of shame the person is already trying to escape. On this view, a state that punishes pushes the user into the shadows, while a state that offers a doctor and a job keeps the user inside the system.

The key word is reconnection. Treatment is not offered as a punishment, and a job is not offered as a prize. Both are set up so that the person has a place in ordinary life again.

An honest caveat: how much of the change came from the law itself is debated. The law did not arrive alone. It came with treatment and job support, so it is hard to say which part did how much.

$$**After a failure, make the way back easier.** If a teammate misses deadline after deadline, questioning them in front of everyone moves them further from the group and makes the next conversation harder to start. Asking what is getting in the way, and agreeing on a small plan, keeps them inside the team and still accountable for the work. That way your reaction takes into account whether the person can return, and the fix stays within reach instead of leaving them to face it alone.$$
