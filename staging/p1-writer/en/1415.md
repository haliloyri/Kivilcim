Abraham Lincoln once had a company of men, a captain's rank and a gate he could not get them through. The rank had come to him by custom, and the command he needed would not come at all. Half the work of leading, it turned out, had never been handed over with the title.

[[story]]
In 1832, as John Maxwell tells it in *The 21 Irrefutable Laws of Leadership* (1998), a young Lincoln raised a group of volunteers for the militia in the Black Hawk War. By the usual practice the man who organized the company took charge, so Lincoln became its captain.

He knew very little about soldiering. One day he had to get his men through a gate and could not recall the proper command. What he shouted instead has survived in the retelling:

> "This company is dismissed for two minutes, when it will fall in again on the other side of the gate."

In Maxwell's account, his influence declined from there. Lincoln began as a captain and finished his service as a private. The rank had been handed to him, but his men's willingness to follow was never part of the handover.

Maxwell sets a very different room beside that gate. In 1994 Mother Teresa spoke at the National Prayer Breakfast in Washington, with the capital's establishment in her audience. She talked about family values and abortion, and she offended nearly everyone there. They listened anyway, and most applauded at the end. Her standing rested on her character and her track record, not on an office.

From these two scenes Maxwell draws his second law, the Law of Influence: "The true measure of leadership is influence, nothing more, nothing less." True leadership, he says, cannot be awarded, appointed or assigned. It comes from influence, and influence has to be earned.

He lists five myths about leadership to show how many things pass for it: management, entrepreneurship, knowledge, being a pioneer and holding a position. A manager, an entrepreneur, an expert, a trailblazer and a person with a title can each be a leader. None of them is one automatically.

Retellings of Lincoln's militia service differ in the details, and the tidy drop from captain to private is Maxwell's telling.

[[lessons]]
$$**Watch who acts on your word without being told twice.** A new shift lead who emails orders to the whole team may find that the veteran cashier is still the one who gets the questions. The title decides who gets the first reply, but it does not decide who is listened to. Notice whom people turn to when they are stuck, and ask what that person does that you could do. Influence rarely arrives with the job, and it usually has to be built one small conversation after another, long before anyone needs to be ordered.$$
