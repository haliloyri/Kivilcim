[[story]]
*Getting to Yes* (1981) by Roger Fisher and William Ury contains a chapter whose title is a question: "What If They Are More Powerful?" The answer in the subtitle is short: develop your BATNA. The authors coined the term, short for best alternative to a negotiated agreement. It is the backup plan, the place you go if no deal comes together. It is also the reference point against which the whole table is judged.

One sentence carries the idea: the reason you negotiate is to produce something better than the results you can obtain without negotiating. An offer on the table is not good or bad by itself. It is good or bad compared with something.

The BATNA is that something. Each proposal gets held against it. If the offer beats your alternative, you consider it, and if it doesn't, you can leave. The same test works for every proposal that follows, so the third offer of the afternoon is measured against the same alternative as the first.

##The authors tie a negotiator's power to what happens when there is no agreement, ahead of money or influence.## That changes the first question of preparation. Before "how much will I ask for", you ask "what do I do if this falls through". A weak answer makes any offer feel urgent, and a solid one lets you stay calm at the table.

The chapter's own question follows from that. If the other side looks stronger, much of the weakness you feel comes from having nowhere else to go. Improving your alternative, for instance by sounding out another buyer before the meeting, shifts your position in the room, even when the other side keeps the same size and the same offer.

[[lessons]]
$$**Write down your alternative before you go to the table.** Someone selling an old car doesn't have to say yes under pressure to the first buyer, especially if a second ad is already running. They can hold each offer against that second option. A person who knows the alternative in advance can turn down a poor offer and still recognize a good one, because there is something concrete to compare it with. Once that second option is written down, a buyer's hurry puts far less pressure on you.$$
