A splinter only hurts when something touches it, and Michael Singer builds a whole chapter on that fact. The part of the body he cares about is the heart, and what he says about the splinter explains why people close up.

[[story]]
Part two of Singer's *The Untethered Soul* (2007) is called Experiencing Energy. Its chapters include "Infinite Energy," "The Secrets of the Spiritual Heart" and "Transcending the Tendency to Close." In Singer's account, energy gets blocked when you close up against something or cling to it, and, as a summary puts it, blocked energy cannot reach the heart. The heart then grows weaker and more open to fear.

The summaries give an everyday example. After a partner leaves, you feel depressed and heavy. Then the ex calls and says they want to come over, and energy rushes back at once. One reading is that the energy was there all along and something had been standing in its way.

Singer's picture for the sore place itself is a splinter. It hurts when touched, and an accidental bump can keep you awake at night. You can spend your life arranging things so that nothing touches it, or you can pull it out. The chapter that develops the idea is titled "Removing Your Inner Thorn," and a summary paraphrases it this way: letting go of your fear is just like removing a splinter.

What the summaries describe as the remedy is not to harden. When something stings, you stay open, relax, and let the feeling move through you so that the heart can expand again. They list what closing looks like: a painful breakup that leaves someone shut off, a betrayal that makes someone defensive, a failure that makes someone shrink inward.

This is a spiritual teaching about energy and the heart in Singer's own vocabulary, not physiology. The summaries mention no measurement behind it, so it reads best as a practice to try on yourself, not as a finding.

[[lessons]]
$$**Notice where you close before you decide what to do.** A person who was criticized in a meeting may go quiet for the rest of the day, answer in single words and call that being fine. The tightening is a signal about a sore spot, and noticing it gives you the chance to stay in the conversation a little longer instead of leaving it. That is a small thing to try, and it asks for attention rather than for any large change in how you live.$$
