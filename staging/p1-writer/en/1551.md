[[story]]
In *Traction: Get a Grip on Your Business*, Gino Wickman calls the weekly leadership meeting the Level 10. The first rule is time: it is held on the same day and at the same hour every week, it starts on time and ends on time. It runs ninety minutes, and its seven segments are fixed in advance. The aim is to keep the team aligned, to review what matters and to solve its most important issues.

The first five minutes are a segue. People reconnect as people before they get into business, and move from working in the company to working on it. Then five minutes go to the scorecard: the weekly numbers are checked and anything behind its target is flagged. In the third five minutes the quarterly goals, the Rocks, are marked on track or off track. An off-track Rock is not argued over, it moves to the issues list.

Customer and employee headlines come fourth, the week's important news in five minutes. The fifth looks at last week's to-do list, marking which items are done and which are not. The first five segments exist only to report, and discussion is not allowed at this stage.

The meeting's real weight sits in the sixth segment, IDS: identify, discuss, solve. Sixty minutes go here. The team starts with the most important issue, gets to the root cause, discusses it openly and solves it. A decision can then become a to-do that one person owns over the next seven days.

In the last five minutes the new to-dos are recapped, messages to share are confirmed, the meeting is rated and it ends on time. If a topic drifts, anyone can call "tangent alert!". Together with these small habits, the fixed order itself is what makes Wickman's meeting disciplined.

[[lessons]]
$$**Give a meeting a fixed order and spend the discussion on solving.** At a small design studio, a weekly meeting where everything is discussed but nothing is closed could switch to a pattern where reports stay short and then the most important issue is chosen and solved. Everyone then knows what they bring in and what they leave with, and nobody has to wait through other people's items to find out. What was said turns into one person's task for the coming seven days instead of being reopened next week.$$
