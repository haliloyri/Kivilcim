Aimee Mullins is an athlete with double leg amputations, and her TED talk was about prosthetics. According to one author, prosthetics were not what she cared about. Something bigger sat underneath.

[[story]]
TED began in 1984, and in a piece Gallo wrote around the book's release its talks had been viewed more than a billion times, at over two million views a day. What those talks had in common was what Gallo set out to find.

Carmine Gallo watched five hundred TED talks for *Talk Like TED*, more than 150 hours of video in all, and combined them with interviews of TED speakers. He sorted the nine secrets he drew from them under three headings: emotional, novel and memorable. The first secret is passion. The chapter asks speakers to find a personal link to their topic, and it rests on a claim he states bluntly: passion is contagious, literally, and you cannot inspire others until you are inspired yourself.

His example is Aimee Mullins, an athlete with double leg amputations. Her subject was prosthetics, which would seem to make her an obvious fan of the subject. Gallo writes that she is not passionate about prosthetics. She is passionate about unleashing human potential, and prosthetics are simply what she was asked to talk about.

That gap between subject and passion is where the practical advice sits. A topic is often assigned or picked because it is convenient. What makes a listener lean in is the larger thing underneath it. Gallo argues that the most popular TED speakers present their ideas as a calling rather than a job, and he wants a speaker to find that calling before building anything else, because what reaches the audience is the speaker's own excitement.

He backs this with an investor study. According to Gallo, when investors judged an entrepreneur's potential, the passion they perceived ranked well above criteria such as education and start-up experience. The people holding the money, he reads from this, respond to the same thing a roomful of listeners does.

Storytelling, rehearsal and the eighteen-minute limit all come later in the book. Gallo puts passion in front of them.

[[lessons]]
$$**Find what you care about beneath the topic.** Mullins had a subject, prosthetics, and a passion under it, human potential, and Gallo treats the second as the part the audience catches. A school caretaker asked to say a few words about the new recycling bins could ask himself why he walks the corridors early every morning, and open with that answer. The bins then become the occasion for something he means, and listeners remember the reason he gave instead of the announcement about the bins.$$
