The first words of Kabat-Zinn's definition of mindfulness are "on purpose". Stand in the shower and try to remember whether you already used the shampoo, and you will see why those two words come first. Everything else in the definition depends on them.

[[story]]
Jon Kabat-Zinn defines mindfulness in *Wherever You Go, There You Are* (1994) as paying attention in a particular way: on purpose, in the present moment, and nonjudgmentally. Each part can be checked separately. Something done on purpose has been chosen. Being in the present moment means your mind is where your body is. Nonjudgmentally means that when your attention drifts, you don't scold yourself.

The book says much of life runs on automatic, and many moments pass without being lived. Meditation is presented as a way of waking up from that. The Afterword warns against telling ourselves nice stories and sleepwalking through our days.

His suggestions are ordinary ones. In the shower, over a meal, with a child, do one thing at a time. It sounds too small to bother with, and it is easy to test. Eat lunch without a screen and notice how often the fork goes up while your mind is somewhere else. In the shower it means feeling the water on your shoulders and smelling the soap, instead of rehearsing the morning's first meeting under the spray. When the rehearsal starts anyway, you notice it and come back to the water.

##One of the book's more comforting lines says that forgetting or neglecting to be mindful can teach you a lot more than being mindful all the time.## So the moment you catch yourself drifting is not a failure of the practice. It is the practice.

That changes what you do after a lapse. You don't need a verdict on yourself. If you reach for the phone after the first bite, you go back to the bite, and the plate is still there. The "nonjudgmentally" in the definition does its work here: the third slip of the meal gets the same treatment as the first, a quiet return to the fork.

[[lessons]]
$$**Count the noticing as part of the practice.** Say you are listening to your partner over dinner and your mind slides to tomorrow's workload; the moment you see it, you can return and hear the rest of the sentence, even if you missed the start. Returning without a lecture to yourself makes the next slip quicker to spot, because you are no longer busy defending the last one. Counted this way, every drift you catch is a small piece of practice already done, and each return takes only a breath.$$

[[reflect]]
&&Which single routine this week, a shower, a meal or a walk, will you try doing as the only thing, and what will you do when your attention wanders?&&
