Three a.m., and the ovens are already on. By seven the shop smells of fresh pies, the floors gleam, and the woman who bakes them is wiping her eyes with her apron. She opens in thirty minutes. She has just said something no customer would believe from her.

[[story]]
Michael E. Gerber met Sarah three years after she opened her pie shop, and he tells the meeting in *The E-Myth Revisited*. He calls the shop All About Pies and admits that isn't its real name. Her first line to him was short: "They have been the longest three years of my life."

Then came the confession. She hated all of it, she said, sweeping her arms around the little room. But most of all she hated baking pies. She couldn't stand the thought of pies, the smell of pies, the sight of pies. And she started crying while the air filled with that sweet, fresh smell.

She walked him through the day. Up at two, in the shop since three. Get the pies ready, open, serve the customers, clean, close, do the shopping, reconcile the cash register, go to the bank, eat dinner, prepare tomorrow's bake. Done at nine-thirty or ten. And then she still had to sit down and work out how to pay next month's rent.

Her best friends had talked her into it. They told her she was crazy not to open a pie shop when she was so good at it, and she believed them. It looked like a way out of a job she hated. She bought the best oak floors, the best ovens and charming displays, and went deep into debt. Halfway through talking, she kicked the huge black oven. Twice.

She had learned to bake as a girl from an aunt who lived with the family. The aunt taught her the kneading, the flour, the careful cutting of apples, cherries, rhubarb and peaches, and slowed her down when she rushed. "Sarah, dear, we have all the time in the world. Baking pies is not about getting done. It's about baking pies." Now her whole day was about getting done.

Gerber has names for what happened. The urge that started the shop is an Entrepreneurial Seizure. The mistake underneath it is the Fatal Assumption: if you understand the technical work of a business, you understand a business that does that work. Everyone who starts a business, he says, is three people at once. The Entrepreneur dreams, the Manager keeps order, the Technician does the work, and each one wants to be the boss. Sarah had opened the shop as a Technician only.

##"Sarah," he told her, "it's time to learn all about pies all over again."##

[[lessons]]
$$**Plan the business around the craft before it swallows the craft.** Being excellent at a piece of work does not bring with it the skill of running the till, the rent, the suppliers and the staff that keep that work alive. A talented hairdresser thinking about her own salon could write down every task that is not cutting hair before she signs a lease, from ordering stock to chasing late payments. That list shows her which jobs she will do, which she will hand off, and how much of her week the scissors will actually get, so the work she loves has a protected place.$$
