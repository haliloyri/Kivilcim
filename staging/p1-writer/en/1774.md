[[story]]
In September 1951, during the Korean War, HMCS Cayuga was north of the 38th parallel when a junk carrying 19 wounded men, mostly young, from an ambush approached the ship under a flag. The only medical man aboard was a Lieutenant Dr. Joseph Cyr. Maria Konnikova opens *The Confidence Game* with what he did next. He had never finished high school, and his real name was Ferdinand Waldo Demara.

Over 48 hours he performed the surgeries using a medical textbook, a field guide written by an Ontario physician, and antibiotics for the patients. A navy press officer looked for a story, and the heroism of Dr. Cyr spread across Canada. The navy did not press charges. It quietly dismissed Demara and asked him to leave the country.

Demara was exposed again and again, and kept succeeding. He posed as a monk, a psychologist, a professor, a prison warden, a teacher at a school for disabled children, and a civil engineer who nearly won a bridge contract in Mexico. He also posed as Robert Crichton, the author of the 1959 book about him, *The Great Impostor*. After Crichton exposed him, Demara asked him for money and babysat his children.

Konnikova's other cases are less theatrical. A New York psychic took more than $128,000 from Lee Choong, a Singaporean businesswoman who was in a romantic and professional rut. Robin Lloyd, new to New York and short of cash, lost money in a street game of three-card monte. Victor Lustig, who called himself a count, twice sold investors shares in the Eiffel Tower.

The first step of a con, in her account, is the put-up: identifying the victim and what they want. After that the con artist never has to force anyone. People dislike uncertainty and crave a story that explains things, and the victim supplies the persuasion, and the story, themselves.

[[lessons]]
$$**Notice what you hope is true before you judge the offer.** A freelancer who badly needs a client can read a vague offer as promising, simply because it arrives at the right moment. Writing down the answer she is hoping for, before she replies, shows her which questions she has been avoiding. She can then ask them in the same message and judge the offer by the replies. Everyone has hopes, so the point is only to know which of them is doing the reading when something sounds right.$$
