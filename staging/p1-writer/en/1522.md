[[story]]
M. Scott Peck starts *The Road Less Traveled* with three words: "Life is difficult." He calls it one of the great truths, and he says that once we really accept it, the fact stops troubling us. Those three words open the book, and everything after them is built on that acceptance. The reasoning is simple: a person who keeps expecting life to be easy is troubled every time it is not.

The book rests on two ideas, love and discipline. Peck defines love as the will to extend yourself to nurture your own or another person's spiritual growth. He calls it an act of will. That is a demanding definition for a word people use so lightly. That definition puts the weight on effort: to extend yourself means doing something that costs you, for the sake of someone's growth.

Discipline is how that growth happens, and Peck names four tools: delaying gratification, accepting responsibility, dedication to truth, and balancing. Delaying gratification means giving up a comfort today for a larger good later. Accepting responsibility means owning your part instead of taking shelter in outside causes. Dedication to truth means keeping your picture of the world honest even when it costs you comfort. Balancing is the fourth tool on his list. A person who skips discipline may mean well and still stay exactly where they are.

##The discipline, Peck says, is fed by love.## A parent who loves a child will hold the hard conversation, and a person who values herself will choose the demanding discipline. Care comes first, and the hard action follows from it.

Love without discipline falls short of what it intends, because good intentions with no effort behind them do not change anything.

[[lessons]]
$$**Treat the hard action as the proof of care.** A baker who cares about her apprentice may find it easier to praise his loaves than to tell him they come out underbaked. Telling him plainly, and showing him how to judge the crust, is the harder thing and the one that helps him grow. It lets her see that a kind word and a caring act are different things, and she can ask herself which one the moment needs before she speaks.$$
