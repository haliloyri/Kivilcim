[[story]]
At random times during the day, a pager beeped. Wherever the person happened to be, they stopped and wrote down what they were doing and how they felt. This was the experience sampling method that Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi used to catch ordinary life while it was happening, and he writes about what it showed in *Finding Flow*. Because the beeps came at random, the record covered dull hours as well as good ones, and each person answered many times. Each beep gave a snapshot, and many snapshots gave a pattern.

The results held a puzzle. People reported flow more often at work than in their free time. Yet at work they also more often said they wished they were doing something else. Csikszentmihalyi offers an explanation: free time may leave people unable to organise their attention.

##They wished to be elsewhere, and their best moments came where they were.##

Csikszentmihalyi's explanation of flow rests on a balance between the challenge of a task and the skill of the person doing it. When the two match, the task absorbs the person. When the challenge is much higher than the skill, the result is anxiety. When the skill is much higher than the challenge, the result is boredom.

Work, even when people complain about it, often hands them a task with a goal and a level of difficulty already set. Free time does not. An unstructured evening gives no challenge to match a skill against, which fits that explanation.

His account therefore changes where to look for a good moment. He looks for it in a task that asks for a bit more than you currently have, and that you can just about do.

[[lessons]]
$$**Adjust the task to fit your skill.** The same piece of work can feel dull or overwhelming depending on how its difficulty compares with what you can do. A cook who finds chopping onions dull can set herself a goal such as making every slice the same width, which raises the challenge enough to hold her attention. If the same job overwhelms her on a busy night, she can cut it into one tray at a time and finish each before the next, so the job stays within what she can do.$$
