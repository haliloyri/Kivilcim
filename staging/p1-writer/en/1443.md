Turn a thermostat to the top in a cold room and the room does not warm any faster. A great many people believe it does.

[[story]]
Don Norman uses this in his account of conceptual models. In the traditional thermostat there is a dial for the desired temperature. People believe that turning it to the top will warm a cold room faster. The setting only decides when the heating shuts off. The heat output is the same, so the dial turned to the top does not speed anything up.

The refrigerator is a stranger case. It has two controls, one marked for the freezer and one for the fresh-food compartment, and users take each to set the temperature of its own compartment. In fact one control sets the compressor level and the other shifts how cold air is divided between the two spaces. Making the freezer colder chills the fresh-food section too, and a salad can freeze. Making the fresh-food section colder pulls cold out of the freezer, and ice cream can thaw. The temperatures take about a day to settle, so adjusting them can take the better part of a week.

Norman's diagnosis is that the controls suggest a false conceptual model. According to the book, a conceptual model is usually highly simplified and does not have to be complete or even accurate, as long as it is useful. The test is whether the picture in the user's head is good enough to predict what the device will do. The thermostat belief and the refrigerator labels both fail it.

One critic of the example argues that the real flaw lies in the controls themselves and not in how they are labelled, since renaming them would not make regulating the temperatures workable.

[[lessons]]
$$**Ask people what a control does, and change the setup where they are wrong.** The thermostat and the refrigerator are two cases where the picture in someone's head differed from what the machine did. If a new tool at work has a button called Archive and staff assume the file is safe when it is only hidden, a manager can ask newcomers what they think the button does. When the answers differ, she can rename the button or change what it does, and nobody has to learn it the hard way.$$
