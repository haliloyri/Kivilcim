A shepherd beds his flock down in a ruined church. A sycamore has grown up through the roof of what used to be the sacristy, and he falls asleep under it. The dream he has there stops one step before the answer. Then it comes again, and stops in the same place.

[[story]]
Santiago, the young Andalusian shepherd in Paulo Coelho's *The Alchemist*, sleeps in an abandoned church with his sheep around him. The roof is gone in places, and a sycamore grows through it from the old sacristy. It is a quiet place to spend a night, and nothing about it is meant to be important.

In the dream, a child takes him by both hands and leads him to the Egyptian pyramids. If he goes there, the child says, he will find a hidden treasure. The child is about to show him the exact spot when Santiago wakes up.

He decides it was only a dream. A while later he is back in the same church, and the dream comes again. The child, the pyramids, the promise, and the same waking just before the place is pointed out.

A second time is harder to wave away. In the town of Tarifa he goes to a gypsy woman who reads dreams. She tells him to go to the pyramids and find the treasure, and for the reading she asks for a tenth of whatever he digs up.

Then he meets an old man in a square, who turns out to be the King of Salem. The king talks about a Personal Legend, the thing a person has always wanted to do. Most people, he says, stop believing it is possible.

##Santiago sells his sheep and crosses the water to Africa.##

That is not a small step. The sheep are how a shepherd earns his living, and he has to turn the whole flock into travel money before he can leave the fields he knows.

He has the sea behind him and a new continent ahead. On his first day in Tangier, a young man offers to guide him, and by the end of it Santiago's money is gone. He stands in a foreign port with no flock, no money, and a dream that has never once told him where to dig.

[[lessons]]
$$**Give a repeating wish one real, affordable try.** A cook who keeps imagining her own small place can go on imagining it for years without learning a thing about it. If she rents a stall for a single weekend, she finds out whether she likes the early mornings and whether strangers will pay for her food. Either way the wish stops being a vague pull and turns into something she can describe, measure and decide about, which a hundred more daydreams will never do. Santiago paid with his whole flock, but your first test can cost far less.$$
